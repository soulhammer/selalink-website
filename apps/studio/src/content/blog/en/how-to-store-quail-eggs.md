Have you ever stored quail eggs in the refrigerator only to find them dried out or with broken yolks faster than expected? 🥚

Quail eggs have significantly thinner eggshells and cuticle layers compared to chicken eggs. This causes carbon dioxide and moisture to evaporate more than twice as fast, making them relatively more vulnerable to external pathogens like Salmonella.

In this guide, based on official guidelines from the USDA and MFDS, we share a 3-step scientific storage method to preserve the freshness and nutrition of quail eggs without moisture loss. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Store refrigerated with the blunt end (air cell) facing up — Cold Storage at 1–4°C (21 Days) 🥚</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Quail eggs lose moisture faster than chicken eggs, making 1–4°C (34–39°F) cold storage essential. 💡 Storage Tip: Keep eggs in their original carton with the blunt end pointing up on an interior refrigerator shelf to keep the yolk centered for 3 weeks.
  </p>
</div>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not store quail eggs with the pointed end facing up ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      The air cell is located at the blunt end of the quail egg. Storing the pointed end up causes the yolk to float against the air cell, damaging the yolk membrane and increasing the risk of bacterial contamination.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not wash quail eggs before refrigerating 💧</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Washing quail eggs dissolves the cuticle layer—a natural protective barrier that blocks bacterial entry. Microscopic pores are exposed, allowing external pathogens to penetrate rapidly.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
