Do you routinely store raw potatoes in the refrigerator crisper drawer? 🥔

Unlike most vegetables, storing potatoes at cold temperatures below 4°C (39°F) triggers 'cold-induced sweetening', converting their starches into reducing sugars. While this makes them taste sweeter, frying or roasting these potatoes at high temperatures generates dangerously high levels of Acrylamide, a known Group 2A carcinogen.

Furthermore, exposure to light causes potato skins to turn green, synthesizing a toxic glycoalkaloid called solanine. Today, FreshSnap shares a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on guidelines from the UK FSA and MFDS to prevent toxins, suppress sprouting, and keep your potatoes healthy and fresh. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Always cut away green patches and sprout eyes deeply 🥔</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Potato sprouts and green-tinged skins contain high concentrations of 'solanine', a neurotoxin that causes nausea, vomiting, and food poisoning. Solanine is heat-stable and won't be destroyed by cooking. You must slice away green skin thickly and dig out sprout eyes deeply before cooking.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Never store potatoes together with onions 🧅</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Onions and potatoes are storage antagonists. Onions emit moisture and ethylene gas that trigger potatoes to sprout and soften rapidly. Conversely, the moisture from potatoes causes onions to rot and grow mold. They must be isolated in separate storage areas.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
