"Architecture should be a space where people can breathe and dream, never trapped inside a rectangular box." 🏛️

Zaha Hadid (1950–2016) was an Iraqi-British architect who revolutionized contemporary world architecture as the first woman to win the Pritzker Architecture Prize. Her futuristic 3D organic structures—such as Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP) in Seoul and the Heydar Aliyev Center in Baku—left profound artistic marks on major global cities.

Hadid completely discarded traditional rigid grids and rectangular thinking. On massive blank sketchbooks, using ink and pens, she continuously drew fluid, organic curves and freeform lines by hand. The visuospatial cues captured during this tactile sketching process powerfully stimulated dopamine circuits responsible for 3D spatial thinking and non-linear ideation.

This post explores Zaha Hadid's 'free curve sketching' routine and outlines a 3-step ideation method for modern professionals.

## 1. Stimulation of Right-Brain Visuospatial Processing Circuits

Relying strictly on rigid guidelines and straight lines causes the left brain's logical areas to dominate, reinforcing stereotypes. Executing unrestricted <strong>organic hand sketching</strong> like Hadid activates the right brain's visuospatial processing areas, maximizing novel visual ideas.

## 3. Caution Against Obsessing Over Initial Precision and Perfection

If you use a ruler or correct asymmetry early on trying to 'draw a pretty picture', the brain's free ideation circuit closes. For the first 5 minutes, focus solely on the flow of lines rather than perfection. Rather than relying on pure willpower, design a physical environment and habit cues that naturally trigger action.
