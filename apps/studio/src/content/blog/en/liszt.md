Hungarian composer Franz Liszt, the emperor of the piano during the Romantic era of the 19th century and the greatest virtuosic pianist of his time, expanded the expressive limits of piano music to the maximum through overwhelming sophistication and practice methods. Instead of practicing playing quickly and recklessly on the keyboard, he practiced every day by slowing down the metronome tempo to an extreme slow-motion level and playing only one key per second without error. He programmed the perfect muscle movement trajectories of the keyboard into his brain, and then used them to create magical, spectacular moves.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of the habit of ‘slowing down the metronome tempo and hitting one key per second accurately’ and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of brain science principles that lead to success

Training to consciously control the movements of fingers and body muscles by slowing down the tempo of movement stimulates fine motor learning and neuron myelination in the cerebellum. Slow repetition of perfect trajectories thickens the myelin, the covering of the corresponding nerve fibers, dramatically improving the efficiency of brain signal transmission by more than 10 times. This is the ultimate error-free routine (Errorless Learning) formation method that does not input any motion errors into the brain.
