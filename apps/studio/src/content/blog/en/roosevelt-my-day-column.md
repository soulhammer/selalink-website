Eleanor Roosevelt, the political figure who provided warm comfort to the hearts of Americans during the historical turmoil of the Great Depression and World War II, and led the UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights. The secret behind her solid peace of mind amidst extreme workloads and political pressure lay in her nightly writing. ✍️

From 1935 until her death in 1962, Eleanor published a syndicated newspaper column called 'My Day' six days a week for 27 years without a single miss. This writing was not a grand political manifesto. It read like a personal diary containing stories of marginalized neighbors she met that day, scenes of sites she visited, and thoughts she processed. By holding a pen at her desk every night, she stepped back from the chaos of the day, looking at and objective-analyzing her own mind.

Today, BuildSelf presents the cognitive benefits of Eleanor Roosevelt's daily journaling and a 3-step reflective writing method to clarify values and manage mental health.

## 1. Long-Term Memory Consolidation and Metacognitive Stimulation

If left disorganized, the experiences and emotions we go through during the day stimulate the amygdala, turning into internalized stress or evaporating, while clogging the brain's working capacity.

Structuring the day by writing it down like Eleanor Roosevelt activates the <strong>'hippocampus,'</strong> facilitating the consolidation process that transfers episodic memory into stable long-term memory. Furthermore, substituting experiences and emotions with objective words stimulates the prefrontal cortex, promoting 'metacognition'—the ability to view oneself from a third-person perspective. This forms a solid neural pillar that dampens emotional impulsivity and sustains mental peace.
