Henri Poincaré, the solver of the three-body problem, pioneer of chaos theory, and regarded as one of the greatest genius mathematicians in modern history, surprisingly had a strict rule of working at his desk for only 4 hours a day. ⏰

He worked deeply for only 4 hours—usually from 10:00 AM to 12:00 PM and from 5:00 PM to 7:00 PM—and spent the rest of his day walking or socializing to let his brain rest offline. Interestingly, Poincaré recalled that the core equations of unresolved mathematical problems flashed into his head in complete form not at his desk, but at the moment he stepped onto an omnibus during a trip or while walking on a beach.

Today, BuildSelf uncovers the science behind Henri Poincaré's '4-hour focus limit' routine and how modern people can leverage the interaction between focused and diffuse brain modes to solve complex creative problems through a 3-step action plan.

## 1. Focused Mode and Diffuse Mode: The Problem-Solving Power of the Subconscious

According to learning sciences and neurophysiology, the brain operates in two distinct cognitive systems: 'Focused Mode' and 'Diffuse Mode.' In focused mode, where you sit at a desk and calculate with all your energy, frontal lobe functions are narrowed to activate only specific and localized neural circuits. However, when you release concentration and take a light walk or rest, the brain enters diffuse mode, awakening the Default Mode Network (DMN) which links broad regions of the brain.

During this diffuse mode, the brain subconsciously undergoes an 'Incubation' process, randomly combining existing fragments of knowledge. The reason Poincaré solved complex formulas during rest was that the brain, entering diffuse mode, lowered the cognitive barriers of the frontal lobe to connect entirely new neural pathways.
