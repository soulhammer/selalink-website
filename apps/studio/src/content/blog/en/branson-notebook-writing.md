Richard Branson, the billionaire entrepreneur who established the British icon Virgin Group and built a diverse business empire of over 400 companies spanning aviation, space, finance, and entertainment. Famous for his creative marketing and adventurous spirit, he has one unique signature item. It is not a luxury watch or a high-tech smart device, but a small analog paper notebook tucked in his jeans pocket.

For decades, whether in business meetings, parties, or even while adventuring, Branson immediately jotted down fleeting ideas, customer feedback, and unique quotes in his notebook. He still treasures thousands of these filled notebooks in his study, asserting that 'without the notebook, Virgin Group would never have been born.' For him, note-taking was a trap to catch ideas before they escaped into the subconscious.

Today, BuildSelf introduces why Richard Branson's 'handwritten note' habit produces stronger execution power than digital alternatives neuroscientifically, and how modern people can utilize this for goal planning.

## 1. The Brain's Reticular Activating System (RAS) and Filtering Crucial Information

The <strong>Reticular Activating System (RAS)</strong> located in the brainstem serves as a gateway that filters only critical information for the brain to focus on consciously amid countless daily inputs. The physical stimulus of pressing a pen onto paper stamps a powerful bio-signal into the brain: 'This information is crucial for survival and achievement.'

Once activated through handwriting, the RAS triggers the brain's attention control system. The brain then enters a state of <strong>'Selective Perception,'</strong> subconsciously searching for information, opportunities, people, and resources connected to that note. This drastically increases the probability of translating ideas into execution compared to mere thoughts or typing.
