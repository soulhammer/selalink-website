Alexander Graham Bell, the great inventor who changed the paradigm of human communication by inventing the first practical telephone. Where did his creative inspirations come from? 🌌

During the day, Bell was exposed to severe cognitive fatigue and sensory overload due to humanitarian work for the deaf and business contracts. To overcome this, he designated 10 PM to 2 AM as his own 'darkness deep work time.' Turning off all candles to create absolute silence and darkness, he visualized complex mechanical structures in his mind. This sensory deprivation habit was the source of his inventions.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the scientific mechanisms behind Bell's 'sensory deprivation' routine and a 3-step practice for modern people.

## 1. The Principle of 'Sensory Deprivation' for Maximizing Brain Association

According to modern brain research, intentionally isolating external visual and auditory inputs (sensory deprivation) stops unnecessary information processing. This conserves the massive energy consumed by the visual cortex, maximizing signal strength in the parietal and frontal lobes responsible for spatial reasoning and mental simulation. It guides the brain into a creative state within the Default Mode Network, combining complex information into logical structures.
