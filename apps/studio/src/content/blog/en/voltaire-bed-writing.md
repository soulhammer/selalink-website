Voltaire, the symbol of the 18th-century European Enlightenment, who left behind countless novels, tragedies, and historical texts. Where did his sharp criticisms and brilliant humanistic ideas begin each morning? ✍️

Surprisingly, after waking up in the morning, Voltaire spent several hours reading or writing without stepping out of bed. He knew that during these half-asleep morning hours, cognitive flexibility is maximized, and sharp satire and flashing metaphorical expressions flow naturally. Voltaire kept several secretaries in his bedroom, dictating thoughts or writing dozens of letters while leaning back in bed. Much of his vast correspondence was born from this bedside routine.

## 1. Cognitive Science of 'Sleep Inertia' for Maximizing Creativity

The state in which the brain is not fully awake immediately after waking up is called <strong>'sleep inertia'</strong> or the <strong>'hypnopompic'</strong> state in cognitive science. During this period, the censorship of the prefrontal cortex, which is responsible for rational judgment and inhibition, weakens. On the other hand, alpha (α) waves in a relaxed state and theta (θ) waves in a light dream state lead the brain. This lowers the censorship barrier of thoughts, enabling non-linear and original associations. Voltaire was able to pour out brilliant satire and ideas from his bed because he perfectly utilized this gap in the brain where the subconscious is active before conscious logic takes over.
