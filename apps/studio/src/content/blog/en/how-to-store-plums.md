Plums are beloved worldwide during the summer for their sweet, tangy flavor and rich, juicy flesh! 🍑

Have you ever bought a box of plums, left them in the pantry only for them to turn mushy and moldy overnight? Or put hard, sour plums straight into the fridge only to find them tasteless and mealy?

Plums are climacteric fruits with thin, delicate skin that makes them highly vulnerable to mechanical damage. Knowing the exact timing to transition plums from room-temperature ripening to cold refrigeration is the key secret to maximizing sweetness and freshness. In this guide, introduces a scientifically proven 3-step plum storage guide based on expert agricultural guidelines to boost sweetness and keep your plums crisp and juicy for 2 to 3 weeks. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not refrigerate hard, unripened plums immediately ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Placing firm, unripened plums below 5°C (41°F) triggers chilling injury. Respiratory ripening stops, internal moisture dries up, the flesh turns mealy, and sugars fail to develop. Always ripen at room temperature before chilling.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Prevent plums from touching and pressing against each other 🍑</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Plums have delicate skins and soft flesh. When stacked together, the contact pressure causes bruising and moisture breakdown. These bruised spots become primary targets for mold spores (such as brown rot), so keep them cushioned and spaced apart.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-slate-200/80 bg-white/60 dark:border-white/10 dark:bg-slate-900/40 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md">
  <h3 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white mt-0 mb-6 flex items-center gap-2">
    <span>📌</span> Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
  </h3>
  
  <details class="group border-b border-slate-200/80 dark:border-slate-800/80 pb-4 mb-4 cursor-pointer" open>
    <summary class="flex justify-between items-center font-bold text-slate-900 dark:text-white list-none text-base md:text-lg tracking-tight" role="button" aria-expanded="true">
      <span class="pr-4">Should I wash off the white powdery coating on plum skins before storing?</span>
      <span class="transition-transform group-open:rotate-180 text-xs text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500 flex-shrink-0">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm md:text-base text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed pl-1 m-0 font-normal">
      No, absolutely not! The white powder on plums is natural bloom (epicuticular wax), which is harmless and non-toxic. Bloom is the plum's natural protective shield that prevents moisture evaporation and wards off pests and microbes. Keep it intact during storage and wash right before eating.
    </p>
  </details>

  <details class="group cursor-pointer">
    <summary class="flex justify-between items-center font-bold text-slate-900 dark:text-white list-none text-base md:text-lg tracking-tight" role="button" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="pr-4">My plum has turned soft, mushy, and is leaking juice. Is it still safe to eat?</span>
      <span class="transition-transform group-open:rotate-180 text-xs text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500 flex-shrink-0">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm md:text-base text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed pl-1 m-0 font-normal">
      If a plum becomes soft and leaks juice, fungal spores (such as Monilinia brown rot) or bacteria have likely invaded the damaged tissue. Microscopic toxins spread quickly through soft fruit flesh, so discard damaged plums immediately and isolate them from healthy ones.
