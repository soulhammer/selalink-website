Henry Ford, the automotive giant who built the historic conveyor belt mass production system. Where did his structural design inspirations come from? 🚗

Every morning, Ford walked around the Highland Park factory for 30 minutes. He did not walk to police workers, but to observe the physical movement and rhythm of machines. When he caught bottlenecks, he took out a small notebook and sketched the process flow using arrows and basic shapes. Offloading complex computations onto paper allowed him to maintain clarity of mind.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes the physiological principles behind Ford's 'diagram sketching' routine and how to apply it today.

## 1. Visual Smooth Pursuit and Cognitive Offloading

Visually tracking ordered objects moving in a fixed trajectory synchronizes the visual cortex with the motor timing control areas of the cerebellum, stabilizing attention. Furthermore, spatial sketching, drawing arrows and shapes by hand, transfers complex logical computations onto paper. This 'cognitive offloading' protects the working memory capacity of the prefrontal cortex, enabling clearer, more creative decision-making.
