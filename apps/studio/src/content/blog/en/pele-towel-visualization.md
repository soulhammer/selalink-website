"I close my eyes and vividly picture myself playing on the pitch. That's when my body becomes completely ready." ⚽

Pelé (1940–2022) is widely regarded as the greatest footballer in history. Stepping onto the World Cup stage in his late teens, the roar of tens of thousands of spectators and the enormous weight of national expectations placed immense pressure on him. Yet, from the very first moment he walked onto the field, he maintained absolute composure, producing miraculous plays. The secret lay hidden in a quiet corner of the locker room.

Fifteen minutes before kick-off, amidst locker room noise, Pelé lay on the floor and covered his face with a dry towel. Completely isolating himself from the external world, he recalled the pure joy of kicking a soccer ball in the streets of Brazil during childhood to relax his brain. He then visualized every movement—dribbles, passes, shots, and even goal celebrations—like a vivid movie playing in his mind.

In this post, we examine the sports science and neuroscience behind Pelé's 'Towel Visualization' routine and how high performers today can utilize it.

## Mental Imagery Training That Stimulates the Brain's Motor Cortex

When vividly imagining physical movements, the brain activates the same <strong>'Motor Cortex'</strong> region used during physical execution. Neuroscience shows neural firing during visualization mimics real action, pre-training the brain. Blocking light with a towel induces <strong>Alpha waves</strong>, calming the amygdala and sharpening focus.

---

## Detailed Visualization After Positive Experience Relaxation

Relaxation must precede visualization. Just as Pelé imagined happy street soccer memories first to soothe tension, entering an alpha wave state allows motor cortex simulation to function unobstructed.
