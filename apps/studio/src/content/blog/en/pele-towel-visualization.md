Pele, the greatest football player in history and the very synonym for soccer. The roaring cheers of tens of thousands of spectators and the enormous pressure from his nation to win presented a colossal psychological burden for the teenager on the World Cup stage. Yet, he always stepped onto the pitch with absolute composure. The secret was hidden in a corner of the locker room.

Fifteen minutes before matches, amidst the locker room chaos, Pele lay down and covered his face with a dry towel. In complete isolation from the outside world, he relaxed his brain by recollecting the pure joy of playing street soccer in Brazil during his childhood. Then, he visualized the dribbles, passes, shots, and even goal celebrations like a vivid movie.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the scientific principles behind Pele's 'towel visualization' routine and how modern people can adapt this to their mindset before crucial events.

## 1. Image Training That Stimulates the Brain's Motor Cortex

When vividly visualizing a physical action, the brain activates the same areas of the <strong>'Motor Cortex'</strong> that are used when actually moving the body. Neuroscientific research shows that neural firing patterns during visualization mimic those during real actions, effectively pre-training the brain.

Additionally, blocking visual stimuli with a towel induces brainwaves into <strong>Alpha waves</strong>, a state of relaxation and deep attentional focus. This shuts down abnormal excitation in the amygdala, which regulates fear and anxiety, preventing distractions and remodeling the mind for peak performance.
