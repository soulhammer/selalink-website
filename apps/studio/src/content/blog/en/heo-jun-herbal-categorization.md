"No matter how noble medical knowledge is, if the common people cannot understand or use it, it is merely dead scholarship." 🌿

Heo Jun (1539–1615), the legendary royal physician of the Joseon Dynasty and author of the immortal medical encyclopedia 'Dongui Bogam'. Amidst the devastation of war and rampant epidemics, without modern database tools, how did he synthesize over 500 complex classical medical texts into a perfectly structured, accessible knowledge system?

Heo Jun practiced a relentless habit of 'Herbal Medicine Categorization'. Alongside classical Chinese names of over 1,400 medicinal herbs, he meticulously cross-referenced their vernacular Hangul names ('Hyangmyeong'), harvest seasons, efficacies, and preparation methods. By translating obscure academic jargon into the living language of the people, he created 'Dongui Bogam'—not just a medical manual, but a UNESCO Memory of the World masterpiece that stands as one of humanity's finest historical databases.

In today's post, we examine the cognitive principles behind Heo Jun's data categorization habit and explore a 3-step practical guide for modern individuals to master complex professional knowledge and communicate it with crystal clarity.

## 1. 'Dual Coding' and Information Architecture: Bridging Academic Jargon and Experiential Language

Inputting only obscure jargon or abstract concepts into the brain impairs accessibility and recall speed. Cross-referencing professional terminology with familiar, everyday language activates Allan Paivio's <strong>'Dual Coding Theory'</strong>. Mapping academic knowledge to intuitive terms reduces <strong>Working Memory</strong> load and dramatically accelerates information retrieval and cognitive internalization.

## 3. Rule: Map One-to-One Intuitive Properties Beyond Mere Data Collection

The crucial rule in data categorization is avoiding passive copying. You must map professional terms one-to-one with your own intuitive language and clearly define properties such as practical use, key features, and application context to build a lasting cognitive architecture in your brain.
