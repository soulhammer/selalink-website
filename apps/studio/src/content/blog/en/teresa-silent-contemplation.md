Mother Teresa, recipient of the Nobel Peace Prize for dedicating her life to serving the poorest and suffering while spreading love and peace. What routine nurtured the unyielding equanimity and inner strength that allowed her to endure the indescribable sorrow and psychological strain in the slums of Kolkata? 🕊️

Facing hundreds of critically ill patients daily, Mother Teresa was exposed to severe emotional contagion and depletion. To alleviate emotional burnout and cognitive exhaustion, she strictly observed a morning silent contemplation routine, waking at 4:30 AM every day. Before speaking with other missionaries or beginning outside duties, she spent her first hour kneeling in complete silence (Silent Contemplation) with closed eyes, recovering reflection and inner peace. She noted that this complete morning stillness was essential to clearing away residual emotional residue and gaining a shield to sustain her through the day.

In this post, we examine the neuroscientific benefits of Mother Teresa's 'morning silent contemplation' routine and provide 15-minute actionable tips for modern individuals.

## 1. Warming Up the Empathy Network and Suppressing Amygdala Hyperactivity

Early morning silent contemplation suppresses sympathetic nervous system hyperarousal and stabilizes the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis that regulates stress hormones. This physically inhibits hyperactivity in the amygdala, which governs emotional reactions. Simultaneously, it strengthens functional connectivity between the prefrontal cortex and emotional regions, preconditioning the empathy network and emotional control to maintain equanimity without being overwhelmed by others' pain or stress.

## 3. The Pitfall of Thought Suppression in Silence and Letting Go Tips

When practicing silence, attempting to forcibly suppress worries or to-do lists by thinking 'I shouldn't think about this' creates a rebound effect where those thoughts swirl more intensely. When thoughts arise, do not fight to control them; observe them dispassionately like passing clouds in the background of your consciousness, intentionally redirecting focus to the movement of your breath for neural stability.
