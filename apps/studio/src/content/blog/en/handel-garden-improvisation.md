"When you lay down evaluation and censorship, the brain finally produces its greatest melodies." 🎼

George Frideric Handel, the master composer who left an indelible mark on music history with masterpieces like 'Messiah' and 'Water Music', possessed an extraordinary creative drive. Despite facing severe stress, a stroke, and financial ruin, he composed countless monumental works at an explosive speed. His secret lay in a unique improvisational sketch routine he never skipped each morning.

Before embarking on a major composition, Handel walked leisurely through a quiet garden every morning to clear his head and calm his mind. After the walk, he sat down at the harpsichord or organ and played freely for 30 minutes without self-criticism or evaluation, letting his fingers lead the way. The fleeting melodies and ideas that surfaced during this time became the grand motifs of masterpieces that moved humanity.

This routine was an advanced cognitive strategy that liberated the brain from 'self-censorship' and unlocked latent creativity. In today's post, we introduce the cognitive neuroscience principles of Default Mode Network (DMN) activation and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) deactivation embedded in Handel's garden walks and improvisational sketches.

## 1. Creative Synergy of Default Mode Network (DMN) Activation and Internal Critic (dlPFC) Deactivation

A morning walk in the garden activates the brain's <strong>'Default Mode Network (DMN)'</strong>, promoting the 'Incubation Effect' where unconscious ideas freely connect.

Performing a 30-minute uninhibited improvisation after the walk temporarily dampens activity in the <strong>'Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (dlPFC)'</strong>, which is responsible for self-criticism and censorship, thereby inducing a <strong>'Flow State'</strong> that opens inner creative pathways. This scientific routine maximizes Divergent Thinking from the spontaneous unconscious rather than controlled logic, bringing grand inspiration into reality.

## 3. Modern Focus Guide to Turning Off Self-Criticism and Harnessing Unconscious Inspiration

Rather than demanding perfection from the start when generating ideas, strictly separate the creation stage from the evaluation stage. The key is to first secure time for uninhibited sketching without judgment.
