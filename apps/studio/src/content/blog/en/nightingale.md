Florence Nightingale, the founder of modern nursing and an innovator in medical administration, a British statistician who saved thousands of wounded soldiers by overcoming the horrors of field hospitals during the Crimean War in the 19th century with scientific data and thorough sanitary reform. In the harrowing field hospital, she constantly patrolled more than four miles of dark hospital hallways each night, armed with only a lamp. She went beyond simply comforting the patients' pain, and meticulously archived numbers on notepad for the patient's fever level, the wetness of the bedding, and the flow of fresh air inside the room. This collected fact data later became the basis for statistical indicators that dramatically lowered the hospital mortality rate.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of his habit of ‘observing 4 miles of hospital hallways with a single lamp every night’ and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of brain science principles that lead to success

The act of looking closely at minor risk factors in the environment and physical clues of others and quantifying the data strongly stimulates the mirror neurons in the cerebral cortex and the cognitive filter network in the frontal lobe. The process of converting qualitative phenomena into quantified numbers and recording them awakens the brain's lateral prefrontal cortex, firmly connecting metacognitive cognitive resolution to preemptively capture minor business risks or system errors that can easily be overlooked in daily life.
