Toni Morrison, the novelist who managed to write Nobel Prize-winning masterpieces that will remain in literary history forever, while raising two children as a single mother and working as a senior publisher editor. The secret to her rich literary depth despite time poverty was a quiet pre-dawn ritual. ☕

Morrison got up at 5:00 a.m. every morning when the whole house was wrapped in silence, and brewed warm coffee. Before turning on a computer or holding a pen, she stood by her study window holding the coffee cup in both hands. She quietly watched the cold pre-dawn light (dawn) gradually clearing the darkness of the night. She called this 'watching the light arrive,' confessing that this dim time was the only space where she could escape daily social duties and exist as a pure creative subject.

Today, BuildSelf explains the science behind Morrison's dawn routine and introduces a specific morning ritual to wake up your brain peacefully and sharply.

## 1. Circadian Clock Awakening and Retinal Photoreceptor Stimulation

Exposing the brain to the strong blue light of a smartphone as soon as you open your eyes in a dark room causes a sharp spike in cortisol, increasing anxiety levels.

The dawn observation practiced by Toni Morrison transmits natural, gradually brightening light through the retina, sending a peaceful awakening signal to the <strong>'suprachiasmatic nucleus,'</strong> the circadian clock regulator. This gently suppresses the sleep hormone melatonin and balances neurotransmitters that induce stable attention. Furthermore, the quiet atmosphere of the dawn eliminates unnecessary cognitive interference in the frontal lobe, providing an optimal neural environment to focus entirely on creative writing and ideas.
