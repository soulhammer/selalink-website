In the Three Kingdoms period of late Eastern Han, Zhuge Liang (諸葛亮) was the master strategist who assisted Liu Bei in founding Shu Han (蜀漢), crafted the Tripartite Division of China, and earned fame as a legendary Prime Minister (名宰相) amidst fierce conflicts between Wei, Shu, and Wu. Shoulder-deep in responsibility for the lives of hundreds of thousands of soldiers and the survival of his nation across battles like Red Cliffs and the Southern Campaign, he endured extreme emotional pressure daily. 🎶

Every dawn under the crisp air of his eaves, Zhuge Liang observed a quiet routine of playing the guqin (古琴), a traditional pentatonic zither. Plucking the strings in the serene morning, he washed away military attachments and anger, and at night stood under the starlit sky to observe constellation and meteorological shifts, refining grand strategies. His philosophy in 'Admonition to My Son' (誡子書)—"Without indifference to fame and wealth, one cannot clarify one's ambition; without tranquility, one cannot reach far" (非淡泊無以明志, 非寧靜無以致遠)—stemmed directly from this dawn routine.

In today's post, we explore the cognitive science behind Zhuge Liang's lifelong dawn string performance and perspective expansion routine, inducing alpha brainwaves and decision-making clarity.

## 1. Blocking Emotional Overload and Enhancing Macro Spatial Judgment via Alpha Wave Induction

When faced with crucial tasks or high anxiety, the brain's <strong>Amygdala</strong> becomes hyperactive, narrowing vision and causing indecisiveness.

Zhuge Liang's guqin playing and serene stargazing acted as a powerful <strong>'Emotional Clearing System'</strong> inducing alpha brainwaves (8-12Hz). Calming the mind blocks anxiety and activates insight circuits in the prefrontal cortex, allowing one to derive optimal grand strategies amidst complex choices.

As Zhuge Liang's famous maxim states, without tranquility one cannot reach far. Block out noisy news or SNS notifications and secure 10 minutes of intentional relaxation and silence. Do not rely solely on willpower; pre-designing physical environments and habit cues that naturally trigger action is key to sustainability.
