faqs:
  - question: "Why is my Black Molly shaking its body side-to-side without swimming forward (Shimmying)?"
    answer: "This phenomenon is known as 'The Shimmies,' a classic symptom of osmotic stress and water maladaptation. Mollies thrive in hard, mineral-rich alkaline water (pH 7.5–8.2). If kept in soft or acidic water, or if temperatures drop below 24°C, they lose osmoregulatory control and experience muscle tremors. Elevating the temperature to 25–27°C and adding aquarium salt usually resolves the issue quickly."

Beloved by aquarium hobbyists for its striking velvety black scales and graceful fins, the Black Molly (Poecilia sphenops) is a popular livebearing tropical freshwater fish. While generally considered easy to care for, Black Mollies are exceptionally sensitive to cold water temperatures and soft, acidic water conditions, which frequently trigger 'The Shimmies' and white spot disease (Ich). They naturally thrive in hard, slightly alkaline to brackish water (pH 7.5–8.2). Rapid water parameter shifts or ammonia spikes rapidly suppress their immune system. In this guide, presents a 3-step daily care routine to decode your Black Molly's behavioral signals and protect its health.
