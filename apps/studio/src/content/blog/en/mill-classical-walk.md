John Stuart Mill wrote On Liberty and led 19th-century utilitarianism. How did this giant of intellect develop his cognitive capacity? 🚶

The answer lies in his 'walk-and-talk' routine, which began in childhood. His father, James Mill, took him walking along forest paths near London early every morning. During this walk, Mill summarized and recited Latin and Greek classics read the day before, while his father asked questions to provoke critical reasoning. Combining physical movement, deep reading, and active discussion stimulated his brain, enabling him to build a vast intellectual framework in his teens.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neuroscientific principles of Mill's routine and proposes a practical morning walk-and-read routine to boost memory and activate brain cells.

## 1. The Combination of BDNF and Neuroplasticity to Activate Brain Cells

Unlike stationary reading, walking while contemplating offers immense neuroscientific benefits. Physical activities like walking increase cerebral blood flow and stimulate <strong>brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)</strong> in the hippocampus. BDNF acts as a catalyst for generating and sustaining neurons. When high-level cognitive information (like philosophy) enters the brain during this physical stimulation, it triggers enhanced <strong>neuroplasticity</strong>. This constructs faster pathways to long-term memory, improving memory, logic, and integrative intelligence.
