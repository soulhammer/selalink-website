"The path of an intellectual giant began in the morning meditation and deep discourse along woodland paths in the fresh morning air." 🚶

John Stuart Mill (1806–1873) was the British philosopher and political economist who authored 'On Liberty', defending individual dignity and freedom of thought, while synthesizing 19th-century Utilitarianism. Renowned as one of the most rigorous minds in modern history, how did he cultivate such overwhelming intellectual capabilities starting from early childhood?

The answer lay in the unique 'Walking Discussion' habit he practiced with his father, James Mill, starting around age three. Every early morning, James Mill took young John by the hand to walk along quiet forest paths near London. During these morning walks, Mill recited summaries of Latin and Greek classics he had read the previous day, while his father posed probing questions that forced him to critically infer core conceptual connections. This hybrid routine—combining simple aerobic walking with complex classical reading, active recall, and verbal debate—highly stimulated Mill's brain, enabling him to independently build a vast intellectual framework in his early teens.

In this post, we explore the neuroscientific principles behind John Stuart Mill's walking-and-discussion habit and propose a practical morning walking-reading routine for modern individuals to enhance memory and activate brain cells.

## 1. The Combination of BDNF Brain Activation and Neural Plasticity

Unlike static reading while sitting still, walking while engaging in discourse or reflection holds tremendous neuroscientific benefits. Aerobic physical activity like walking rapidly boosts cerebral blood flow and heavily stimulates the expression of <strong>Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)</strong> in the hippocampus—the hub of memory and learning. BDNF is a master catalyst that promotes the growth and survival of new neurons.

When high-level intellectual information such as philosophy or logic enters the brain during this physically stimulated state, the brain rapidly enters a state of <strong>neuroplasticity</strong>, rewiring synaptic connections. In short, cognitive processing during walking lays down a express highway for transferring information into long-term memory, boosting recall, abstract reasoning, and left-right brain integration to maximum levels.

## 3. When Physical Movement Stops, Thought Stagnates Too

Modern people spend hours sitting motionless every day consuming information, causing brain BDNF levels to bottom out and chronically lowering the capacity to digest new knowledge. Like Mill's habit of merging reading with physical activity, a walking ritual—disconnecting completely from your smartphone immediately after reading to walk and contemplate—is the ultimate integrative cognitive practice to keep brain intelligence flexible and dramatically expand reasoning power.
