Munchkin cats, beloved for their short, stubby legs and outgoing personalities, are surprisingly agile and energetic runners. However, due to their shortened limbs, the impact force exerted on their spine and joints when jumping down from high places is significantly higher than that of standard cats. Consequently, they possess a genetic predisposition to Lordosis and early-onset Osteoarthritis. Today, PetSelf decodes Munchkin body language indicating joint distress and outlines a 3-step low-height homecare routine to safeguard their spine and preserve healthy mobility for life.

## 2. Munchkin Cat (Felis catus)'s Body Language & Behavioral Signals

    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-600 dark:text-emerald-400 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/20">Body Signal 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Kangaroo Standing (Upright Hind-Leg Posture)</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    <strong>• Meaning:</strong> A natural vision-expanding posture to compensate for short stature by balancing solely on hind legs and pelvis.<br/>
    <strong>• Response:</strong> Lay down non-slip floor mats or carpets so they do not slip and place unnecessary strain on their hip joints when standing upright.
  </p>

  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    <strong>• Meaning:</strong> Repeatedly tilting the head and pacing before climbing low furniture, serving as a warning of spinal or hind leg joint pain.<br/>
