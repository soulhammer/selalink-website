Lee Kuan Yew, the founding Prime Minister who transformed Singapore, a small and resource-poor Asian city-state, into a world-class global financial hub and an economic powerhouse. He faced immense psychological pressure formulating survival strategies amid major powers and decision paralysis determining the nation's fate at every turn. How did he maintain a sharp brain and resolute insight into his 90s?

Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew adhered to an extremely strict mind-body care routine. Every evening immediately after work, he ran on the treadmill for 30 minutes, sweating profusely. And right after pushing physical limits and sweating, he sat quietly in a lotus position, calming his active sympathetic nervous system through deep breathing meditation for 20 minutes. This unique routine combining active training and static relaxation was an excellent antidote resetting his brain to pure equanimity.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neurological principles of Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew's 'post-exercise 20-minute meditation' routine and a guide for modern people to relieve burnout.

## 1. Synergy of Aerobic Catalyst and Parasympathetic Activation

Aerobic exercise intense enough to cause sweating promotes the discharge of accumulated waste in the brain and sweeps away harmful chemical substances produced during cognitive fatigue. The subsequent breathing meditation rapidly shifts the <strong>sympathetic nervous system</strong> state, which made the heart beat vigorously, to the <strong>parasympathetic nervous system</strong> state that regulates relaxation.

This rapid autonomic shift decreases the activity of the stress hormone cortisol in the shortest time, driving the brain's homeostasis to its peak. Perfect relaxation following extreme tension accelerates brain cell fatigue recovery, eliminates complicated distractions, and restores optimal sleep environment and intellectual clarity.
