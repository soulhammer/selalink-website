The Apple Snail, commonly known as the Mystery Snail (Pomacea bridgesii), is a globally popular aquatic invertebrate prized for its vibrant shell colors ranging from golden yellow and magenta to violet and blue. Outstanding scavengers of leftover food and algae, they feature long sensory tentacles and a breathing siphon. However, soft or acidic water erodes their calcium-based shells, causing pitting and deterioration. Furthermore, their instinct to lay egg clutches above the waterline makes secure tank covers mandatory to prevent fatal falls and escapes. In this guide, we decode Mystery Snail behavioral signals and present a 3-step homecare routine for shell calcium maintenance, escape prevention, and planted tank cohabitation.
