Have you ever found your container of miso or fermented soybean paste turning dark black, drying out on top, or puffing up with gas? 🍲

Even after fermentation is complete, active proteases (protein-decomposing enzymes) and free amino acids remain inside miso and doenjang. Exposure to room temperature and oxygen triggers continuous non-enzymatic Maillard browning, turning the paste dark, altering flavor, and releasing CO₂ gas through heat-resistant yeast fermentation.

Based on official guidelines from the MFDS and USDA, this 3-step scientific airtight storage guide explains how to block Maillard browning and keep the vibrant color and deep flavor of miso fresh for over a year. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Press plastic wrap directly against the surface to block air — Air-barrier Sealing 🧴</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Smooth out the paste in its container and press a layer of plastic wrap tightly onto the surface (Air-barrier). 💡 Storage Tip: Eliminating air contact stops oxygen-driven Maillard browning, surface crusting, and aerobic mold growth.
  </p>
</div>

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Store in cold refrigeration at 0–4°C — Cold Refrigeration (365+ Days) ❄️</h4>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Room temperature exposure causes Maillard browning and over-fermentation ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Exposing miso to room temperature (above 20°C / 68°F) accelerates Maillard browning, turning the paste dark red or black. Residual yeasts also cause over-fermentation, producing sourness, harsh bitterness, and CO₂ gas that swells the container.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Block air contact and prevent moisture/food contamination 🛑</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Oxygen contact causes surface oxidation, drying out the top layer and encouraging mold growth. Using wet or dirty utensils introduces micro-organisms that cause spoilage.
    </p>
  </div>
