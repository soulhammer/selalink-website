"It does not matter if I have no canvas or expensive oil paints. The moment I scratch a line onto discarded cigarette box foil with a sharp nail, all hunger and sorrow vanish, leaving only art." 🐂

Lee Jung-seob (1916–1956) was the iconic Korean modern artist whose masterworks—such as *The Bull*, *White Bull*, and *Family on the Road*—fused Fauvism and Expressionism with raw Korean emotional resonance. During the catastrophic Korean War, Lee endured grueling refugee hardship in tiny shacks across Seogwipo on Jeju Island and the port city of Busan. Separated from his beloved Japanese wife (Masako Yamamoto) and two young sons who returned to Japan, Lee suffered from severe malnutrition, intense loneliness, and deep depression.

Utterly unable to afford canvases or tubes of oil paint, Lee refused to abandon his creative drive. He scavenged discarded cigarette packets from the streets, flattened the thin interior aluminum foil sheets, and used sharp iron nails, awls, or bone styluses to score fine lines into the metallic surface. After etching his drawings, Lee r믭 oil paints, soot, or ink into the scratched grooves and wiped the flat surface clean, sealing vivid dark line drawings embedded within the shimmering foil (*Eunjihwa*). Today, these works are celebrated globally and held in prestigious collections such as the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York. Neuroscientifically, scoring metal foil engages the Somatosensory Cortex through intense haptic resistance, creating a Multisensory Feedback Loop that saturates working memory, suppresses emotional rumination, and induces profound mindfulness.

In today's post, we examine the cognitive neuroscience and art therapy principles behind Lee Jung-seob's 'tin foil drawing' routine and present a modern 3-step action guide to harness tactile focus and overcome emotional distress.

## Visual-Tactile Multisensory Feedback and Somatosensory Cortex Activation

In neuroscience, haptic and tactile stimulation strongly engages the <strong>Somatosensory Cortex</strong>, a primitive sensory processing node. Engraving lines onto metallic foil using a sharp stylus stimulates mechanoreceptors in fingertips, inducing immediate <strong>Tactile Arousal</strong> that wakes up executive prefrontal networks. This combination of haptic resistance, scratching auditory feedback, and visual intaglio depth forms a <strong>Multisensory Feedback Loop</strong>. Saturated with multi-channel sensory inputs, <strong>Working Memory</strong> buffers block intrusive anxiety and emotional rumination, bestowing a profound state of present-moment <strong>Mindfulness</strong>.

## Caution: Bypassing the Wait for Ideal Tools to Immerse Immediately in Fingertip Haptic Feedback

Avoid delaying action by blaming poor tools or waiting for flawless working conditions. The neurobiological capacity for haptic immersion exists within you regardless of medium—even on a discarded scrap of foil or paper. Surrender internal anxiety and brain fog to the physical sensation of friction as your pen or stylus engraves the surface.
