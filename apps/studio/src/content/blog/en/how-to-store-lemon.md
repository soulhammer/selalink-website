Have you ever left fresh lemons in a fruit bowl on the counter only to find them dry out, turn rock-hard, or grow blue-green mold in the fridge? 🍋

Although lemons have a thick rind, they are citrus fruits whose moisture continuously evaporates through microscopic pores on their skin. When exposed to ambient room temperature, their skins shrink and juice dries up within a week. Conversely, trapped moisture in sealed spaces causes green and blue mold (Penicillium) to thrive.

In this guide, shares a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on research from the USDA and FSA to lock in moisture, block mold, and keep your lemons bursting with juice for over a month. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Isolate damaged or soft lemons immediately ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Lemons that are bruised or starting to rot release large amounts of Penicillium mold spores. Even a single moldy lemon in a sealed bag can rapidly spoil all surrounding healthy lemons. Always sort and separate damaged lemons upon purchase.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Never wash lemons before storage 🍋</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Washing or scrubbing lemons under tap water removes their natural protective wax coating and traps surface moisture, creating an ideal environment for mold growth. Lightly wipe off dust with a dry cloth and wash lemons right before use.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
