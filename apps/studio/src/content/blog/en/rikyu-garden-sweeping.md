"Sweeping the dust of the garden is not merely removing fallen leaves, but clearing away unnecessary noise from the mind and confronting the empty space of nature."

Sen no Rikyu was a legendary tea master of Japan's Sengoku period who perfected 'Wabicha,' maximizing rustic simplicity and the beauty of margin. Even amid intense political strife and warfare, he maintained inner serenity and proudly upheld his tea philosophy before the highest rulers. The source of his profound artistry and wisdom lay in his daily morning 'garden sweeping routine,' where he meticulously swept the tea room garden with a broom.

A famous anecdote in tea history recounts that when Rikyu's son proudly claimed to have swept the garden spotlessly clean, Rikyu gently shook a maple tree, scattering a few fallen leaves onto the garden ground, remarking, "This is true cleaning." For Rikyu, cleaning was not merely a hygienic act to eliminate debris, but a tool for 'Kinesthetic Meditation.' Through repetitive sweeping physical motions and the rhythmic sound of the broom, he washed away tangled thoughts from his brain, creating mental open space.

This article analyzes the scientific connection between repetitive physical activity and the reduction of cognitive brain noise in Rikyu's garden sweeping routine, providing a 3-step actionable plan for modern individuals to induce brain tranquility and deep immersion through simple cleaning actions.

## 1. Anchoring Effect of Broom Sounds and Brain Cognitive Noise

Rhythmic sweeping delivers immediate and simple physical feedback to the brain, anchoring attention to the present. This sensory focus effectively stabilizes cognitive noise signals in the cerebral cortex that foster anxiety and distraction. According to neuroscientific research, this action suppresses unnecessary anxietyin the amygdala and activates visuospatial sketchpad circuits, dramatically enhancing long-term executive function.
