    answer: "Fire-Bellied Newts originate from cool mountain streams and ponds. Water temperatures exceeding 24°C (75°F) cause thermal stress, anorexia, fatal fungal infections, and organ failure. During summer, use aquarium cooling fans, keep the tank in a climate-controlled room, or place sealed ice packs against the outside glass to maintain 18–22°C."
  - question: "Can I handle a Fire-Bellied Newt with my bare hands?"

Additionally, their skin secretes mild toxins (tetrodotoxin family). Always use a soft aquarium net to move them, and wash your hands thoroughly if accidental contact occurs."

The Fire-Bellied Newt (Cynops species) is one of the most beloved small aquatic amphibians worldwide, renowned for its striking red or orange-red aposematic belly pattern and hardy nature. Naturally protected by mild defensive skin toxins, these semi-aquatic creatures split their time between shallow water and moist land. Because they are weak swimmers, strong filter currents easily exhaust them. Furthermore, exposure to water temperatures above 24°C (75°F) rapidly suppresses their immune system and can prove fatal. Maintaining a gentle water flow, cool temperatures (18–22°C / 64–72°F), a well-balanced paludarium layout, and target feeding using tweezers are essential for their health. In this guide, decodes Fire-Bellied Newt behavioral signals and presents a 3-step daily homecare routine for water management, habitat enrichment, and nutrition.

During summer, use aquarium cooling fans, keep the tank in a climate-controlled room, or place sealed ice packs against the outside glass to maintain 18–22°C.

Additionally, their skin secretes mild toxins (tetrodotoxin family). Always use a soft aquarium net to move them, and wash your hands thoroughly if accidental contact occurs.
