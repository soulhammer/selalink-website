Catherine the Great (1729–1796), the Enlightened Monarch who elevated 18th-century Imperial Russia into a leading European superpower through legal, cultural, and diplomatic reforms. Rising from minor German nobility of Anhalt-Zerbst to the imperial throne, she sustained an extraordinary workload through continuous reading and mental discipline, expanding borders, restructuring administration, and drafting the famous *Nakaz* (Grand Instructions). ☕📜

Behind her extraordinary executive capacity lay a strict 3-hour morning silent ritual. Every morning at 5:00 AM (6:00 AM in winter), Catherine rose independently. Before her servants woke, she lit her own stove to warm her study, brewed potent black coffee, and sat at her desk. The three hours before her official court sessions at 9:00 AM were her inviolable, solitary deep-work period. During this window, she exchanged handwritten letters with top Enlightenment thinkers like Voltaire and Diderot, refined diplomatic dispatches, and personally drafted state legislation. This morning deep work was the secret that enabled her to maintain sharp strategic clarity amidst political rivals and foreign threats.

In today's post, we dissect how Catherine the Great's 5 AM reading and statecraft routine shields against distraction and maximizes prefrontal executive function from a cognitive neuroscience perspective.

## 1. Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR) and Prefrontal Executive Function Optimization

The <strong>Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)</strong> occurring 30–45 minutes post-waking sharply enhances executive functioning within the brain's <strong>Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)</strong>, creating an optimal neurobiological state for complex decision-making and long-term strategic vision. Practicing 3 hours of <strong>'Uninterrupted Deep Work'</strong> before social demands or administrative friction prevents cognitive fragmentation caused by task-switching costs. This neural environment ensures peak synthesis of complex legislation and diplomatic strategy without incurring cognitive overload.

## 3. Caution: Preventing Sleep Deprivation and Excessive Caffeine Dependency from Misguided Perfectionism

When adopting an early morning rising habit, avoid prematurely shifting your wake-up time without securing adequate prior sleep. Waking early while sleep-deprived and relying heavily on caffeine degrades prefrontal cortex performance and depletes cognitive resources prematurely. A synchronized circadian rhythm—going to bed earlier to wake earlier naturally—must precede early rising, and dawn hours should remain strictly isolated from external communication devices.
