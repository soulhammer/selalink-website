Have you ever bought fresh mackerel only to find it smelling heavily fishy or turning mushy within a couple of days in the fridge? 🐟

Mackerel is packed with healthy omega-3 fatty acids, but because of its high autolytic enzyme activity and rich fat content, it oxidizes and turns rancid quickly upon exposure to air. Furthermore, if left at room temperature, histidine rapidly converts into toxic histamine, causing food poisoning. Proper temperature control is critical.

Today, FreshSnap shares a scientifically proven 3-step mackerel storage guide based on guidelines from NIFS and USDA to eliminate fishy odors and preserve peak freshness. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3.5 py-1.5 text-xs font-black tracking-wider bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-700 dark:bg-emerald-500/20 dark:text-emerald-300 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/30 shadow-xs">STEP 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Gut and clean blood clot — Stop Autolysis & Fishy Odor 🔪</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Remove the gills and guts, and rinse away the blood and black belly membrane along the spine under running water. 💡 Storage Tip: Viscera spoil fastest. Patting the inside and outside completely dry with paper towels is the crucial first step to stopping bacteria and fishy odor.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Tightly wrap and refrigerate — Short-Term Storage (1–2 Days) ❄️</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    To prevent fatty acids from reacting with oxygen and turning rancid, air exposure must be minimized. 💡 Storage Tip: Lightly brush the surface with cooking sake, vinegar, or salt, then wrap each piece tightly with plastic wrap without gaps. Store at 0–2°C in the fridge crisper or meat drawer for 1–2 days.
  </p>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Remove viscera and blood immediately upon purchase 🐟</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Mackerel viscera decay rapidly right after death due to autolytic enzymes, letting bacteria invade the flesh. Blood accelerates lipid oxidation and releases Trimethylamine (TMA), the main cause of fishy odor. Scrape away blood clots and viscera under cold running water.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Dry thoroughly after washing to prevent rapid oxidation 💧</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Residual surface moisture fosters bacterial growth and forms ice crystals during freezing, damaging cell tissue and leaking juices upon thawing. Always pat completely dry with paper towels after washing.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
