Warren Buffett, the chairman of Berkshire Hathaway and widely regarded as the most successful investor in history, still spends 80% of his day reading and analyzing reports. 📚

Buffett believes that knowledge builds up like compound interest. By consistently absorbing books and information every day, you can make far more accurate and wiser decisions than others over time. In a lecture, when students asked for the secret to acquiring knowledge, he pointed to a stack of books and replied, 'Read 500 pages like this every day. That's how knowledge builds up, like compound interest. All of you can do it, but I guarantee not many of you will.'

Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive science principles behind Warren Buffett's 'reading habit' and proposes a 3-step practical action plan for modern people to enjoy compound learning effects.

## 1. Brain Plasticity and Knowledge Accumulation: The Neuroscience of Reading

According to neuroscience research, the brain does not simply receive information passively when reading a book. As a research team at Emory University discovered, reading consistently enhances the brain's neural connectivity for hours, and even days. When reading, the white matter tracts in the frontal lobe and somatosensory cortex are coordinated to maximize the efficiency of brain signal transmission.

This enhancement of connectivity builds a 'Cognitive Reserve' that suppresses age-related cognitive decline and prevents dementia. Furthermore, as knowledge accumulates, the speed of retrieving information from long-term memory and identifying patterns improves exponentially. The compound effect of knowledge is literally like building highways in your neural network.
