The Pietà, David, and the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel in the Vatican—masterpieces that stand immortal in human artistic history were created by the Renaissance genius sculptor Michelangelo Buonarroti (1475–1564). Behind these monumental works lay an obsessively intense habit of flow. 🏛️

According to contemporary biographer Giorgio Vasari, when Michelangelo immersed himself in a work, he would not take off his riding boots or clothes for weeks at a time, sleeping in brief naps in the corner of his workshop while surviving on a few crusts of bread. In later years, when he finally pulled off his boots, skin came off with them; yet he channeled 100% of his cognitive resources into a single endeavor: liberating the living form trapped within the marble block.

In this article, we examine the cognitive science behind Michelangelo's minimization of daily rituals and extreme flow habit, offering practical ways for modern professionals to master decision fatigue and direct maximum focus toward their core objectives.

## 1. Minimizing Decision Fatigue and Preserving Cognitive Resources

Modern behavioral science defines the exhaustion of self-regulatory mental energy caused by trivial daily choices as 'Ego Depletion' or 'Decision Fatigue.' Deciding what to wear or eat each morning fatigues the brain's executive control center. By isolating himself and minimizing mundane decisions regarding clothing and meals, Michelangelo preserved 100% of his mental energy exclusively for creation, sustaining unprecedented artistic productivity.
