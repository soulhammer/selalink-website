Admiral Yi Sun-shin of Joseon set an unprecedented record of 23 victories in 23 battles in naval history. Facing political frame-ups, torture, the death of his son, and an overwhelming enemy fleet, where did he draw the strength to maintain composure and precise strategy? He lit a lantern every single night and meticulously recorded the day's weather, troop conditions, and his deepest sighs in Nanjung Ilgi. Writing a diary was not just a military report, but a self-healing ritual to clear away frustration and anxiety. Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive psychological effects and modern nightly journaling practice based on Admiral Yi's 'reflective diary' routine.

## 1. The Emotional Catharsis & Amygdala Calming Effect of Expressive Writing

When faced with negative emotions such as extreme fear or frustration, the brain's fear center, the <strong>Amygdala</strong>, becomes highly overheated. In this state, frontal lobe functions responsible for rational judgment and willpower are paralyzed. According to cognitive psychology studies, <strong>'Expressive Writing'</strong>, which structures these amorphous emotions by writing them down by hand, forcibly calms the abnormal activation of the amygdala. By labeling emotions and organizing them into objective facts, the brain perceives the uncontrollable situation within a controllable range, instantly freeing cognitive resources and exerting strong stress resilience.

## 3. Do Not Conscious Other's Evaluation, Write the Truth Only for Yourself
<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-indigo-500 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">

  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      To fully enjoy the emotional purification effect of a reflective diary, you must not care about others' eyes at all. If you start checking or censoring sentences due to concern like 'what if someone reads my diary', the objectification mechanism that relaxes the amygdala will not work. Just as Admiral Yi Sun-shin expressed his fear, anger, and anxiety without addition or subtraction through his brush, you must be thoroughly honest with yourself in the most private writing space to finally relieve cognitive pressure.
    </p>
