"While quietly gazing at dew-drenched blades of grass, cucumber vines, and the tiny footsteps of insects creeping underneath, complex brain fatigue is washed away, springing forth a clear well of creativity." 🌿

Shin Saimdang (1504–1551) was a prominent female artist, poet, and calligrapher of Korea's Joseon Dynasty, as well as the mother of the revered scholar Yi I (Yulgok). Her paintings depicting eggplants, grasshoppers, watermelons, and field mice are celebrated today as masterworks of Korean art history, known as Chochungdo (Paintings of Insects and Plants). The secret behind her timeless artistic depth and elegant creativity was none other than her daily morning habit of walking into her garden to meticulously observe plants and insects.

In modern life, we fix our eyes on digital stimuli such as computer monitors and smartphones, continuously draining the brain's 'Directed Attention'. When sustained, this depletes the prefrontal cortex's cognitive battery, causing mental fatigue, stress, and diminished creativity. Shin Saimdang gifted her brain deep rest each morning by quietly contemplating the organic, geometric order of nature that demands no artificial focus.

In today's post, we examine Attention Restoration Theory and the scientific principle of 'Soft Fascination' hidden within Shin Saimdang's nature observation routine, presenting a 3-step analog observation practice to recharge tired brains and open fresh creative vision.

## 1. Attention Restoration Theory and Brain Healing of 'Soft Fascination'

According to Attention Restoration Theory in environmental psychology, 'Directed Attention' used in urban environments or on digital screens causes severe cognitive drain in the prefrontal cortex, leading to fatigue. Conversely, comfortably observing natural elements like blades of grass, flowers, or breeze enters a state of 'Soft Fascination'. This recharges the frontal lobe's cognitive battery and activates the creative visualization network, drawing fresh inspirations.

## 3. Saimdang's Advice for Recharging Inspiration and Mind

Wise creativity does not spring forth by forcing your brain. Step away from your desk for a moment, quietly observe a blade of wild grass or a creeping insect, and give your brain a rest. In natural relaxation, the brain refills depleted energy and returns fresh inspiration.
