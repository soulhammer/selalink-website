Have you ever kept a bottle of yellow, Dijon, or whole-grain mustard sitting out on your dining table or in a pantry cabinet long after opening it? 🌭

Thanks to its high acid content from vinegar and salt, mustard is a naturally self-preserving condiment with high resistance to microbial growth. However, once opened, exposure to oxygen rapidly dissipates its signature pungent flavor compounds (allyl isothiocyanate), causing the mustard to darken, lose its sharp kick, and separate into liquid and solids.

In this guide, introduces a scientifically proven 3-step mustard storage guide based on safety guidelines from food safety authorities to help preserve its sharp kick and vibrant color. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not leave opened mustard at room temperature for long periods ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">

    </p>
