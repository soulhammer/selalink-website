Che Guevara, the symbol of active intellectuals who led the Cuban Revolution to success. Throughout his life, suffering from severe asthma and the extreme stress of guerrilla warfare in the jungle, what was the secret behind maintaining his bold combativeness and clear ideological depth? Che Guevara always carried thick books in his backpack during arduous marches. He immersed himself in reading inside trenches or perched on tree branches whenever combat paused. For him, reading was not an escape, but a conscious struggle to erect the spine of belief without stopping learning even in unfavorable realities. Today, BuildSelf introduces the neurological benefits and modern implementation of Che Guevara's 'jungle reading' routine.

## 1. Cognitive Restructuring & Stress Blocking via Deep Reading

Imminent environmental threats and fears lock the brain into a survival-instinct mode, hindering creative judgment. According to cognitive neuroscience, <strong>'Deep Reading'</strong>, which tracks sentences in books precisely, forces the brain into a state of 'Cognitive Restructuring'. The brain entering text activates the frontal lobe's cognitive control network and blocks stress circuits of external stimuli. Dopamine secreted while interpreting new ideas regulates anxiety and preserves macro value beliefs.

## 3. Read Deeply Thinking About Sentence Structure, Not Light Feed-Centered Speed Reading
