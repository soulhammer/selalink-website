Instead of fixing her squeaky door, she left it broken to serve as a natural alert system. Today, BuildSelf reveals how establishing sensory focus boundaries can shield your deep work.

## 1. The Cognitive Cost of Task Switching and Attention residue

Every interruption during deep focus triggers a 'cognitive switch cost.' When attention is abruptly diverted, it takes an average of 23 minutes to return to the original task due to 'attention residue' left by the interruption. Austen's creaky door functioned as an early warning cue, giving her brain a few precious seconds to wrap up her train of thought before engaging with others. In cognitive science, this is known as establishing a 'sensory boundary' to preserve mental momentum.
