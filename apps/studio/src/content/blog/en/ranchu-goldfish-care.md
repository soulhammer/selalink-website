Revered as the 'King of Goldfish' for their rounded egg-shaped body, lack of a dorsal fin, and prominent headgrowth (Wen), Ranchu Goldfish (Carassius auratus) are iconic ornamental fish beloved worldwide. However, their highly compressed body structure leaves their internal organs compacted, making them extremely susceptible to 'Swim Bladder Disorder' if they gulp air while eating floating pellets or suffer from constipation. Furthermore, missing a dorsal fin makes them weak swimmers easily exhausted by strong filter currents, while their high waste output requires robust filtration to prevent ammonia spikes and bacterial infections in their delicate headgrowth. Today, PetSelf presents a 3-step daily care routine to read your Ranchu's signals and protect its health.

## 2. Ranchu Goldfish (Carassius auratus)'s Body Language & Behavioral Signals

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/5 dark:bg-emerald-500/10 shadow-sm">

    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-600 dark:text-emerald-400 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/20">Body Signal 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Gulping Air Repeatedly at the Surface (Surface Gulping)</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    <strong>• Meaning:</strong> A critical signal of low dissolved oxygen, water pollution (ammonia/nitrite spikes), or inadequate aeration.<br/>
    <strong>• Response:</strong> Immediately increase aeration with an air pump, test ammonia levels, and perform a 20–30% partial water change.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/5 dark:bg-emerald-500/10 shadow-sm">
  <div class="flex items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-600 dark:text-emerald-400 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/20">Body Signal 2</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Floating Upside Down or Lying Motionless on the Bottom (Swim Bladder Sign / Bottom Sitting)</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    <strong>• Meaning:</strong> Indicates swim bladder impairment caused by ingesting air with floating pellets, digestive constipation, or cold temperature shock.<br/>
    <strong>• Response:</strong> Fast the fish for 1–2 days, feed deshelled cooked green peas to aid digestion, switch permanently to sinking pellets, and stabilize water temperature (18°C–23°C / 64°F–73°F).
  </p>
