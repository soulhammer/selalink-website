Franz Schubert, the genius Austrian composer who represents 19th-century Romantic music and is revered as the King of Songs (Liederfürst), produced over 600 magnificent, lyrical lieder and symphonies before dying prematurely at the young age of 31. Every morning the moment he opened his eyes, he went straight to his composing desk right next to his bed without even taking off his glasses, and began writing notes on staff paper. To avoid losing the thread of musical unconsciousness that surfaced in a drowsy half-awake state, he went so far as to sleep with his glasses on. This instant writing routine, which eliminated all friction steps between waking up and starting work, was the force that made him an immortal composer.

In today's post, we introduce the cognitive neuroscience principles behind his habit of removing all action friction between waking and starting work, along with a practical 3-step guide for modern life.

## Neuroscientific Principle Analysis of Driving Success

Immediately upon waking, the human brain maintains a creative Theta wave state before logical cognitive filters and distracting thoughts set in. Like Schubert, by completely blocking preparation steps between waking and key creative action (checking smartphones, washing face, tidying desk) to reduce action friction to zero, one can directly draw brilliant intuitive ideas latent in the unconscious onto paper.

---

## Behavioral Science Advice for Successful Adoption

Please be aware that checking smartphone notifications or messaging apps for even 10 seconds right after waking up will flood the brain with information stress from others, causing it to completely lose its unique Theta wave unconscious state.
