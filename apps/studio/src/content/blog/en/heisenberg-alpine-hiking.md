Werner Heisenberg laid the cornerstone of modern quantum physics and won the Nobel Prize. When the young Heisenberg faced cognitive burnout and insomnia from electronic orbital transition matrix calculations, he set off for the Bavarian Alps alone. For days, he hiked quietly through the coniferous forests and orderly paths of the Alps, tuning his thoughts. In the autumn of 1925, during his recovery on the remote North Sea island of Heligoland and hikes in the Alps, the uncertainty equations that marked a historical milestone in physics were born. We uncover the attention restoration principles of hiking.

## 1. Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and the Neural Circuits of Cognitive Incubation

When focusing on screens and numbers for hours, the brain depletes its 'Directed Attention' resources, falling into attention fatigue. Hiking through lush nature triggers 'Attention Restoration Theory (ART).' Nature's soft fascination allows the voluntary brain areas to rest and awakens involuntary attention, relaxing the frontal lobe. This fires up the 'Default Mode Network (DMN),' the brain's subconscious processing pathway, carrying out 'Cognitive Incubation' to rearrange complex mathematical and physical fragments in the background, catalyzing the eureka moment.
