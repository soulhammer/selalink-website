King Sejong the Great, the most respected monarch in Korean history who created Hangeul and advanced science and culture. What was the secret behind his extraordinary creativity and wise decisions despite severe workload and illness? He woke up before anyone else at 3 or 4 AM and read books under candlelight. In this quiet time, he focused on reading history and classics to prepare for academic debates with officials. Today, BuildSelf introduces the neurological principles and modern implementation of King Sejong's 'dawn reading' routine.

## 1. The Effect of Cognitive Warm-Up to Wake Up the Brain Right After Waking

Immediately after waking up in the morning, the brain is not plagued by external noise or unnecessary visual stimuli, allowing it to exert a high level of pure immersion. Neurologically, reading classics or dense text quietly in the early morning acts as a <strong>'Cognitive Warm-Up'</strong> for the brain. Early morning reading slowly awakens the neural networks responsible for logic and the frontal lobe, significantly reducing cognitive fatigue in complex decisions faced throughout the day and maximizing creative pattern-matching abilities.
