"Not learning is like sitting in a pitch-black room; reading books is like holding up a lamp."

King Sejong the Great, the greatest monarch of the Joseon Dynasty, created Hangeul (Hunminjeongeum) and orchestrated golden breakthroughs in science, culture, and defense. Despite battling chronic health issues like severe eye ailments and diabetes alongside crushing state duties, the driving force behind his extraordinary wisdom and creativity lay in his dawn routine.

Every day around 3 to 4 AM, King Sejong was the first in the palace to awake, reading classical scriptures and historical texts beneath candle lamp light. During these quiet hours, he prepared for 'Gyeongyeon' (經筵)—the daily royal policy debate and academic forum with high ministers. This early dawn study session, never skipped even amidst exhausting schedules, served as cognitive warm-up that enabled him to resolve complex national crises and engineer groundbreaking cultural innovations like Hangeul.

Here we explore the brain science behind King Sejong's 'Dawn Reading' routine and how modern individuals can easily practice a morning cognitive activation ritual.

## 1. Effects of Cognitive Warm-Up Awakening the Brain Right After Waking

Right after waking up, the brain is free from ambient digital clutter or sensory noise, creating an optimal state for pure deep focus.

Neuroscientifically, reading dense classical texts early in the morning acts as a <strong>'Cognitive Warm-Up'</strong>. Early reading gently awakens the prefrontal cortex circuits responsible for logic and decision-making, significantly lowering decision fatigue during the rest of the day and boosting creative pattern-recognition.

## 3. Choose Paper and Warm Lighting Over Screen Blue Light

The key to dawn reading is preserving the quiet, focused state your brain possesses upon waking. Checking your smartphone immediately bombards your prefrontal cortex with blue light and reactive notifications, inducing instant cognitive fatigue. As King Sejong savored paper texts under warm lamplight, keep digital screens away and use physical books to convert early calm into cognitive wealth.
