Unlike painters who worked in chaotic studios, Magritte put on a neat suit and bowler hat every morning, standing before an easel in his tidy living room. He painted with control, never dropping a spot of paint on his clothes or rug. We analyze the cognitive mechanism of how attire primes the brain for peak focus.

## 1. Enclothed Cognition: Activating Identity and Attentional Control

The brain's working memory is influenced by somatic sensations, including the clothes touching our body. Cognitive psychology terms this 'Enclothed Cognition.' Wearing professional or structured attire activates the unconscious identity mapped to those clothes (e.g., discipline, diligence). This primes the prefrontal networks, focusing attention and suppressing distractions. Magritte's suit served as a physical trigger to instruct his brain to enter work-mode, even within his comfortable home environment.
