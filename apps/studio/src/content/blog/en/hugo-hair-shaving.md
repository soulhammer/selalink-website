Victor Hugo, the monumental French writer who authored 'Les Misérables' and 'The Hunchback of Notre-Dame.' Facing an incredibly tight deadline for Notre-Dame, he resorted to an extreme measure: he shaved half of his hair and beard and locked himself in his study. Unable to go outside due to his appearance, he finished the masterpiece on time. Today, BuildSelf discusses how physical commitment devices can enforce deep creative focus.

## 1. Commitment Devices and the Psychology of Environmental Constraint

Relying on raw willpower to resist temptation is highly inefficient, rapidly depleting cognitive resources. In cognitive psychology, a 'commitment device' is a voluntary choice that locks you into a course of action by making alternative paths impossible or highly costly. Shaving his hair created a severe social penalty for leaving the house, eliminating the option to wander. By physically restricting escape routes, Hugo freed his brain's executive function from fighting temptations, channeling all mental resources into writing.
