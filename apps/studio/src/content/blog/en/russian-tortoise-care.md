Native to the arid steppes and deserts of Central Asia, the Russian Tortoise (Agrionemys horsfieldii), also known as Horsfield's Tortoise, is a beloved small diurnal reptile kept worldwide due to its manageable adult size (15–25 cm) and robust hardiness. Possessing four clawed digits on each front paw, Russian Tortoises have an intense instinct for burrowing. However, indoor captive environments expose them to severe Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD) if UVB radiation and dietary calcium are deficient. Furthermore, dehydration causes white uric acid to solidify into lethal bladder stones, while feeding fruit or animal protein triggers severe intestinal microbial collapse and gout. In this guide, presents a 3-step daily care routine to decode your Russian Tortoise's body language and protect its health.
