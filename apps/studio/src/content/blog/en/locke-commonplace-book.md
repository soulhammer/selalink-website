John Locke, the giant of Enlightenment philosophy who laid the philosophical foundation of parliamentary democracy and became the basic idea of the American Declaration of Independence. How did he, who argued that the human mind is like a blank sheet of paper with no records at birth, tabula rasa, fill his blank canvas with great knowledge? 📰

Locke kept a notebook called 'Commonplace Book' throughout his life to efficiently accumulate vast information and reading notes. He rejected the common method of filling a notebook in chronological order. Instead, he designed and drew a special alphabetical vowel index table in the front of the notebook. Whenever he recorded a new concept, medical prescription, or philosophical thought, he assigned an address to this index. Thanks to this organic record system, Locke could accurately find even minor articles written 10 years ago in just 1 minute and combine them with his thoughts.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes the cognitive connection principles of the 'indexing record' habit that John Locke obsessively stuck to for managing knowledge, and discovers how to apply it to the modern digital information flow.

## 1. Strengthening Associative Memory via Systematic Indexing

The brain's memory structure is not an independent box but an 'associative memory' network in which related information is intertwined like a net. John Locke's index recording method is like artificially arranging organic associative links from the encoding stage when the brain accepts information as memory.

The process of fixing (tagging) information into pre-committed categories instead of randomly scattering it narrows the <strong>'semantic distance'</strong> between information. Knowledge organized this way is not isolated in the brain but remains in an active standby state where it can connect at any time, dramatically reducing the search load on the frontal lobe cortex and helping recall it in 1 second when needed.
