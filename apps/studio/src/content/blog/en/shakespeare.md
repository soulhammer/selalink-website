William Shakespeare, the greatest playwright in human history and a great British writer who pioneered the horizons of English literature, created dramatic art with the sharpest and most three-dimensional insight into the abyss and contradictions of human nature, including “Hamlet” and “Macbeth.” He sat in the corner of the practice room behind the dark stage of the theater and wrote down the rhythm of the actors' intonations, the audience's real-time conversational slang, and facial expression changes depending on the situation in a pocket notebook in his coat pocket. He scraped the subtle linguistic patterns of love, jealousy, and anger that ordinary humans experience and transplanted them three-dimensionally into the lines in his tragedies and comedies. This routine of thoroughly imitating others and scraping language was the foundation for plays that have touched the hearts of mankind even 400 years later.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of his habit of ‘sitting in the corner of the theater practice room and scraping the audience’s intonation and body language’ and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of brain science principles that lead to success

The training of precisely tracking the language rhythm and emotional state of others and recording them in sentences and dialogue structures maximizes the connection between the language association area of ​​the cerebral cortex and mirror neurons. This operates as a powerful neuroscience mechanism that enhances cognitive attunement, negotiation and communication coordination skills that precisely decompose the cognitive intentions and context hidden behind the other person's actions.
