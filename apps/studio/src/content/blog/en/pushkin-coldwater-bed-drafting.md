"Should life deceive you, grieve not, nor be angry. The days of trial will pass, and joyous days will come." ✒️

Alexander Pushkin, revered as the founder of modern Russian literature for masterpieces like the poem 'If Life Deceives You' and the verse novel 'Eugene Onegin', lived a passionate, incandescent life. Behind his extraordinary productivity lay a unique morning routine that blocked external stimuli immediately upon waking and placed his brain in an instant state of high arousal.

Every morning as soon as he opened his eyes, Pushkin washed his face with cold water or massaged it to awaken his nervous system through thermal sensory stimulation. Instead of dressing or preparing to go out, he stayed in a comfortable posture in bed and vigorously wrote drafts with a pencil for 3 hours without any external interruption. During this period, Pushkin allowed no conversations with anyone nor any routine household obligations.

This routine was a strict environmental control strategy that stimulated sympathetic arousal while completely preventing the cognitive loss caused by social interactions. In today's post, we explore the cognitive neuroscience principles of autonomic arousal and blocking Attention Residue embedded in Pushkin's cold water refreshment and bed drafting habit.

## 1. Hyper-Focus Created by Cold Water Neural Arousal and Blocking Attention Residue

Applying cold water to the skin immediately after waking stimulates the sympathetic nervous system, boosting norepinephrine secretion and transitioning the brain into a state of high arousal.

Furthermore, focusing on drafting in bed for 3 hours before engaging in conversations, checking news, or opening messages completely blocks <strong>'Attention Residue'</strong> generated by social interactions. This mechanism overcomes sleep inertia while directing the brain's cognitive resources entirely toward creative production.

## 3. Building a Modern Cognitive Firewall to Protect Waking Focus

Avoid picking up your smartphone or checking emails immediately upon waking, which fractures your attention. The key is to awaken neural focus first and complete your priority task before letting the external world enter.
