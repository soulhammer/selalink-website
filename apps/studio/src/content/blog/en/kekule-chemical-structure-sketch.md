"Let us learn to dream, gentlemen, then perhaps we shall find the truth." 🧪

In 1865, August Kekulé revolutionized organic chemistry by proposing the cyclic structure of benzene, inspired by a vision of Ouroboros—a snake biting its own tail. Struggling for a long time with an unsolved chemical riddle, what was his secret to unlocking one of chemistry's greatest mysteries? 🐍

When hitting cognitive roadblocks after intense focus, Kekulé sat back on the upper deck of a London omnibus or by a quiet fireplace and closed his eyes. In that state of relaxation, visual reveries unfolded in his mind: geometric molecular particles dancing and chaining together. Upon awakening, he immediately opened his pocketbook, sketching those imaginary shapes into geometric diagrams and converting them into precise chemical structures. This 'hypnagogic sketching routine' bridged unconscious insights to reality.

In today's post, we explore the cognitive neuroscientific principles of hypnagogia and unconscious visual recombination within Default Mode Networks (DMN) behind chemist August Kekulé's post-immersion relaxation and sketching routine.

## 1. Neuroscience of Hypnagogia and Unconscious Visual Recombination

Entering a hypnagogic state of relaxation after intense immersion shifts brainwaves into alpha and theta bands, loosening the prefrontal cortex's rigid control.

During this phase, the Default Mode Network (DMN) and Visual Cortex interact closely, embodying high-level abstract concepts into geometric visual imagery previously suppressed by conscious executive control. Sketching these visions immediately upon waking translates metaphorical unconscious insights into conscious logical structures.
