"He who wins a battle makes many calculations before the battle is fought; he who loses makes few calculations beforehand." ⚔️

Sun Wu (Sun Tzu), a legendary military strategist from the State of Qi during China's Spring and Autumn period and the author of Eastern antiquity's greatest military treatise 'The Art of War', played a decisive role in helping King Helü of Wu defeat Chu and establish hegemony. Instead of relying on reckless bravery or emotional fervor, he was a legendary strategist who built winning grounds through cold, objective analysis.

Before mobilizing troops, Sun Wu always performed 'Miaosuan' (temple pre-calculation), sitting in the ancestral temple to precisely compare and calculate the strength of his forces versus the enemy across five objective variables: the Way (moral alignment/purpose), Heaven (timing/climate), Earth (terrain/distance), the General (leadership/wisdom), and Law (organization/discipline). Not a single soldier moved until odds were quantitatively proven. As recorded in Sima Qian's 'Records of the Grand Historian', pre-calculation was a solemn cognitive simulation for Sun Tzu to block optimism bias driven by desire and erase unexpected failure risks in advance.

In today's post, we introduce the neuroscientific principles of Prospective Hindsight and Optimism Bias suppression embedded in Sun Tzu's Miaosuan routine.

## 1. Pre-mortem Simulation Effects Induced by Prospective Hindsight and Optimism Bias Suppression

Quantitatively checking failure possibilities and risk factors before launching a project activates neuroscientific mechanisms of <strong>'Prospective Hindsight'</strong> and <strong>'Pre-mortem'</strong> analysis.

The human brain easily succumbs to optimism bias (Optimism Bias) and emotional impulses when initiating new endeavors. However, quantitatively simulating risk across five core variables beforehand suppresses reckless impulses in the amygdala (Amygdala) and activates the prefrontal cortex's (Prefrontal Cortex) executive functions and high-level risk assessment circuits, dramatically elevating decision-making win rates.
