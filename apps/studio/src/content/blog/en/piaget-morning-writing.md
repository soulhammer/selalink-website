Jean Piaget, the giant who changed the paradigm of cognitive psychology by proving the developmental stages of children. Behind his great academic achievements was a unique dual-rhythm habit. 📝

Piaget sat in his study early every morning and wrote at least four pages using only a pen and paper. This writing was not an organized text for others, but a process of expressing fragmented knowledge into physical words to clear the working memory of the brain. When his cognitive resources were cleared after writing, he walked for hours in the peaceful Swiss nature and lakeside in the afternoon. Surprisingly, whenever his soles were stimulated in time with his steps, the complex subjects he wrote in the morning were organically harmonized and refined in his brain.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes the cognitive science behind Piaget's morning writing and afternoon walk routine and suggests a 3-step action guide for modern people.

## 1. Harmonizing Working Memory Externalization and Default Mode Network

When we try to solve complex problems by thinking alone, the brain's working memory space quickly reaches its limit and becomes overloaded. Piaget's morning writing was a process of forcibly reducing the brain's cognitive load by recording concepts on an external medium (paper), which is called <strong>'working memory externalization.'</strong>

The afternoon walk right after clearing the working memory activates Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) through physical activity. At the same time, it starts the 'Default Mode Network (DMN),' which lights up when the brain is in a daydreaming or contemplative mode. In this process, the fragments of knowledge written down in the morning are randomly recombined in the subconscious mind, leading to elaborate logical systems and creative insights.
