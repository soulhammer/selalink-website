Have you ever frozen fresh squid only to find that pungent seafood odors transferred to all your other frozen foods? 🦑

Squid is high in moisture, and its internal organs contain dense concentrations of microorganisms and digestive enzymes (proteases). If stored uncleaned, these enzymes trigger rapid autolysis and decomposition, releasing fishy trimethylamine (TMA) odors that easily taint neighboring foods.

Today, FreshSnap shares a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on guidelines from the National Institute of Fisheries Science (NIFS) and USDA to eliminate fishy odors, prevent freezer burn, and maintain a tender, chewy texture. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Completely remove guts, ink sac, and suction cup ring debris — Block Fishy Odors & Rot 🦑</h4>
  </div>

    Squid viscera and suction cup impurities are the primary sources of fishy odors and bacterial growth. 💡 Storage Tip: Slice the mantle open, remove entrails, ink sac, and quill (backbone). Rub the tentacles with coarse salt or flour under running water to scrub away chitinous suction rings.
  </p>

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Dry surface moisture 100% and wrap tightly in plastic wrap — Cold Storage (1–2 Days) 📦</h4>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Blanching before freezing prevents toughness during long-term storage ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      When freezing squid for extended periods, blanching sliced squid in boiling water with a drop of vinegar or salt for 10–15 seconds, then cooling and drying it completely before freezing, stops enzyme activity and maintains a chewy, tender texture upon thawing.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Limit raw squid refrigeration to 1–2 days (48 hours) max ❄️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Raw seafood has soft protein structures and high water activity, allowing psychrotrophic spoilage bacteria to multiply rapidly even in the fridge (0–4°C). Clean and cook raw squid within 48 hours or freeze immediately below -18°C.
    </p>
  </div>
