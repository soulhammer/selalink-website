Alfred Hitchcock, the world-famous British maestro who redefined the grammar of thriller movies by directing immortal suspense films such as 'Psycho', 'Vertigo', and 'Rear Window'. Where did his amazing control, commanding the filming set with absolute confidence and without a single doubt, come from? 🎬

Months before starting to shoot a movie, Hitchcock sat at his study desk. He sketched every scene, camera angle, light placement, and even the actor's movements with a pencil on storyboard paper and wrote details. He believed that when the storyboard design was finished, the film was already 100% complete in his mind. Therefore, when he arrived at the set, he said it was enough to just copy the storyboard and he didn't even need to look through the camera lens. For Hitchcock, the storyboard went beyond a simple plan, acting as a mental simulation that eliminated all execution risks in advance.

## 1. 'Mental Simulation' to Eliminate Decision Fatigue

Cognitive research shows that when the brain mentally simulates or visualizes a concrete process before actually performing a behavior, the motor execution network governing the movement is similarly activated. This is called <strong>'mental simulation'</strong>. Hitchcock's habit of sketching storyboards perfectly before filming allowed him to visually experience variables and decide alternatives beforehand. This eliminates the decision fatigue that arises from unexpected variables and choices during actual work execution, helping achieve high-momentum, error-free results.

## 3. Guarding Against Plan Indulgence and Delay of Execution

  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
