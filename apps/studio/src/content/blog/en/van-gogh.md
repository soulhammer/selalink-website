Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890), one of the greatest painters in late 19th-century art history and a pioneer of Expressionism, sold only a single painting during his lifetime amid extreme poverty and mental isolation, yet left behind immortal masterpieces that captivate humanity today. As a master of French Post-Impressionism, he wrote over 660 letters to his brother Theo throughout his life to overcome loneliness and internal torment. Every night, stepping away from his canvas, he lit a candle in his room and poured out his agonizing thoughts and color inspirations in epistolary form to purify his mind.

In today's post, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles behind his habit of 'late-night letter writing to release complex emotions and creative anguish', along with a 3-step practical guide for modern individuals.

## 1. Neuroscientific Analysis of Emotional Purification and Focus

Epistolary reflection that objectifies and records emotions through writing strengthens prefrontal cognitive control and regulates excessive anxiety in the amygdala. Translating emotions into sophisticated language suppresses limbic system arousal in a top-down manner, enhancing metacognitive resolution to restore mental clarity and psychological resilience.

## 3. Behavioral Science Advice for Successful Habit Formation

Do not try to exhaustively write about every emotional wound experienced during the day in a lengthy diary. A light 3-minute exercise of noting emotional cues to objectify them is sufficient. Rather than relying solely on willpower, designing a physical environment and habit cues that naturally trigger action is key to sustainability.
