Bruce Lee, the immortal icon of martial arts who combined Jeet Kune Do and existential philosophy. His fluid movements and deep philosophical remarks were not simple improvisations, but the accumulation of meticulously recorded ideas. 📓

Bruce Lee always carried a small, palm-sized black leather notebook and a pen in a pocket. Whether reading books, waiting in a movie set, having tea with friends, or walking the streets, he immediately opened his notebook whenever an inspiration crossed his mind. His notebook was packed with detailed gym workout logs, dietary calculations, and deep philosophical reflections. These notes served as a firm anchor that guided his life without wavering.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes the cognitive science behind Bruce Lee's pocket memo habit and proposes a practical routine for modern people to accumulate fleeting ideas into fuel for growth.

## 1. Stimulating the Reticular Activating System (RAS) and Information Processing

The human brain receives millions of bits of information while awake, but deletes most of it immediately through cognitive filters unless it concerns survival or current interests.

Jotting down ideas immediately on a physical pad strongly stimulates the <strong>'reticular activating system (RAS)'</strong> located in the brainstem. The RAS acts as a filter that prioritizes information the brain deems important. By writing down thoughts, you tell your brain, "This is important, so preserve it and gather related external inputs." As a result, the brain becomes highly sensitive to detecting clues and ideas needed to achieve goals in daily life, while resolving short-term memory memory leakage.
