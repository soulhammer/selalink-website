"I really want to clear my life to make it so that I have to make as few decisions as possible about anything except how to best serve this community." 👕

Mark Zuckerberg is the founder and CEO of Meta, a massive social media empire connecting over 3 billion people worldwide. Operating at the heart of an ever-changing tech business landscape where he decides the direction of multi-billion dollar projects daily, his morning routine is surprisingly simple. Zuckerberg's closet contains dozens of identical gray t-shirts and blue jeans. When asked during a 2014 town hall meeting why he wears the same clothes every day, he explained that he wanted to minimize trivial daily choices to focus all his mental energy on core decisions that serve his community. This post reveals the brain science behind this simple habit and the mechanism to maximize decision-making efficiency.

## 1. Protecting the Brain's Finite Glucose Resources and Preventing Ego Depletion

The energy used by the prefrontal cortex during decision-making is finite. As decisions accumulate, brain cells consume glucose and oxygen, leading to reduced executive control known as 'Ego Depletion.' Trivial daily choices like 'What should I wear this morning?' or 'What should I eat for lunch?' drain executive function capacity. By automating routine processes and adopting a single uniform like Zuckerberg, the prefrontal cortex preserves its glucose budget for vital business decisions and long-term strategy, significantly enhancing decision quality.

## 3. Cautions & Tips

A common mistake when adopting a uniform routine is stressing over how others perceive your clothing consistency. If you obsess over fashion style or aesthetic perfection, it creates new psychological strain on the brain, adding decision fatigue rather than reducing it. The key is choosing a simple, comfortable style that requires zero conscious thought, freeing your mind entirely from external evaluation.
