Ashoka the Great, the holy king who unified most of the ancient Indian subcontinent, opened a golden age for the Maurya Empire, and transformed from a ruthless conqueror into a guardian of nonviolence and peace. After witnessing the horrific tragedy of the conquest of Kalinga, what was his secret to controlling his power and desires throughout his life and adhering to righteous ethical leadership? 🏛️

King Ashoka inscribed his guilt, reflection, and the peace and moral values (Dharma) that the empire had to uphold onto massive rocks and pillars erected at key crossroads throughout his realm. Rather than merely instructing his subjects, he—as king—personally reflected on the inscriptions on the pillars every day to check if his actions aligned with his core values. The inscriptions etched into solid rock served as a powerful Visual Anchor that remained unshaken by fickle human emotions.

In today's post, we reveal the neuroscientific working principles behind King Ashoka's 'value inscription reflection' routine and present a 3-step practical guide for modern individuals to restore their core values in daily life.

## 1. External Value Visual Anchor and the Prefrontal Self-Identity Enhancement Mechanism

Human emotions and momentary desires fluctuate frequently under the influence of the hypothalamus and limbic system. When you externalize your core beliefs into visible physical structures like King Ashoka, the brain immediately activates the <strong>'Visual Anchoring'</strong> effect upon recognizing the physical text.

The act of seeing and reading your core values at a set time every day strongly stimulates the <strong>'Self-Referential Processing'</strong> circuit in the prefrontal cortex, helping you proactively choose the right actions you must uphold, even amid momentary temptations or fatigue.

## 3. Beware of Value Declarations Becoming One-Off Events and Empty Formalism

If you write a value card nicely, stick it up, and never read it again, no neuroscientific anchoring effect will occur. As King Ashoka reflected on the pillar inscriptions every day, the core lies in 'daily repetition of visually checking and reciting'.
