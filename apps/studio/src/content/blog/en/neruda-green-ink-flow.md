"Green is the color of hope and the color of nature where my poetry breathes." ✒️

Pablo Neruda (1904–1973), a Chilean national treasure and poet praised as "the greatest poet of the 20th century in any language," who received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1971. Publishing 'Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair' at the age of 19, he touched the hearts of readers worldwide. Serving as both a diplomat and politician representing the Chilean people, he sang of nature, love, and humanity from his coastal cottage in Isla Negra, southern Chile.

Throughout his life, Neruda maintained an almost obsessively unique writing habit: composing all his poems, autobiographical manuscripts, diplomatic correspondence, and personal letters exclusively in 'Green Ink'. To him, black ink was a cold, rigid color of oppression, reminiscent of death certificates and official bureaucracy. Green, by contrast, was the color of his soul, symbolizing the Pacific coastal forests, moss, hope, and the vitality of nature.

As green ink vividly saturated the white paper, Neruda experienced a poetic flow where visual cortex tension relaxed and suppressed inspiration surged like a spring. Neuroscientifically, green light wavelengths (520–550nm) deliver the gentlest natural stimulation to the retina and visual cortex, shifting brainwaves into an Alpha Wave state—the epitome of cognitive color psychology that triggers tension-free deep flow (Flow State).

## 1. Why Green Light Wavelengths Reduce Visual Fatigue and Stimulate Brain Alpha Waves

According to research in cognitive color psychology and neuroscience, the green light spectrum (520–550nm) represents the natural color zone that exerts the lowest stress on the retina and visual cortex. This visual comfort induces neural Alpha Waves, triggering an effortless, highly focused Flow State.
