Elizabeth I, the great Queen of England who led the Golden Age in the 16th century. She faced national rebellions and wars under enormous political pressure. 👑

The secret to guarding her logical mind was none other than translating Latin classics for the first 1 hour before morning duties. Waking up early, she translated the dense and difficult Latin philosophy texts of Seneca and Cicero into English. This parallel mapping of grammar sharpened her brain, helping her distinguish complex political situations with a logical eye.

Today, BuildSelf looks at the brain science behind Elizabeth I's 'classical translation' routine and how to fully activate logical thinking in the busy morning.

## 1. Effect of Translation Work on the Brain's Frontal Cortex

Translation activates not only the visual character recognition area but also the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) responsible for high-level logical rule switching. Dissimilar to reading, translating is a cognitive control training where the processor runs at 100% capacity. This develops the ability to understand conceptual depth and identify errors in complex causal relations.
