Do you habitually store your milk cartons lined up on the refrigerator door shelves? 🥛

The refrigerator door pocket is the area exposed to warm room air every time it opens, causing the most severe temperature fluctuations. Unstable temperatures allow cold-tolerant bacteria to multiply quickly, accelerating spoilage and ruining flavor.

In this guide, shares a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on hygiene guidelines from the FDA and MFDS to keep your milk rich, creamy, and fresh. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Avoid touching the spout and drinking directly from the carton 🥛</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Touching the carton spout with fingers or drinking directly introduces oral bacteria into the milk. These bacteria multiply rapidly even under refrigeration, so always pour milk into a clean glass.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Discard immediately if curdled or emitting a sour odor ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      If milk curdles into clumps at the bottom of the carton or smells sour like spoiled yogurt, bacteria have broken down the lactose and proteins. Heat will not destroy these toxins, so discard the entire carton immediately.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
