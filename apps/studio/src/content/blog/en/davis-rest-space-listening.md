"Music is not in the notes you play, it's in the silence between the notes." 🎺

Miles Davis, a legendary trumpeter and relentless innovator who embodied the history of modern jazz—from bebop and cool jazz to hard bop and jazz fusion. As one of the most influential musicians in 20th-century history, he was a visionary who stripped away unnecessary notes to sculpt a deeply lyrical, understated 'aesthetic of space' during an era dominated by flashy, hyper-fast playing. His landmark albums, such as *Kind of Blue*, stand as monumental achievements that redefined the landscape of modern music.

The secret behind his ability to build a restrained aesthetic amidst complexity and leave an indelible impression on listeners lay in his 'rest-space listening' routine. Every night in bed, Davis listened to recordings of his previous performances. Rather than focusing on flashy melodic lines, he obsessively analyzed the length of 'the empty space where playing stopped (rests)', the tempo of breath, and the balance of silence between notes.

Instead of continually filling the air with notes, he meticulously sculpted silence and tailored the exact placement of rests, giving even the unplayed spaces dramatic tension and resonance. In today's post, we introduce the cognitive science principles of Inhibitory Auditory Processing and minimalist decision-making embedded in Miles Davis's rest-space analysis routine.

## 1. Inhibitory Auditory Processing and Brain Mechanisms Maximizing Sensory Silence

Deliberately pausing performance within continuous stimulation and placing silence activates the brain's Inhibitory Auditory Processing and neural clearing mechanisms in the auditory cortex. When notes flow continuously, the brain gradually habituates to stimuli, diminishing emotional impact. However, the instant silence or a rest is introduced, the auditory cortex switches to a state of heightened novelty detection, powerfully absorbing the lingering resonance of the preceding note.

Furthermore, analyzing the duration of unplayed space engages the prefrontal cortex's metacognitive evaluation system. This minimalist selection—stripping away redundant overstimulation to leave only essential sound—is an optimal neural resource strategy that prevents cognitive overload and maximizes message delivery.

## 3. Focus on the Value of Space by Emptying Rather Than Blindly Filling

Davis's routine was not a practice to add flashy technique, but a minimalist analysis to subtract unnecessary overstimulation. When modern individuals attempt to constantly fill work or conversations without pause, severe cognitive fatigue accumulates in the brain. Focus intentionally on pausing or placing empty spaces (rests) between tasks to enhance the lingering resonance and impact of your message.
