Vladimir Nabokov, who showed the peak of 20th-century English literature with elaborate structures in Lolita. Surprisingly, he did not write his novels in chronological order. Instead, he wrote dialogue, descriptions, and plot fragments on 3x5 inch index cards. Nabokov laid these cards on his desk and rearranged them freely, even carrying them in his car or while collecting butterflies. We explore why this modular writing method is a powerful cognitive strategy for complex projects and writing.

## 1. Neural Mechanisms of Preventing Cognitive Load and Combinatorial Creativity

The brain's working memory has a limited capacity for processing information at once. Attempting to write complex structures all at once causes cognitive overload, leading to mental blocks and procrastination. Externalizing thoughts into small modular cards empties the working memory, allowing higher-level analysis and creative intuition to emerge. Additionally, physically rearranging cards stimulates the association cortex, sparking unexpected creative connections.
