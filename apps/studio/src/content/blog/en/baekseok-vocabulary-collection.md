"Language is a vessel holding a nation's emotion, and a single word breathes with the warmth and scenery of life." ✍️

Baek Seok, a modern icon of the 1930s who built one of the most original and sensory language aesthetics in modern Korean poetry history, was a passionate collector of native words. Wandering through the Hamgyong, Pyongan, and Manchurian regions, he captured the warm, affectionate vernacular of his homeland into vibrant poetic narratives.

Baek Seok meticulously recorded in a small notebook regional dialects, folklore terminology, and evocative ancient words passed down orally by marketplace folks and village elders. Sophisticated, sensory poetic diction embedded in his collection 'Deer' originated not from random inspiration, but from a structured 'vocabulary database' accumulated in his notebooks. As attested in Ahn Do-hyun's biography, collecting vocabulary was Baek Seok's cognitive routine and creative asset to preserve the breath of native language during an oppressive colonial era while maximizing his linguistic reach.

In today's post, we introduce the neuroscientific principles of Semantic Memory expansion and Semantic Priming embedded in poet Baek Seok's native vocabulary collection routine.

## 1. Richness of Expression Induced by Semantic Memory Expansion and Semantic Priming

Discovering and recording new words or striking expressions expands the brain's <strong>'Semantic Memory'</strong> system and induces a powerful <strong>'Semantic Priming'</strong> effect.

Moving beyond a fixed vocabulary state to externalize and store novel, compelling diction dense-packs the semantic network (Semantic Network) in the temporal lobe. This aids rapid lexical retrieval (Lexical Retrieval) from the brain during planning or writing, dramatically elevating prefrontal expressiveness and sentence construction capabilities.

## 3. Avoid Simple Word Lists and Collect Context and Emotion Together

The core of Baek Seok's vocabulary collection was not listing dictionary definitions, but recording the situational, emotional, and sensory context where words were used. Contextless word lists fail to activate properly in neural networks, so always note the emotional nuance and usage context alongside the word.
