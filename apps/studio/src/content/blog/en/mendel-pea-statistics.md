Gregor Mendel, the father of genetics who unlocked how parental traits are inherited. In a mid-19th century monastery without proper lab equipment, how did he discover the laws of inheritance? 🫛

The secret lay in his meticulous 'pea statistics journal' habit, which he practiced for 8 years in the St. Thomas Monastery garden. Every afternoon, Mendel went to the garden to plant and cultivate peas, examining 7 traits like flower color and stem length. He recorded numerical data in a ledger with zero error. He observed 29,000 pea plants. This daily analog tracking laid the foundation of modern genetics.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive science of Mendel's data collection routine and how modern people can boost working memory through self-data tracking.

## 1. Working Memory Training That Restructures Data Analysis and Cognition

Observing details and writing numerical data daily activates the brain's <strong>working memory</strong> in the frontal lobe. Working memory functions like the brain's RAM, temporarily holding and processing information. Sorting through a large amount of morphological data (like round vs. wrinkled seeds) stimulates the inferior parietal lobe, boosting mathematical reasoning and spatial perception. Manually reviewing this data trains the brain to filter noise and identify hidden patterns.
