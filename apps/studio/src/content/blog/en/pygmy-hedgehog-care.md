The African Pygmy Hedgehog is a quiet, fascinating exotic pet known for its twitching snout and cautious nature. Naturally shy, they rely on their quills for defense and paint foamy saliva onto their backs when encountering new scents. Furthermore, if room temperatures drop even slightly, they risk entering fatal hibernation shock (torpor). Today on PetSelf, we outline hedgehog quill behaviors, safe self-anointing handling tips, and a 3-step homecare routine to maintain 24-hour warmth and cleanliness.

## 1. African Pygmy Hedgehog Instincts: Self-Anointing Froth and Quill Defense

  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    When African Pygmy Hedgehogs taste or smell something unfamiliar, they generate foamy saliva in their mouth and spread it across their quills—a behavior known as 'Self-Anointing'. This is a normal wild instinct used to disguise their scent. Conversely, when frightened, they curl into a tight ball and emit sharp 'Hissing' sounds to warn off physical contact.
