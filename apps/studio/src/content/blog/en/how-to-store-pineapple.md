Have you ever bought a sweet, juicy pineapple only to find it molding at the bottom or sour at the top and sweet only at the base? 🍍

Pineapples are classic non-climacteric fruits, meaning their sugar content does not increase after harvest. Because natural sugars concentrate heavily at the base (bottom), leaving a pineapple upright causes an uneven sugar distribution and encourages bottom rot. Additionally, prolonged exposure to cold temperatures below 7°C causes chilling injury, turning the flesh brown.

In this guide, introduces a scientifically proven 3-step pineapple storage guide recommended by agriculture and food safety experts to evenly distribute sweetness and maximize freshness. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Cut off the crown (top leaves) and store upside down — Sweetness Redistribution Hack 🔄</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Natural sugars sink and concentrate at the base of the fruit. 💡 Storage Tip: Slice off the leafy crown, wrap the pineapple in paper towels or newspaper, and stand it upside down on its top end for 1–2 days at room temperature or in the fridge. This allows gravity to redistribute sugars evenly throughout the flesh.
  </p>
</div>

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Store cut fruit in airtight containers in the fridge — Cold Storage (5–7 Days) 📦</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">

  </p>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not leave whole pineapples at room temperature to ripen ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Pineapples are non-climacteric fruits whose sweetness is locked at harvest. Leaving them at room temperature does not make them sweeter; instead, acidity drops and fermentation (spoilage) begins. Consume promptly after purchase.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Avoid direct cold exposure below 7°C (Prevent Chilling Injury) ❄️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      As tropical fruits, pineapples stored below 7°C (45°F) for extended periods suffer chilling injury, causing flesh browning and hardening of the core. Use the refrigerator crisper drawer for optimal cold storage.
    </p>
  </div>
