"When trapped in complex equations, nothing awakens the brain with absolute clarity like vigorous physical movement." ⚡

James Clerk Maxwell, revered alongside Isaac Newton and Albert Einstein as one of history's greatest physicists, formulated the unified theory of electromagnetism. Engaging in continuous high-level theoretical physics and intricate mathematical calculations, he constantly faced severe cognitive fatigue and brain overload.

Yet Maxwell possessed a unique nighttime reset routine that maintained his research immersion at peak levels. Every night at precisely 10 PM, even while solving complex formulas, he stopped working, left his study, and headed to the long attic hallway. For 10 minutes, he ran intensely back and forth down the hallway until his footsteps echoed, finishing with quick stretching before returning to his desk.

This 10-minute high-intensity burst served as an advanced neuroscientific strategy to rapidly flush metabolic waste accumulated in the prefrontal cortex during continuous intellectual work. In today's post, we introduce the cognitive neuroscience principles of cerebral blood flow redistribution and Cognitive Fatigue reset embedded in Maxwell's 10 PM attic running habit.

## 1. Cerebral Blood Flow Redistribution and Cognitive Fatigue Reset Driven by Short High-Intensity Aerobic Exercise

Prolonged high-level cognitive tasks accumulate metabolic byproducts in the prefrontal cortex, inducing <strong>'Cognitive Fatigue'</strong> and sharply declining attentional control.

An intense 10-minute run at 10 PM redistributes stagnant cerebral blood flow throughout the body and stimulates catecholamine secretion (dopamine and norepinephrine), inducing a <strong>'Prefrontal Reset'</strong>. This scientific routine swiftly restores an overloaded brain into a state of high arousal, maximizing focus and immersion for late-night research.
