Henri Matisse, the master of Fauvism, freed intense colors and set the direction of 20th-century art. Even for this genius, a moment of extreme physical pain arrived at the end of his life when he couldn't hold a paintbrush. ✂️

After undergoing multiple abdominal cancer surgeries, Matisse was confined to a wheelchair or bed. Unable to paint on heavy canvases, he began 'Découpage' (paper cut-outs). In bed, he used long scissors to cut vibrant shapes of leaves, birds, and figures painted by assistants, arranging them on walls. This routine of fine-motor scissors work blocked his pain pathways and stimulated dopamine.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neuroscientific principles of Matisse's paper-cutting habit and proposes a practical visual-tactile calming routine using paper scissors for modern people.

## 1. Visual-Tactile Feedback to Distract Pain and Stimulate Neuroplasticity

When the brain is under physical pain or mental stress, simply lying down lets the brain focus on that discomfort. Fine motor control tasks like holding scissors and cutting paper stimulate the motor cortex and visual association areas simultaneously. In addition, the rich colors of assistant-painted papers trigger dopamine and endorphins. Immersing in this high-density visual-tactile feedback disrupts pain pathways to the frontal lobe, reducing suffering and triggering positive <strong>neuroplasticity</strong>.
