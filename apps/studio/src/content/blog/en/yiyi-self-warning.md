"He who examines every morning the boundaries he set for himself will not be swayed by daily impulses and can fully govern his own life." 📜

Yi I (Yulgok) was a paramount Neo-Confucian scholar and statesman during the Joseon Dynasty. Known as 'Gudo Jangwon-gong' for placing first in nine state civil service examinations, he dedicated his life to reforming state governance. Behind his monumental academic and political achievements lay 'Jagyeongmun' (Self-Warning Text)—a personal manifesto and rigorous behavioral code he wrote at age 20 to end his period of wandering after the death of his mother, Shin Saimdang. Yi I recited these self-imposed moral and practical rules every morning throughout his life.

While many modern individuals set morning goals, they quickly forget them when faced with immediate distractions. Rather than obeying rules enforced by others, Yi I conducted a daily ritual of instilling self-defined rules into his mind. Neuroscientifically, this daily declaration activates the prefrontal planning network, maintaining peak cognitive control. This post analyzes the self-reference effect and willpower control behind Yi I's Jagyeongmun routine, offering a 3-step modern practice guide.

## 1. The Brain Science of the Self-Reference Effect and Goal-Directed Declarations

In neuroscience, the Self-Reference Effect describes how processing information in relation to oneself activates the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), leading to stronger long-term memory and value internalization. Yi I's daily reading of his self-imposed rules continually reinforced this neural pathway. Instilling these principles into the brain every morning activates cognitive control networks, serving as an internal GPS to manage impulses and align decisions with long-term goals.

## 3. A Word on Self-Vigilance and Preserving Autonomy
