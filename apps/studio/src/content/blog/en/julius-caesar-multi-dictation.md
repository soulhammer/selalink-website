Julius Caesar, the hero who expanded the territory of the late Roman Republic and laid the foundation of the empire. He had his own superhuman time-saving secret.

While moving on horseback or carriage, he carried 3 or 4 scribes side by side, and dictated letters, military instructions, and historical records on different topics simultaneously. It was a parallel workflow that quickly shifted cognition to another topic while calling a sentence to one scribe. This parallel processing was the source of his overwhelming administrative capacity.

Today, BuildSelf looks at the cognitive principles behind Caesar's 'Simultaneous Dictation' and how modern businessmen can emulate it to maximize task switching.

## 1. Brain Plasticity That Increases Working Memory Capacity

Working memory is a kind of cognitive 'workbench' where the brain temporarily holds and processes information during decision making. This workbench has a limited capacity, so sudden transitions overload it, causing the previous task to be forgotten.

Caesar's simultaneous dictation requires high attention control to activate the logic needed for the next scribe while the brain temporarily preserves the work information of each scribe. Brain science shows that this intentional fast task switching training strengthens the network of the prefrontal cortex, expanding working memory capacity and improving cognitive flexibility.
