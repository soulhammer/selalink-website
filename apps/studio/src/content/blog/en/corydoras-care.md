faqs:
  - question: "Why are my Corydoras' barbels melting away or eroding (Barbel Erosion)?"
    answer: "Corydoras barbels are crucial sensory organs used to forage for food in substrate. Sharp, coarse gravel creates micro-cuts on the barbels, and when uneaten food rots on the substrate bed, opportunist bacteria invade these wounds, causing 'barbel erosion' or melting. To prevent this, always use soft, fine sand and siphon substrate detritus regularly."

Native to the freshwater rivers of South America's Amazon basin, the Corydoras Catfish ('Cory') is a beloved bottom-dwelling armored catfish cherished by aquarists worldwide for its cute barbels and industrious bottom-scanning swimming style. While famously peaceful, Corydoras catfish possess delicate sensory barbels around their mouths that easily erode or develop bacterial infections if kept on sharp gravel or dirty substrates. Furthermore, they are highly sensitive to nitrogenous waste buildup (ammonia and nitrite) and possess a specialized physiological mechanism for 'intestinal respiration,' darting to the surface to gulp air when dissolved oxygen drops. In this guide, presents a 3-step daily care routine to decode your Corydoras' body language and protect its health.
