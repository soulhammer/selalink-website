Have you ever bought fresh shallots for French or Southeast Asian dishes, only to find them sprouting or covered in blue mold shortly after? 🧅

Shallots feature higher dry matter solids and alliin genome concentrations than standard bulb onions, giving them rich culinary value. However, when relative humidity exceeds 70%, their physiological dormancy breaks, triggering rapid sprouting and blue mold (*Penicillium*) growth.

Based on official guidelines from the UC Davis Postharvest Technology Center and USDA, this 3-step scientific storage guide demonstrates how to keep shallots fresh for over 6 months without softening or sprouting. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3.5 py-1.5 text-xs font-black tracking-wider bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-700 dark:bg-emerald-500/20 dark:text-emerald-300 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/30 shadow-xs">STEP 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Store unpeeled shallots in a cool, dark, ventilated area at 10–12°C — Room Ventilation (180+ Days) 🧅</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Whole shallots maintain stable dormancy when kept at 10–12°C (50–54°F) with 60–65% relative humidity, minimizing respiration. 💡 Storage Tip: Keep in a mesh bag or wire basket inside a dark, cool pantry or shade to preserve freshness for 6+ months.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Refrigerate peeled shallots in an airtight container — Peeled Cold Storage (14–21 Days) ❄️</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Once peeled, exposure to open air dries out the flesh and invites bacterial contamination. 💡 Storage Tip: Thoroughly dry peeled shallots, place them in a paper towel-lined airtight container, and store in the fridge (1–4°C / 34–39°F) for 2–3 weeks.
  </p>
</div>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not refrigerate unpeeled whole shallots in plastic bags ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Storing whole unpeeled shallots in high-humidity refrigerator compartments traps moisture and breaks dormancy. The tissue collapses rapidly and fosters blue mold (*Penicillium*) growth. Always store them at cool room temperatures in a dry, ventilated space.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Avoid storing near potatoes or in direct sunlight 🛑</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Moisture and gases emitted by potatoes promote sprouting in shallots. Direct sunlight increases internal temperature and respiration rates, causing them to wither quickly. Use breathable mesh bags kept in dark areas.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
