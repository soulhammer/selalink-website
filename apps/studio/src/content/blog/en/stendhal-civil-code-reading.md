Stendhal, famous for Scarlet and Black, is celebrated for his objective and transparent prose that captured the complexities of human ambition. During an era saturated with flowery Romanticism, Stendhal insisted on mathematical precision in his sentences. His secret morning routine before writing was reading a few pages of the Napoleonic Civil Code (French Civil Code). We analyze how Stendhal used dry, logical legal texts to prime his brain, stripping away rhetorical fluff and sharpening his objective analytical power.

## 1. Verbal Priming and Emotional Cognitive Inhibition

The psychological concept of Priming shows that prior exposure to specific stimuli unconsciously shapes subsequent cognitive actions. By reading the dry, structured sentences of the Civil Code right before writing, Stendhal primed his brain's language processing centers for objectivity and logic. This conceptual priming triggers cognitive inhibition, letting the brain filter out emotional adjectives and sentimental rhetoric. Consequently, it primes the mind to construct clear, fact-driven narrative structures.
