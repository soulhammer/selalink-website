Louisa May Alcott, the great American novelist who touched countless readers by writing the global classic novel 'Little Women'. How did she focus on writing without being disturbed when her large family lived together in a bustling house? 🎀

Whenever Alcott started writing, she tied a special black velvet ribbon to the doorknob or a corner of her desk. This ribbon was a silent promise and visual signal to her family, called the 'Mood Ribbon', meaning she was in a state of high concentration and should not be disturbed. When she finished focusing, she removed the ribbon or replaced it with a bright ribbon, gently signaling that visitors and conversation were welcome. Through this clever boundary setting, Alcott protected her creative space and created immortal masterpieces.

## 1. Distraction Shielding to Eliminate Cognitive Reboot Waste

When the flow is interrupted by external noise or someone's question in a state of high concentration, the brain immediately switches to multitasking mode and consumes huge energy. Cognitive science calls this <strong>'task-switching cost'</strong> and reports that it takes an average of 23 minutes to reboot distracted attention back to its original state. The black ribbon Alcott used was a perfect <strong>'distraction shielding'</strong> that removed distraction stimuli in advance by sending a focus signal to the surrounding environment. This visual reminder helps secure the workflow without friction with surrounding people and allows the cognitive resources of the working memory to be used entirely in one place.

## 3. Creating an Agreed Focus Signal Based on Mutual Respect

    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">

If you suddenly close the door of communication without prior promise, it can make others uncomfortable.
