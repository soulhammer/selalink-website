Michael Faraday, who laid the foundation of electric civilization by discovering electromagnetic induction. Although he lacked a formal mathematical background, the key to his precise physical laws was in his notebook. Throughout his life, he assigned sequential numbers to his observations and ideas in his journal. His research archives were indexed from number 1 up to 16,041. Let's find out how this precise record-keeping compensated for cognitive limits and unlocked his genius.

## 1. Impact of Record Indexing and Knowledge Externalization on Working Memory

The brain's working memory capacity is highly limited. If information is randomly scattered without system or kept only inside the head, cognitive load spikes, impairing analytical power. However, by establishing knowledge externalization (numbering and indexing notes like Faraday), the brain dramatically saves search energy. The brain offloads physical knowledge to external storage, focusing its active cognitive resources entirely on complex reasoning tasks.
