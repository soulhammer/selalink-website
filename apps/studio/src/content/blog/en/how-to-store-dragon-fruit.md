Have you ever stored dragon fruit (Pitaya) in your refrigerator only to find its peel covered in black spots and its flesh turned soft and watery? 🌵

Unlike passion fruit or bananas, dragon fruit is a non-climacteric fruit, meaning its sugar content will not increase after harvest; it simply wilts over time. The optimal storage temperature to maintain its red antioxidant pigment, betacyanin, and high moisture content is 7–10°C (45–50°F). Exposure to temperatures below 5°C (41°F) leads to physiological chilling injury, causing internal breakdown, watery flesh, and dark surface spots.

In this guide, we share a scientifically proven 3-step dragon fruit storage guide based on safety guidelines from the USDA, FDA, and MFDS to safely preserve its crisp texture and antioxidant nutrients. 🛡️
