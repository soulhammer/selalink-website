"When I quietly turn on the lamp in my room and read the classics, only then do I meet my true soul behind the silver screen." 📖

Marilyn Monroe (1926–1962) was the most enchanting icon in 20th-century pop culture history and the paramount star of her era. Gracing the silver screen in classic films such as 'Gentlemen Prefer Blondes', 'The Seven Year Itch', and 'Some Like It Hot', she captivated global audiences with her radiant smile and iconic platinum glamour.

Yet behind the spotlights, forced to live as public property, Monroe suffered from intense psychological stress, identity fragmentation, severe anxiety, and insomnia. Contrary to superficial public stereotypes, the true Marilyn was a deeply passionate bibliophile who amassed a personal collection of over 400 literature and philosophy books—including James Joyce's 'Ulysses', Oscar Wilde, Sigmund Freud, Walt Whitman, and Marcel Proust. Every night after studio lights dimmed, she sat under the soft warm glow of her bedroom lamp, immersing herself in classic literature and penning personal poems and emotional journals.

In today's post, we examine the neuroscientific mechanisms of nervous system relaxation and identity preservation behind Monroe's nightly reading routine, offering a reflective reading guide for weary modern individuals.

## 1. Autonomic Nervous System Relaxation and Hippocampal Memory Consolidation Through Literary Reading

When the brain is exposed to prolonged external evaluation and performance stress, sympathetic nervous system hyperarousal leads to chronic fatigue and sleep disorders. According to cognitive studies in the UK, reading literary works quietly for just 6 minutes reduces muscle tension and slows heart rate, slashing stress levels by up to 68%. Immersing oneself in classical narratives allows the brain to disengage from its own ego and reach a restorative state through narrative empathy. Furthermore, post-reading journaling prompts the hippocampus in the cerebral cortex to consolidate stressful memories peacefully, optimizing glymphatic brain cleansing during sleep.

## 3. Cautions and Practical Tips

The most important behavior to avoid during nighttime reflective reading is reading under bright fluorescent light or staring at smartphone screens. Bright artificial blue light blocks the secretion of melatonin, the sleep-inducing hormone, worsening insomnia. Therefore, when reading at night, use only a single indirect lamp with a warm yellow tone, maintaining environmental control so your brain can safely transition into sleep mode.
