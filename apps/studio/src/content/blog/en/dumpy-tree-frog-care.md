The White's Tree Frog (Litoria caerulea), also known as the Dumpy Tree Frog, is a popular arboreal amphibian famous for its plump, docile appearance and friendly expression. While hardy compared to other amphibians, they possess highly permeable skin through which they absorb water and oxygen directly via osmosis. Exposure to tap water chlorine, heavy metals, or chemicals from bare hands (soaps, lotions, salts) can cause severe skin burns or toxic absorption. Additionally, perpetually high humidity leads to bacterial and fungal dermatological infections, making proper ventilation, water quality management, and nighttime humidity regulation vital. Today on PetSelf, we detail White's Tree Frog skin signals, handling precautions, and a 3-step care routine.
