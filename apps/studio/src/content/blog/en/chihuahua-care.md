Chihuahuas, originating from Mexico, are the smallest dogs in the world but boast a courageous, lion-like personality. Known for their large ears, big eyes, and proud steps, they are fiercely loyal to their owner. 🐕

However, their tiny frames come with serious structural vulnerabilities. First is the Molera (open fontanelle), an opening in their skull bones. Pressure on their forehead translates directly to the brain, requiring special handling. Also, they have minimal body fat and high metabolic rates, making them susceptible to shivering and Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) during long fasting periods.

Today, PetSelf decodes Chihuahua body language and introduces a precise 3-step home care routine for molera protection and metabolic health.

## 1. Chihuahua's Signals: Emotional Shivering and Defensive Growling

Chihuahuas are highly sensitive and express strong alerts due to their tiny size. Key behaviors include 'shivering' and 'growling'. Shivering occurs not only due to cold but when experiencing intense emotions like excitement or fear; their brain signals muscles to contract. Growling while showing teeth is a warning born of fear, asking you to step back. Forcing physical contact during this time can trigger defensive biting. Always give them space.

## 2. Custom Daily Care Routine

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-indigo-500/20 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 shadow-sm">
  <div class="flex items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-indigo-500/10 text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 rounded-full border border-indigo-500/20">ROUTINE 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Morning: Forehead Protection Check and Indoor Warmth Setup</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    Do not apply pressure to their forehead (molera). Chihuahuas are sensitive to cold, so check that indoor temperature is kept warm around 24-26°C.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-indigo-500/20 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 shadow-sm">
  <div class="flex items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-indigo-500/10 text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 rounded-full border border-indigo-500/20">ROUTINE 2</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Afternoon: Short Nosework and Sound Desensitization Session</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    Avoid forced dog socializations. Let them sniff/nosework, and practice 5-minute counter-conditioning (giving treats when doors/bells ring) to prevent warning barks.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-indigo-500/20 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 shadow-sm">
  <div class="flex items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3 py-1 text-xs font-bold bg-indigo-500/10 text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 rounded-full border border-indigo-500/20">ROUTINE 3</span>
    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Evening: Timely Meal Feeding for Hypoglycemia and Paw Care</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    Their small size makes them prone to low blood sugar shocks. Keep to strict evening feeding times, and trim hair between paw pads to prevent slipping indoors.
  </p>
