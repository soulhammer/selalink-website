The giant of Korean Neo-Confucianism, Yi Hwang. Though he achieved deep academic standards, he was frail, suffering from chronic stomach illnesses throughout his life. The secret he practiced every morning to preserve his health and sharp intellect was the Hwalinsimbang exercise. Waking up early at dawn, he sat straight on his bed to perform 24 stretching movements and regulate his breath. Let's reveal how this classic morning routine controls morning hormone spikes and brings calm alertness.

## 1. How Low-Intensity Exercise and Breath Regulation Induce Autonomic Nervous System Calmness

Immediately after waking up, the human body experiences a spike in cortisol levels (Cortisol Awakening Response) to transition to an active state, which can cause temporary pulse elevation and anxiety. Facing aggressive activities or digital media at this point over-activates the brain's anxiety circuits. Conversely, combining gentle stretching and prolonged abdominal exhalations, like Yi Hwang's Hwalinsimbang, stimulates the vagus nerve and activates the parasympathetic nervous system, stabilizing heart rate variability (HRV) and establishing the brain's most rational, calm state of focus readiness.
