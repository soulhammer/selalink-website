Jawaharlal Nehru led the non-violent struggle for Indian independence and served as the first Prime Minister. Behind his elegant white attire lay long, painful years spent in cold prison cells. 🧘

Nehru was arrested 9 times and spent 9 years in solitary confinement. In those small cells, his physical and mental health were pushed to the limit. However, instead of breaking down, he greeted every morning by performing a headstand (Sirsasana) on the cold floor. This habit restored his balance amidst loneliness. It became his brain activation routine to manage complex international diplomacy with logic.

Today, BuildSelf explores the science of cerebral blood flow behind Nehru's headstand habit and introduces a practical calming routine using inverted gravity for modern people.

## 1. Inverted Gravity Effects to Induce Brain Blood Flow and Quiet the HPA Stress Axis

Sitting or standing all day pools blood in the lower body due to gravity and compresses spinal discs. Inverting the body reverses gravity, sending a surge of oxygen and blood to the brain. This blood flow flushes waste and supplies nutrients. Neurologically, this regulates the <strong>HPA (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal) axis</strong>, lowering cortisol and activating the parasympathetic system. It also decompresses the spine, resetting muscle tension.
