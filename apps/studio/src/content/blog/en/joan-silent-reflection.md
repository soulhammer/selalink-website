Joan of Arc, the legendary figure who saved France during the Hundred Years' War and carved out her destiny through faith alone to become a Catholic saint. Where lay the source of strength that enabled a peasant girl who couldn't read to overcome the fear of death and maintain indomitable belief amidst threats of war, cold shoulders from nobles, and trials by the church? She sought quiet spaces, like cathedral corners or behind army tents, to engage in deep silent prayer every morning. This routine of blocking out all surrounding noise to focus solely on her inner call and promise granted her strong mental balance. Today, BuildSelf introduces the neurological relief effects and modern implementation of Joan of Arc's 'silent prayer' routine.

## 1. Amygdala Stabilization & Self-Esteem Recovery via Physical Noise Blocking

When suffering from excessive visual and auditory fatigue, the brain's emotion and alarm center, the <strong>Amygdala</strong>, easily becomes sensitive and promotes anxiety. According to brain science, intentionally blocking noise and experiencing silence for 10 minutes at the start of the day stabilizes the overloaded amygdala and calms the sympathetic nervous system. Furthermore, cognitive alignment—pondering one's values and beliefs in silence—clears cognitive dissonance and stimulates self-esteem neural circuits, helping to restore a firm mental defense line unshaken by external negative stimuli or stress.

## 3. Tune Into Your Inner Value, Not Other's Nagging or Evaluation
