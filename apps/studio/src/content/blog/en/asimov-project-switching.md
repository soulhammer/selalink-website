"I don't stare at a blank sheet of paper. When I get stuck, I don't try to force it. Instead, I move to another typewriter and write a manuscript on an entirely different topic." ⌨️

Isaac Asimov, who wrote over 500 books across vast disciplines throughout his life, becoming a master of science fiction and the premier popular science writer of his era. Behind this phenomenal master of prolific writing pouring out manuscripts like magic every day was his unique and ingenious working habit.

That was precisely the 'autonomous switching of tasks'. Asimov arranged multiple typewriters side-by-side in his study, setting each up with projects on different topics (such as novels, science explanations, essays, etc.). When his flow stalled on one manuscript or his mind grew bored, instead of forcing it under stress, he simply stood up and moved to the adjacent typewriter to write a manuscript in a completely different field.

In today's post, we propose the cognitive science principles hidden within Isaac Asimov's 'typewriter switching' routine and a 3-step practical action plan for modern individuals to overcome cognitive stagnation in a single task and maximize work efficiency.

## 1. Context Switching and the Novelty Effect: Regional Brain Fatigue Recovery

According to research in cognitive psychology and working memory, hanging onto a single task for a long time causes the brain to hyperactivate only specific neural networks, inducing cognitive stagnation. When squeezing the brain forcefully during the resulting Writer's Block, cortisol is secreted, drastically reducing cognitive efficiency.

When you switch context (Context Switching) to a completely different task like Asimov did (e.g., shifting from writing fiction to explaining scientific facts), the cortical areas previously activated with accumulated fatigue enter forced rest, and an entirely different neural network corresponding to the new stimulus awakens. This is called the 'Novelty Effect', allowing the newly stimulated brain to safely re-enter a state of immersion without recognizing prior cognitive fatigue.

## 3. Neuroscientific Advice for Efficient Focus Switching

When stuck on one task, holding on relentlessly to the bitter end is not the only virtue. Neurons in the brain also need shift-work across different regions. Flexibly break through work plateaus through clever context switching between drafting minor emails or writing an entirely different proposal.
