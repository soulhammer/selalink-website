"In his walk he walked much and mused... and his cane had an inkhorn at the head of it." 🦯

Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679) was the titan of English political philosophy whose 1651 masterwork *Leviathan* established the foundations of modern social contract theory. Where did he formulate his intricate philosophical deductions? Remarkably, they were carved out not while sitting behind a desk, but while walking across open fields and country roads.

Hobbes far preferred deep contemplation during long outdoor walks to forced agonizing at his study desk. However, he quickly realized that brilliant metaphors and philosophical arguments emerging during a walk vanished from his memory by the time he returned home. To solve this cognitive challenge, as recorded by his contemporary biographer John Aubrey in *Brief Lives*, Hobbes engineered a unique solution: he mounted an inkhorn directly onto the head of his walking cane and carried sheets of paper in his pocket. Whenever a breakthrough idea struck him, he immediately halted, dipped his pen into the cane's inkhorn, and transcribed the thought on the spot.

In today's post, we unpack the cognitive neuroscience behind Thomas Hobbes's 'cane inkwell' immediate note-taking method and present a 3-step practical framework to capture fleeting ideas before they decay.

## 1. Preventing Working Memory Decay and the Neuroscience of Cognitive Externalization

When the brain enters a relaxed walking state, the <strong>Default Mode Network (DMN)</strong> activates, firing creative associative sparks. However, because these flashes reside inside the short-term buffer of <strong>Working Memory</strong>, they are fleeting and prone to rapid decay within 10 to 15 seconds unless recorded immediately. Hobbes's cane inkwell served as the ultimate tool for <strong>Cognitive Externalization</strong>. Instead of trapping volatile insights in prefrontal working memory, offloading them onto paper via physical motor action prevents cognitive overload and frees central executive bandwidth for deeper conceptual processing.

## 3. Caution: Protocol for Recording Within the 10-Second Memory Decay Window

Procrastinating with 'I will write this down when I get home' severely underestimates working memory decay. Most thoughts generated while walking are overwritten in the hippocampus by new sensory inputs by the time you sit at a desk. To preserve their value, externalize sparks within the 10-second golden window via voice recording or rapid keyphrase logging.
