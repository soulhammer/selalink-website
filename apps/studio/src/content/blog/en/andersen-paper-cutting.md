Hans Christian Andersen, the father of fairy tales who brought dreams and tears to children and adults worldwide. Suffering from a severe appearance complex, depression, and abandonment anxiety throughout his life, what was the secret behind maintaining his beautiful fairy tale imagination and finding comfort? When conceptualizing inspirations, Andersen always took out paper and scissors from his pocket. He folded and paper-cut while speaking and conceiving fairy tales. For him, paper-cutting was not children's craft, but a tool for emotional recovery that stimulated fingertip senses to draw out the imaginary world. Today, BuildSelf introduces the psychiatric healing benefits and modern manual routine of Andersen's 'paper-cutting'.

## 1. Default Mode Network (DMN) Stabilization via Fine Manual Work

The <strong>'Default Mode Network (DMN)'</strong>, which operates when the brain rests without a specific task, causes modern humans to amplify unnecessary overthinking, such as past regrets and future anxiety. According to occupational therapy and neuroscience, <strong>'fine manual work'</strong> like paper-cutting or drawing, which delicately controls fingertips, warms up the motor cortex while instantly deactivating the overloaded DMN. Plucking and symmetrically cutting paper in front of you blocks overthinking and provides deep cognitive rest to the brain.

## 3. Focus on the Trajectory of Fingertips & Cutting Sound, Not Perfect Design Plan

  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      To enjoy the relaxation effects of the scissors routine, you must let go of the obsession to make great results. If you start planning designs in your head like 'I should complete a pretty pattern', the planning and control areas of the frontal lobe overheat, making it hard to get the DMN deactivation effect. Just as Andersen closed his eyes and cut as his hands moved, letting your attention completely follow the crispy friction sound of scissors cutting paper and analog vibrations of fingertips is the first step to relaxation.
    </p>
