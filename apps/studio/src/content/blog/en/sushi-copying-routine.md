"Knowledge is not merely read; it truly becomes yours when categorized and organized by your own hand." 📜

Su Dongpo (Su Shi), a monumental literary figure and statesman of the Northern Song Dynasty, left genius literary achievements across poetry, prose, calligraphy, and painting. The core intellectual bedrock supporting his vast erudition was none other than his 'Choso copying method.'

When tackling massive amounts of history and scholarship, including the Seven Classics and official histories, Su Dongpo never read passively with his eyes alone. He categorized knowledge into thematic bins—such as 'Politics,' 'Governance,' 'Goods,' and 'Geography'—opened his notebooks, and transcribed characters by hand across three cycles. Through this process, he constructed a precise 'cognitive index' (an intellectual search engine) in his mind, allowing him to retrieve knowledge from tens of thousands of volumes effortlessly.

In today's post, we introduce the cognitive science principles of Chunking, Elaborative Rehearsal, and Long-Term Memory Consolidation embedded in Su Dongpo's routine of thematic copying and 3-pass reviews.

## 1. Powerful Cognitive Indexing Effects Created by Knowledge Chunking and Elaborative Rehearsal

When vast information is received in a disconnected state, the brain fails to process it and loses it. Categorizing knowledge into themes and writing it down by hand triggers neuroscientific mechanisms of <strong>'Chunking'</strong> and <strong>'Elaborative Rehearsal'</strong>.

By grouping information into meaningful thematic units, the prefrontal cortex strongly connects new information to existing long-term memory structures. Repeatedly transcribing this by hand stimulates neural plasticity between the hippocampus and the cerebral cortex, perfecting a cognitive retrieval structure (Cognitive Indexing) that pulls up knowledge instantly when needed.

## 3. Avoid Mindless Transcription and Focus on Categorization and Understanding

The core of Su Dongpo's Choso method is not blindly copying characters, but the rigorous classification work of deciding 'into which category this knowledge belongs.' Mechanical copying only induces finger fatigue without cognitive elaboration; focus deeply on understanding content structure and categorization.
