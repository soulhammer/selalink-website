As the 44th President of the United States, Barack Obama had to make heavy, world-altering decisions every day. During his presidency, to focus his prefrontal judgment energy entirely on critical national affairs amid countless daily choices, he utilized a very clever personal secret. 👔

It was the 'complete elimination of minor choices.' President Obama always wore only grey or blue suits and simplified his breakfast menu. In an interview, he noted, 'You'll see I wear only grey or blue suits. I'm trying to pare down decisions. I don't want to make decisions about what I'm eating or wearing. Because I have too many other decisions to make.'

Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive science principles behind President Obama's decision-simplification routine and proposes a 3-step practical action plan for modern people to efficiently manage their daily willpower battery.

## 1. Decision Fatigue and Ego Depletion: The Energy Limit of the Frontal Lobe

According to empirical research in psychology and neuroscience, decision-making ability is not an infinite resource but functions like a limited battery. Even micro-choices like deciding which socks to wear or what to eat in the morning consume the cognitive resources (willpower) of the frontal lobe. This progressive draining of resources is called 'Ego Depletion' or 'Decision Fatigue.'

When decision fatigue peaks, rational cognitive control is compromised, leading to impulsive choices or postponing critical decisions. President Obama's automation of mechanical morning routines was a cognitive engineering tactic designed to preserve neuron energy 100% so he could make the best strategic policy choices with the clearest brain state.
