Plato, the intellectual giant who laid the foundation of Western philosophy and established 'Academeia.' Where did his tireless thinking and mental stamina come from? 🤼

While many imagine Plato as a static thinker, the name 'Plato' was actually a nickname meaning 'broad shoulders' from his wrestling days. He was an elite wrestler and started every morning with intense physical training. Plato believed that only when the body experiences extreme physical resistance can the brain prepare to contemplate abstract realms.

Today, BuildSelf covers the physiological mechanisms behind Plato's 'high-intensity warm-up' routine and how to apply a 15-minute home workout version.

## 1. Maximized Cerebral Blood Flow and BDNF Release from Resistance Training

According to modern exercise physiology and cognitive science, high-intensity resistance training like squats or push-ups immediately increases cerebral blood flow and oxygen saturation. This physical arousal triggers a surge in Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF), a key protein for neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity. It also floods the brain with dopamine and norepinephrine, establishing an optimal neural environment for high-level abstract logic and memory consolidation.

## 3. Injury Prevention and Learning Entry Timing Tips
<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-indigo-500 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">💡</span>
  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Starting high-intensity exercise right after waking up without warming up can strain joints and ligaments. Perform light stretching and marching in place first. Also, if you delay study for more than 15 minutes after exercise, the peak brain blood flow benefits will decline. Transition to complex cognitive tasks within 5 minutes after catching your breath.
    </p>
