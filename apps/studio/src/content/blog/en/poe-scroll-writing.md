Edgar Allan Poe (1809–1849), hailed as the pioneer of macabre gothic fiction such as 'The Raven' and 'The Black Cat', as well as the father of the modern detective story. How were his brilliant manuscripts—weaving human psychological anxiety with intricate logical mysteries—crafted in his study? 📜

When writing in his study, Poe rarely used standard rectangular sheets of paper. Instead, he wrote on long, narrow strips of paper ribbon in an extraordinarily fine, neat, almost print-like handwriting. Once a ribbon was filled, he applied paste or wax to the end to attach the next strip. As his work progressed, his manuscript turned into a long 'scroll' rolled up tightly. Poe confessed that focusing on this strangely narrow and extended visual space allowed all sensory resources of his brain to converge into a single narrative flow with immense depth.

In today's post, we examine the visual feedback principles hidden within Poe's 'scroll writing' routine and explore modern focus mode techniques to elevate writing productivity.

## 1. Cognitive Tunneling and Suppression of the Self-Monitoring Circuit

Controlling the field of view into an extremely narrow band reduces unnecessary saccadic eye movements, dramatically preventing the frontal lobe from wasting visual attention. Neuroscience calls this state <strong>'Cognitive Tunneling'</strong>, which restricts visual input width to induce deep immersion.

At the same time, the physical scroll structure continuing downward inhibits a destructive habit common among writers: re-reading and editing freshly written sentences. When the brain's <strong>'Self-monitoring'</strong> circuit—which seeks to immediately evaluate text—is suppressed, the brain maintains an unhindered stream of free association, enabling highly original and fluent writing.

## 3. Isolation from Self-Editing

Fixating on sentence-by-sentence editing while drafting causes constant friction between the right brain's creative circuits and the left brain's critical circuits, causing cognitive fatigue. Just as Poe wrote on a continuous scroll, turn off critical editing circuits during drafting to dedicate brain energy solely to output, reserving revision for later.
