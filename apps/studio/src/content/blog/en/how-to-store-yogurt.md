Have you ever opened a tub of yogurt only to find a layer of liquid on top or mold forming before you could finish it? 🥣

Yogurt is a fermented dairy product containing live probiotics, making it highly sensitive to temperature fluctuations and external contamination. Using a saliva-contaminated spoon or introducing moisture allows unwanted microorganisms to multiply rapidly, while unstable temperatures accelerate whey separation (syneresis) and cause excessive sourness.

Today, FreshSnap shares a scientifically proven 3-step yogurt storage guide recommended by food safety experts to maximize freshness and probiotic vitality. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Do not store yogurt on the refrigerator door shelf ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      The refrigerator door experiences severe temperature fluctuations and light exposure whenever opened. Unstable temperatures disrupt probiotic stability and accelerate whey separation and spoilage. Store yogurt on a cold, deep inner shelf (0–4°C) where temperatures remain constant.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Never use a wet or saliva-contaminated spoon 🥄</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Scooping yogurt with a spoon that has touched your mouth or carries water introduces digestive enzymes and bacteria. This allows mold and unwanted microbes to thrive rapidly. Always use a clean, completely dry dedicated spoon.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
