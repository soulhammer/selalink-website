"What am I thinking? I'm just doing it." ⛸️

Yuna Kim (1990–), the 'Figure Queen' who forged an unprecedented All-Podium legacy in figure skating history, including 2010 Vancouver Olympic Gold, 2014 Sochi Olympic Silver, and two World Championships. Amidst the grueling physical pain and repetitive boredom of daily ice training, how did she execute flawless performances without a moment of emotional hesitation?

Yuna Kim possessed a 'Just-Do-It Routine Execution' mindset that carried out scheduled actions immediately without succumbing to emotional swings. When asked by a reporter what she was thinking while stretching before training, her matter-of-fact response—'What am I thinking? I'm just doing it'—became a legendary summary of behavioral activation science. Before emotions like 'I don't feel like it' or 'This is boring' could hijack her brain, she simply initiated her stretch and stepped onto the ice. By acting first rather than waiting for motivation, she bypassed mental fatigue and remained at the top of the world.

In today's post, we examine the neuroscientific mechanisms behind Yuna Kim's 'Just-Do-It' routine and present a 3-step practical guide to escaping the motivation trap and initiating immediate action.

## 1. Breaking the Motivation Trap: 'Behavioral Activation' and Cognitive Friction Elimination

Many believe motivation must precede action, but cognitive science identifies this as the 'motivation fallacy'. Waiting for the right emotional state allows the <strong>Prefrontal Cortex</strong> to generate excuses and accumulate decision fatigue. Executing physical movements immediately without emotional interference triggers <strong>'Behavioral Activation'</strong>. Commencing action stimulates the brain's <strong>Striatum</strong> circuits, transitioning you into a focused state regardless of initial mood while reducing <strong>Cognitive Friction</strong> to zero.

## 3. Rule: Refrain from Evaluating Emotional States or Searching for Meaning Before Execution

The cardinal pitfall in routine execution is evaluating your internal state—asking 'Do I feel good today?' or 'How do I feel about doing this work right now?'. Like Yuna Kim, flip the emotional off-switch and focus exclusively on reflexively executing the very first physical movement when the set time arrives.
