Have you ever left an opened bottle of Sriracha sauce in a room-temperature pantry only to watch its vibrant red color darken into a brownish hue? 🌶️

Sriracha sauce is made with chili peppers, vinegar, and garlic, giving it a strongly acidic environment with a pH between 3.5 and 4.0. While this high acidity prevents the growth of foodborne pathogens and spoilage bacteria, exposure to oxygen causes ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) and capsaicin compounds to oxidize. This chemical reaction leads to dark browning and flavor degradation over time.

In this guide, we share a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on safety standards from the FDA and MFDS, alongside Arrhenius chemical reaction dynamics, to keep your Sriracha sauce delightfully spicy and bright red for as long as possible. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Keep the nozzle tip clean and free of sauce residue 🧼</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Sauce buildup around the nozzle continuously exposed to air will harden and undergo rapid localized oxidation. Always wipe the tip clean with a dry cloth after use to prevent contamination and maintain hygiene.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
