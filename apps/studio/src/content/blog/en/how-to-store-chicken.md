Have you ever washed raw chicken under the tap, only to worry about spreading foodborne bacteria around your kitchen sink? 🍗

Raw poultry has a high moisture content and delicate muscle fibers, making it an ideal breeding ground for harmful foodborne pathogens like Campylobacter jejuni and Salmonella. Washing raw chicken under running water causes micro-droplets containing bacteria to splash onto nearby countertops, utensils, and food.

Today, FreshSnap shares a scientifically proven 3-step poultry storage guide based on hygiene guidelines from the CDC, USDA, and MFDS to prevent cross-contamination and keep your chicken safe, tender, and fresh. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Always use separate cutting boards and knives for raw poultry 🔪</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Knives and cutting boards used for raw chicken harbor invisible pathogenic bacteria. Sanitize them immediately with hot water (above 75°C / 167°F) or dish soap after use, and never use the same utensils to slice raw vegetables or salads.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block mb-1">Consume within 1–2 days (48 hours) or freeze immediately ❄️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Chicken has higher water activity and softer muscle tissue than pork or beef, allowing bacteria to multiply much faster. Limit refrigerator storage (0–4°C) to a maximum of 48 hours, and freeze (-18°C or below) any portion you will not eat right away.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
