"It is a rediscovery of the world of which I have the happiness to be a part. It fills me with awareness of the wonder of life, with a feeling of the incredible marvel of being a human being." 🎹

Pablo Casals (1876–1973) was one of the grandest musical figures of the 20th century—the legendary Spanish cellist who unearthed J.S. Bach's forgotten 'Cello Suites' from a second-hand music shop to bestow a immortal masterpiece upon humanity. Honored with the UN Peace Medal for advocating human rights and peace through music, he maintained a sacred morning ritual without missing a single day for over 80 years until his death at age 96.

Every morning after a brief walk, Casals sat at his piano to carefully play two preludes and fugues from Bach's 'The Well-Tempered Clavier'. He viewed this not as mechanical warm-up or technical practice, but as a holy blessing upon his home and an awakening of his alignment with the universe. Neuroscientifically, warming up the brain with Bach's mathematically structured classical compositions serves as a powerful cognitive anchor: it dampens morning cortisol spikes, balances the autonomic nervous system, and aligns brainwaves into a serene, focused alpha wave state.

In today's post, we analyze the brain science behind Pablo Casals's 'Morning Bach Prelude' routine—exploring Musical Entrainment—and present a 3-step morning protocol for modern individuals to overcome morning anxiety and establish emotional stability for peak focus.

## 1. Alpha Wave Induction and Autonomic Nervous System Balance via Musical Entrainment

Upon waking, human brains experience a morning cortisol spike—a natural biological surge aimed at breaking sleep inertia that often induces mild anxiety and sympathetic nervous system arousal. Engaging deeply with the structured, mathematical harmonies of Bach triggers 'Musical Entrainment', wherein internal neural oscillations align with the acoustic rhythm and frequency. This strongly stimulates 8–12 Hz alpha waves, inducing a state of relaxed alertness and calm focus. Concurrently, parasympathetic nervous system activity is boosted, stabilizing heart rate and balancing the autonomic nervous system to anchor the brain in emotional composure and readiness.

## 3. Caution & Tips: Excluding Overly Complex or Aggressive Beats & Choosing Harmonious Classics

When designing a morning music routine, avoid overly complex, dissonant, or fast-tempo aggressive genres. Loud rock, pop, or playing highly technical virtuosic pieces can overstimulate the sympathetic nervous system, increasing morning anxiety and cognitive fatigue instead of relaxing the brain. To achieve emotional buffering, select structural, repetitive, and steady-tempo classical or ambient acoustic music, such as Bach's preludes.
