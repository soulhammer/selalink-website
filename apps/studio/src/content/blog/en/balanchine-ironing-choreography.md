George Balanchine, the legendary choreographer who revolutionized 20th-century ballet. Faced with the pressure of producing geometric and innovative movements, he too encountered mental blocks. However, Balanchine didn't force himself to sit and think. Instead, he stood up, set up an ironing board, and ironed shirts or mopped floors. We analyze how this mundane household chore routine rewires the brain's creative pathways to deliver artistic breakthroughs.

## 1. Deactivating Prefrontal Censorship and Igniting DMN Association Networks

When forcing ourselves to solve a problem, the prefrontal cortex initiates tight top-down control, filtering out unconventional or illogical fragments. This mental censorship stifles creativity. In contrast, performing monotonous motor tasks like ironing relaxes this cognitive grip. With analytical filters down, the Default Mode Network (DMN) steps in, facilitating 'combinatorial play' among distant memory nodes. Through the repeating rhythm of the iron, choreographic lines connect effortlessly.
