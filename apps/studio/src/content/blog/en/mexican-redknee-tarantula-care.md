The Mexican Redknee Tarantula (Brachypelma hamorii) is a striking New World terrestrial tarantula known for its jet-black body and vibrant orange-red bands across its knee joints (patellae). Renowned in the arachnid-keeping community for its mild venom and docile nature, it is widely considered the ultimate beginner species. However, when threatened, it kicks barbed urticating hairs from its abdomen that can cause severe irritation to human skin, eyes, and respiratory tracts. Furthermore, improper moisture levels during a molt can lead to fatal dysecdysis. Today on PetSelf, we explain Mexican Redknee defense signals, optimal enclosure climate management, and a 3-step care routine for molting and daily safety.
