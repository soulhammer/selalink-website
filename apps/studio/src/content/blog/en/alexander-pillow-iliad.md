"There is nothing impossible to him who will try." ⚔️

Alexander the Great, the conquering monarch who ascended the throne at the young age of twenty and built an unprecedented empire stretching from Macedonia to the borders of India in human history. Though he fought numerous battles with brilliant strategy and valor, he had to face equally harsh psychological tension and daunting challenges.

His greatest secret to managing immense anxiety and attaining Achilles-like courage was hidden right beneath his pillow. Alexander always cherished the book of Homer's epic poem, the 'Iliad', which he received as a personally annotated edition from Aristotle, the pinnacle of Greek philosophy. Even when sleeping in a tent on the battlefield, he kept this Iliad under his pillow alongside a dagger and read it thoroughly every night. By reading the classical epic intently, the king drove away fear from his mind and completed his preparations every night to awaken as tomorrow's hero.

In today's post, we introduce the scientific grounds of Alexander's 'Iliad Under the Pillow' routine and explore how modern individuals who easily lose sight of their goals can utilize their subconscious to establish willpower.

## 1. Value-Oriented Reading That Induces Subconscious Alignment

When the brain begins to fall asleep, brainwaves transition to alpha and theta waves, and information introduced during this time reaches the hippocampus (the long-term memory storehouse) and deep within the cerebral cortex (which forms the subconscious) much more easily than usual.

The Iliad that Alexander the Great read before sleeping was not merely to pass the time. By repeatedly exposing his brain to hero values, valor, and adventurous spirit right before entering dormant mode, he synchronized his self-identity and goal-oriented neural networks through Value Alignment. In modern neuroscience as well, focusing on positive and proactive narratives right before sleep has been proven to be a useful tool for activating subconscious problem-solving abilities and restoring self-esteem.

## 3. The Text You Read Determines Your Subconscious

Consuming content late at night that stimulates fear, jealousy, or criticism—such as social media or news articles—aligns your subconscious with negative stimuli, disrupting deep sleep and causing you to wake up exhausted in the morning. Intentionally place noble classics or books that elevate your value by your bedside.
