"All human problems stem from interpersonal relationships. But the solution also lies in Social Interest that connects us with others." 🤝

Alfred Adler, the founder of Individual Psychology who laid the groundwork for modern psychotherapy by emphasizing self-determination and overcoming inferiority feelings. As a therapist healing the deep psychological wounds of countless patients, how did he clear his own ego each morning before consultations and maintain optimal mental balance? 📖

Every morning, before welcoming patients and embarking on research, Adler spent 10 quiet minutes opening his personal notebook. He wrote down in concrete sentences: "How will I contribute to others and the community today?". Rather than fixating on his own professional authority or personal anxiety, he pre-set a perspective focused on 'Social Interest' (Gemeinschaftsgefühl). This 10-minute reflective note served as an unshakeable anchor for his psychological stability.

In today's post, we explore the cognitive neuroscientific principles of decentering and prosocial orientation embedded in Alfred Adler's morning Social Interest journaling routine.

## 1. Neuroscience of Decentering and Prosocial Perspective Setting

When excessively ruminating on personal anxiety, inferiority, or achievement, the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) and Default Mode Network (DMN) become hyperactive, driving self-focused stress.

Writing about 'contributing to others and community' in the morning prompts decentering—shifting awareness from 'I' to 'We'. This activates the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), instantly mitigating self-referential anxiety and cortisol release while engaging social cognition networks (Theory of Mind) to cultivate emotional resilience and psychological freedom.
