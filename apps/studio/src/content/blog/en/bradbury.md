"The words were buckets lowered into the deep well of my subconscious, pulling up memories and narratives." 📝

Ray Bradbury (1920–2012) was a titan of modern science fiction and a literary master who envisioned the limits of technological civilization with lyrical poetic sensitivity. Having authored timeless classics such as 'Fahrenheit 451' and 'The Martian Chronicles', his blazing creative fountainhead stemmed from an extraordinary daily morning ritual.

Before his prefrontal cortex's rational censors could awaken each morning, Bradbury practiced a '10-Word Association Routine' designed to detonate subconscious storytelling material. The moment he woke up, he rushed to his desk and furiously typed out 10 random, unrelated words (e.g., sand, skeleton, bell, engine, cloud) on blank paper. Gazing at them briefly, he forcibly combined these disparate concepts to improvise a short micro-scenario or story outline. The vast majority of his hundreds of published short stories and iconic plot hooks originated directly from this daily exercise in forced association.

In today's post, we analyze the brain science underlying Ray Bradbury's '10-Word Random Association Method' and present a 3-step practical action plan for modern individuals to overcome creative blocks and ignite divergent thinking.

## 1. Activation of Divergent Thinking Circuits and Forced Random Association

Forcibly bridging unrelated concepts strongly stimulates the prefrontal cortex's 'Divergent Thinking' networks. Right after waking, during a cognitive window when top-down prefrontal censorship is not yet fully active, organic synaptic recombination (Combinatorial Play) among subconscious memories occurs far more readily. The unfamiliar cognitive shock delivered by random words activates the temporal lobe and association cortices, shattering fixed mental models and yielding innovative business ideas and creative problem-solving.

## 3. Caution & Tips: Abandoning Perfectionism & Immersing in the Associative Flow

When performing this routine, do not try to craft polished, refined sentences or high-realism prose right away. The moment you worry about grammatical perfection or logical elegance, prefrontal censorship intervenes and cuts off subconscious associative chains. The core is immersing yourself entirely in the 'associative process itself'—speedily and forcefully assembling disparate concepts inside your head.
