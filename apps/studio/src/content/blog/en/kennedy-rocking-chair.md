"Amid intense physical pain and national crisis, I need a space that stabilizes my body and nervous system. The gentle rhythm of the rocking chair soothes my back pain and restores composure in the Oval Office." 🪑

John F. Kennedy (1917–1963) was the 35th President of the United States who guided the nation through iconic Cold War crises, including the tense 13-day Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962. While presenting a youthful, energetic image to the public, Kennedy privately suffered from debilitating, chronic lower back pain stemming from severe spinal injuries sustained during WWII when his patrol boat, PT-109, was rammed by a Japanese destroyer in 1943.

To manage his severe lumbar condition without heavy sedation, Kennedy turned to Dr. Janet Travell, a pioneering physician in myofascial pain management whom he appointed as the official White House Physician. Dr. Travell prescribed a woven-seat Oak Carolina Rocking Chair (crafted by the Poteat Chair Company) for the Oval Office. Kennedy sat in his favorite rocker while examining sensitive intelligence reports, conducting marathon cabinet meetings, and hosting foreign dignitaries. Gently rocking back and forth relieved static gravitational load on his intervertebral discs and stimulated his inner ear's vestibular system, down-regulating sympathetic stressand activating the parasympathetic nervous system. This physical motion served as a neurobiological anchor, quelling amygdala reactivity and protecting his prefrontal executive reasoning during nuclear-age decision-making.

In today's post, we examine the cognitive neuroscience and orthopedics behind John F. Kennedy's 'Oval Office rocking chair' routine and present a modern 3-step action guide to relieve spinal stress and induce deep nervous system relaxation.

## 1. Vestibular System Stimulation and Parasympathetic Nervous System Activation

The rhythmic low-frequency back-and-forth motion of a rocking chair gently stimulates sensory otoliths within the inner ear's <strong>Vestibular System</strong>. Neural projections from the vestibular nuclei travel through the brainstem to robustly engage the <strong>Parasympathetic Nervous System</strong>, lowering heart rate, slowing respiratory frequency, and suppressing <strong>cortisol</strong> secretion. Orthopedically, micro-movements induced by rocking promote fluid circulation across intervertebral discs, mitigating spinal static load. This reduction in physical distress quells hyper-reactivity in the <strong>amygdala</strong>, enabling the <strong>Prefrontal Cortex</strong> to maintain clear executive control during complex decision-making.

## 3. Caution: Preventing Static Load and Inducing Micro-Movements in the Spine

Chronic neck and lower back discomfort in modern knowledge workers stems primarily from 'Static Load'—remaining frozen in a fixed posture for hours. Physical rigidity directly induces emotional distress and cognitive stiffness. Following Kennedy's example, intentionally creating slow, fluid micro-movements while seated is the key to preserving both spinal health and executive brain performance.
