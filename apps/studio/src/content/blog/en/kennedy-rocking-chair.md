President John F. Kennedy led America during the most tense moments of human history, from the Cuban Missile Crisis to the Space Race. Though he appeared vibrant and charismatic, he suffered from severe chronic back pain due to a wartime spinal injury. 🪑

The solution came from his orthopedist, Dr. Janet Travell, who prescribed placing a specially designed wooden rocking chair in the Oval Office. Kennedy rocked gently while reading diplomatic documents or holding marathon meetings. This simple habit relieved spinal tension and served as a sanctuary for maintaining cold logic and composure during high-stakes decision-making.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neuroscientific calming mechanism of JFK's rocking chair routine and how modern people can apply it to spinal health and mental relaxation.

## 1. The Neurobiology of Rocking to Activate the Parasympathetic Nervous System

The rhythmic motion of rocking gently back and forth stimulates the vestibular system in the inner ear. This stimulation instantly activates the <strong>parasympathetic nervous system</strong>, stabilizing heart rate and respiration while suppressing cortisol. Physically, the gentle motion continuously contracts and relaxes back muscles, facilitating blood flow to spinal discs and redistributing pressure. When physical discomfort decreases, amygdala hyperactivity is subdued, restoring prefrontal cortex functions.
