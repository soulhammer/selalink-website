"Physical labor is a duty and a salvation for those engaged in mental work. A mind that does not move the body is bound to rust." 🌾

Leo Tolstoy, regarded as an absolute pillar of Russian realist literature through masterpieces such as War and Peace, Anna Karenina, and Resurrection. Although born a count into an aristocratic family, he strongly criticized a life of privilege and pursued a modest life of physical labor.

The source that allowed him to sustain relentless creative energy and philosophical inquiry throughout his life was none other than the physical labor and gymnastics he performed every morning. Before sitting at his desk each morning, Tolstoy plowed fields, chopped wood, or carried water buckets like a peasant. He also installed gymnastics equipment in his yard and performed pull-ups and strength training daily. Tolstoy viewed physical labor as an essential engine that awakened the brain and dispelled distractions; on days when he did not fully activate his body, he refused to hold a pen. This physical awakening routine maintained his vitality throughout his life, allowing him to enjoy horseback riding even past the age of 80.

In this post, we examine the neuroscientific truth hidden behind Count Tolstoy's physical training habits and explain principles for modern professionals to design smart morning exercise routines that maximize cognitive efficiency.

## Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) and Cognitive Effects of Increased Heart Rate

According to neuroscientific research, brief morning exercises that engage large muscle groups to elevate heart rate dilate cerebral blood vessels and enhance blood flow across the cerebral cortex. This increases oxygen delivery, immediately boosting prefrontal cognitive processing speed and memory capabilities.

In particular, full-body strength training and gymnastics maximize the secretion of Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF), which promotes neuron survival and growth while strengthening synaptic networks. Additionally, the synergistic release of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin during exercise tunes a sluggish morning brain into an optimized state of deep focus. Tolstoy's vigorous physical labor habit served as an ideal brain warm-up, opening each day with his cognitive faculties fully primed.

## A Neuroscientific Tip for a Successful Routine

Move your body to ignite a powerful warm-up spark in your brain.

Sitting forcibly at a desk staring at a monitor while half-asleep waste valuable time. Just as Tolstoy vigorously trained his body before writing to awaken his brain completely, expend energy for just 15 minutes until sweat forms. Morning focus achieved in an awakened state with activated brain cells yields productivity several times higher than usual.
