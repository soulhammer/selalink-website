Have you ever put leftover chocolate in the fridge, only to find a white powdery coating on its surface or that it tasted like fridge odors? 🍫

Cocoa butter, the key component of chocolate, is highly sensitive to temperature and humidity changes and acts like a sponge, absorbing strong volatile odors. Storing chocolate unprotected in the fridge causes 'Sugar Bloom' (crystallized sugar from dissolved moisture) or 'Fat Bloom' (recrystallized fats), destroying its smooth texture and rich aroma.

In this guide, introduces a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide recommended by USDA and chocolate experts to preserve rich flavor and velvety texture. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Do not store unsealed chocolate in the refrigerator ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      High refrigerator humidity causes condensation on chocolate, dissolving surface sugars that dry into powdery 'Sugar Bloom.' Additionally, cocoa butter readily absorbs ambient odors from garlic, onions, or kimchi.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">

  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Avoid direct sunlight and heat above 20°C (68°F) ☀️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Temperatures exceeding 20°C cause cocoa butter to melt and rise to the surface, creating a greyish-white film known as 'Fat Bloom' that turns the texture crumbly and degrades flavor.
    </p>
