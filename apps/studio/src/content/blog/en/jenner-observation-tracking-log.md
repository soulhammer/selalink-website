The country doctor who eradicated smallpox—one of humanity's deadliest diseases—and invented the world's first vaccine, Edward Jenner saved hundreds of millions of lives. How did a small-town physician achieve one of the greatest medical breakthroughs in human history? 🩺

Jenner did not ignore local folklore stating that milkmaids who contracted cowpox were immune to smallpox. For years, he visited rural farms daily, systematically tracking patient symptoms and immune responses in a dedicated Observation Log. His patient, long-term data collection built the unbreakable evidence base that led to the smallpox vaccine.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neuroscience of Dr. Edward Jenner's field observation log routine, illustrating how long-term data tracking fuels cognitive grit and breakthrough discoveries.

## 1. Prefrontal Long-Term Planning & Sustained Dopamine via Field Log Accumulation

Chasing quick fixes causes easy surrender when early tests fail.

Consistently logging field facts in a <strong>'Long-Term Field Log'</strong> shifts dopamine rewards to the act of recording itself, powering relentless grit.
