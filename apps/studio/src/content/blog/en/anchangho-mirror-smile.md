"We do not smile because we are happy; we are happy because we smile. Practice a smiling face in front of the mirror every morning and sweep your surroundings clean." 🧹

Dosan Ahn Chang-ho, an independence activist and great educational leader of Korea. He believed that independence could be achieved not only through armed struggle with weapons, but also by cultivating the morality, intellectual capability, and daily dignity of every individual in our nation. He always emphasized to his members to treat each other with a gentle, smiling face, and to clean every space where they stayed, correcting the disorder of the mind. Ahn Chang-ho maintained a lifelong posture of leading by example, practicing smiling in front of the mirror himself, and sweeping and arranging his room clean upon waking up in the morning.

Many modern people start their day overwhelmed by complex thoughts and morning lethargy. However, artificially smiling in front of the mirror and visually organizing one's surrounding environment are scientifically proven behavioral therapies that provide a powerful refresh stimulus to the brain. The brain reverse-engineers emotions through the tension of facial expression muscles, and a clean environment saves the cognitive energy consumed by the brain for information processing.

In today's post, we analyze the principles of the facial feedback hypothesis and spatial psychology embedded in Dosan Ahn Chang-ho's lifelong mirror smile and cleanliness routine, and present a 3-step practical action plan for modern individuals to recover cognitive arousal and life energy every morning.

## 1. Cognitive Therapeutic Effects of the Facial Feedback Hypothesis and Physical Space Organization

According to the Facial Feedback Hypothesis in physiology, the brain does not simply move facial muscles based on emotion; conversely, it detects the contraction state of facial muscles to determine current emotional state. Just forcing a smiling expression in the morning makes the brain perceive it as a stimulus of joy, promoting the secretion of positive neurotransmitters such as serotonin and dopamine, and lowering the level of the stress hormone cortisol.

Furthermore, cleaning and organizing surrounding space significantly blocks unnecessary visual noise received by the brain's visual cortex. The brain interprets visual clutter as a threat or an incomplete task, experiencing stress, but when space is organized, the brain's operating energy is restored, setting it into a state where it can immediately focus on main tasks.
