"No book is so bad that some good cannot be got out of it, and reading without taking notes is meaningless." ✍️

Pliny the Elder, a Roman official and polymath, considered any time spent without studying a loss. Even during meals, baths, or travel, he kept a secretary nearby to read books aloud and extracted key ideas or facts into immediate notes. This lifelong habit of excerpting and note-taking was the force behind the creation of ancient history's largest encyclopedia, 'Naturalis Historia' (Natural History).

His passion for learning without ever stopping his pen has been proven, even from the modern perspectives of learning psychology and cognitive science, to be the most effective approach for solidifying knowledge into memory.

## 1. Why Excerpt Note-Taking Is Cognitively Superior to Mere Reading

A passive receptive stance of merely reading text with the eyes causes the brain to easily spill information out of short-term memory storage. In contrast, when summarizing the core of text and taking physical handwriting notes, the fine movements of fingers stimulate the reticular activating system (RAS) in the brainstem, powerfully activating the entire brain. Because the brain must filter and reconstruct information during this process, powerful retrieval cues are embedded, smoothly settling into long-term memory.

## 3. Guide for Smart Excerpt Note-Taking

Mindlessly copying entire paragraphs from a book (transcription) should be avoided. This is close to simple labor that bypasses the brain, causing only cognitive fatigue. Selectively extract and handwrite only inspiring sentences or key keywords. Additionally, build a habit of always writing the book source and page number in parentheses so you can trace excerpted knowledge later.
