Virginia Woolf, the novelist recognized as a pioneer of 20th-century modernist literature through landmark works like 'Mrs Dalloway' and 'To the Lighthouse'. How was she able to realize the secret inner worlds and complex psychology of her characters with such persistent and refined sentences? ✍️

Behind her extraordinary focus lay a unique working habit. Every morning, Woolf stood holding a pen before a slanted standing desk well over a meter tall.

This intense writing routine was inspired by her older sister and brilliant painter, Vanessa Bell. Captivated by watching Vanessa paint standing before a canvas using her whole body's motion to depict her work multidimensionally, Woolf felt she too had to write standing to perceive and craft sentences and entire story structures with three-dimensional balance. Her insistence on writing standing every morning transformed novel writing into a dynamic art form pouring forth her whole body's energy.

In today's post, we share the scientific benefits behind Virginia Woolf's standing writing routine and concentration-boosting action methods for modern people.

## 1. Whole-Body Proprioceptive Stimulation Induced by Standing Desks and Standing Writing

Virginia Woolf's habit of writing as if painting at a 105cm tall standing desk stimulates whole-body proprioceptive senses, neutralizing psychological resistance barriers to writing and running executive brain functions at peak levels.
