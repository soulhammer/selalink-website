"The sky where the season passes is filled with autumn..." ✍️

During the dark era of Japanese colonial rule, poet Yun Dong-ju sang of national lyricism and conscience with a pure, clear poetic mind. During his student days at Yeonhui Technical School, in order to assemble his collection 'Sky, Wind, Stars and Poetry', he meticulously transcribed his poems in clean calligraphy into handwritten notebooks, constantly revising and refining word choices, dates, and locations.

Yun Dong-ju deliberated intensely even when selecting a single phrase or word. After writing a draft, he would revisit his handwritten notebook months or years later to swap words and clearly record the completed date and location (e.g., '1941. 11. 20. Yeonhui Technical School'), neatly organizing his mental trajectory. As testified by Professor Jeong Byeong-uk, transcribing and documenting his handwritten manuscripts was a sacred rite for Yun Dong-ju to protect his conscience and establish cognitive and emotional order amidst the painful turmoil of his era.

In today's post, we introduce the neuroscientific principles of Affect Labeling and Expressive Writing embedded in Yun Dong-ju's routine of refining handwritten manuscripts and precisely recording dates and locations.

## 1. Emotional Regulation Effects in the Brain Brought by Affect Labeling and Expressive Writing

The act of refining thoughts or emotions into precise language and representing them in writing activates the neuroscientific mechanisms of <strong>'Affect Labeling'</strong> and <strong>'Expressive Writing'</strong>.

When vague anxiety and emotional turmoil are articulated into refined text, hyperactivation of the amygdala (Amygdala) is suppressed, and the prefrontal cortex's (Prefrontal Cortex) rational control circuit is activated. Furthermore, precisely recalling and linking the date and location of writing organizes episodic memory (Episodic Memory), maximizing the self-distancing effect (Self-Distancing) that allows one to view past events or emotions cognitively and objectively.

## 3. Avoid the Trap of Perfectionism and Focus on the Joy of Order

The process of refining handwritten notebooks like poet Yun Dong-ju is an exercise in organizing emotions and thoughts. However, obsessing over handwriting aesthetics or sentence perfection can induce cognitive fatigue. Focus on the value of calmly writing down honest feelings and core learnings alongside the date as you wrap up your day.
