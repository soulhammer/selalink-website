When storing leftover tofu, did you pour the original liquid from the plastic pack into your storage container? 🧊

The water inside packaged tofu contains dissolved soy proteins and organic compounds, making it an ideal culture medium for bacteria once opened. Leaving tofu in this stagnant liquid or exposing it to open air accelerates protein degradation, resulting in a sour odor and slimy surface texture.

In this guide, shares a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide based on hygiene standards from the Mayo Clinic and MFDS to keep your leftover tofu firm, moist, and safe for over a week. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Never reuse the original packaging water; change with fresh cold water daily 💧</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Opened packaging water harbors microbial byproducts and dissolved proteins that ruin tofu within hours. Always discard the original water completely and replace the container water with fresh, clean cold water daily to suppress bacterial growth.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Freezing changes tofu texture into a porous, sponge-like structure ❄️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Freezing tofu causes internal water to expand into ice crystals, which leave behind microscopic holes upon thawing, creating a chewy, honeycomb-like sponge texture. While it loses its silky smoothness, frozen tofu excels at absorbing sauces in stews and stir-fries.
    </p>
  </div>
