"I was working on the proof of one of my poems all the morning, and took out a comma. In the afternoon I put it back again." 🌹

Oscar Wilde, a genius playwright and poet representing late 19th-century English literature, dominated his era as a leading icon of Aestheticism, leaving behind masterpieces rich in peerless wit and sensual linguistic artistry, such as *The Picture of Dorian Gray* and *The Importance of Being Earnest*. His exquisite vocabulary, flawless sentence rhythms, and witty dialogue that drew gasps from audiences remain celebrated to this day.

The secret behind his ability to bestow centuries-enduring linguistic aesthetics upon every single line lay in his extreme 'mirror recitation and sentence refinement' routine. Every morning during writing, Wilde stood before a mirror and recited his written sentences aloud multiple times until a state of flawless rhythmic harmony was achieved. He verified with his own eyes and ears the auditory nuances of words, the cadence of sentences, and the resonance created by the placement of words and commas, endlessly repeating recitation and deletion.

His obsession with spending hours to craft a single perfect sentence and dedicating an entire day to deleting and restoring a single comma was no mere affectation. It was a sophisticated linguistic polishing process that combined auditory and visual senses to extract flawless sentence structures. In today's post, we introduce the cognitive science principles of Auditory Feedback and linguistic prosody refinement embedded in Oscar Wilde's recitation routine.

## 1. Auditory-Visual Dual Feedback and Prosodic Working Memory

Reading written text visually while simultaneously looking in a mirror and reciting aloud is a high-level cognitive system that engages the brain's Auditory-Visual Dual Feedback mechanism. Through vocalization, the visual cortex, auditory cortex, and speech-motor cortex are activated concurrently, precisely detecting subtle word dissonances or rhythmic flaws.

Furthermore, refining linguistic tone and prosody while monitoring facial expressions and lip movements in a mirror allows the prefrontal cortex's Working Memory to evaluate the aesthetic perfection of sentences at a heightened level. This is a linguistic optimization mechanism that maximizes the emotional and witty resonance conveyed by delicate adjustments in word placement and punctuation (commas).

## 3. Avoid Impatience for Quantitative Output and Focus on Precise Aesthetic Verification

Wilde's routine prioritized aesthetic precision—leaving behind a single perfect sentence—over rapid quantitative output. Applying this approach in modern life can induce perfectionist paralysis or severe cognitive fatigue if not managed carefully. It is best to separate the initial drafting phase from the refinement (recitation review) phase, focusing on sound and rhythm exclusively during refinement.
