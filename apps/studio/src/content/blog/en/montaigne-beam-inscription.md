"He who does not look ahead remains behind." Michel de Montaigne retired at age 38 to his round tower library. On the wooden beams supporting his ceiling, he carved 57 Greek and Latin maxims, representing key philosophical questions. Sitting in his armchair, he looked up at these phrases to break his own rigid assumptions, which allowed him to write his highly objective, self-reflective Essays.

## 1. How Inscribed Prompts Activate Metacognition

Exposing yourself to visual philosophical cues utilizes 'visual priming,' which subconsciously activates conceptual nodes in the brain. Furthermore, the physical act of looking upwards activates the frontal and prefrontal lobes. This visual alignment prompts the brain to step out of local task-focus and shift to global, high-level evaluation, which is the definition of metacognition. It helps break bias and encourages objective self-monitoring.
