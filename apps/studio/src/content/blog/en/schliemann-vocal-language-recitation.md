"Reading aloud, reciting at the top of my voice, and memorizing short passages—that was the sole secret to mastering 15 languages." 🗣️

Heinrich Schliemann (1822–1890), the German archaeologist who proved Homer's epic poems were historical fact by excavating the ruins of Troy and Mycenae. Working as a grocer's apprentice without higher education, how did he manage to master 15 languages through self-study and achieve world-changing discoveries?

Schliemann's secret lay in his relentlessly intense 'Vocal Language Recitation' routine. Every night, he read foreign texts aloud at the top of his voice and memorized whole paragraphs. His loud recitation caused so much noise that he was evicted multiple times. When learning Russian, he even hired a poor old man to sit as an audience member just so Schliemann could loudly recite Russian novels in front of him. Engaging both vocal production and auditory reception deeply imprinted foreign linguistic structures on his brain, serving as the ultimate key to reading ancient texts and locating Troy.

In today's post, we examine the cognitive neuroscience behind Schliemann's vocal language recitation routine and present a 3-step practical guide to rapidly acquiring languages or complex knowledge.

## 1. The 'Production Effect' and Phonological Loop Stimulating the Brain

Silent reading relies solely on visual input, but vocalizing aloud while listening to your own voice simultaneously fires the visual cortex, motor cortex (controlling vocal articulation), and auditory cortex. Cognitive psychologists call this the <strong>'Production Effect'</strong>, forming robust neural pathways that transform short-term memory into long-term retention. Furthermore, it continuously activates the <strong>'Phonological Loop'</strong> in Working Memory, imprinting unfamiliar grammar patterns and vocabulary into intuitive schemas.

## 3. Rule: Articulate Clearly and Hear Your Own Voice Beyond Mumbling

The most important rule in vocal recitation is avoiding soft, inarticulate mumbling. Like Schliemann, clearly articulate exact pronunciations and intonations, allowing your ears to hear your own voice. If space is limited, use noise-isolating headphones while whispering clearly to maintain the link between vocal articulation and auditory processing.
