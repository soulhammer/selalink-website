Renowned for curling into a tight ball with its head tucked safely inside, the Ball Python (Python regius) is one of the most popular pet snakes worldwide thanks to its gentle nature and diverse color morphs. As ectothermic reptiles, Ball Pythons require a clear thermal gradient between a cool zone (24°C–26°C) and a hot/basking zone (31°C–33°C). Ambient humidity should be maintained at 55%–60% normally, increasing to 70%–80% during shedding cycles to prevent dysecdysis (incomplete shed). Cold temperatures frequently trigger acute Respiratory Infections (RI) and hunger strikes. In this guide, presents a 3-step daily care routine to read your snake's signals and maintain its health.

      <span class="pr-4">My Ball Python hasn't eaten in several months—what should I do about a hunger strike?</span>
      <span class="transition-transform group-open:rotate-180 text-xs text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500 flex-shrink-0">▼</span>

    <p class="mt-3 text-sm md:text-base text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed pl-1 m-0 font-normal">
      Ball Pythons are infamous for seasonal hunger strikes, often refusing prey during winter, breeding seasons, shedding cycles, or minor temperature drops. If the snake isn't losing significant weight (over 10–15%), maintain the hot zone at 31°C–33°C and offer properly warmed frozen-thawed rodents late at night in a quiet environment.
    </p>

    <summary class="flex justify-between items-center font-bold text-slate-900 dark:text-white list-none text-base md:text-lg tracking-tight" role="button" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="pr-4">My snake's shed came off in patchy pieces instead of one whole piece. How do I fix stuck shed?</span>
      <span class="transition-transform group-open:rotate-180 text-xs text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500 flex-shrink-0">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm md:text-base text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed pl-1 m-0 font-normal">
      Patchy shedding (dysecdysis) occurs when enclosure humidity is too low. Never forcefully peel off dry shed as it tears scales. Instead, place the snake in a ventilated tub with damp warm towels or moss for 20–30 minutes at 28°C–30°C to soften the skin, then gently rub off the loosened shed.
    </p>
