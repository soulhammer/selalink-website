Ivan Pavlov, the great physiologist who won Russia's first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine by discovering learning and behavioral patterns through watching dogs salivate. How did his precise observations that captured the subconscious learning mechanism of animals come about? 🔔

Pavlov realized that the dogs, his research subjects, were extremely distracted by minor external stimuli (footsteps outside the lab, angles of light, scents in the air), which created behavioral variables. Accordingly, he built the 'Tower of Silence', an independent research building equipped with absolute soundproofing, light control, and vibration shielding. In this controlled space, Pavlov eliminated even minor stimuli and focused entirely on observation. For him, environmental control was the ultimate tool to reveal the hidden truth behind behavior.

## 1. 'Stimulus Control Theory' to Revive Executive Function

Neuroscientifically, the prefrontal cortex is responsible for executive functions (planning and execution). However, if visual and auditory stimuli are scattered in the surrounding environment, the brain disperses willpower and cognitive resources to filter and inhibit these stimuli. The <strong>'Stimulus Control'</strong> theory of behavioral psychology is a technique that minimizes brain friction by fixing or blocking environmental stimuli in a specific space. When external variables are removed and the desk environment is simplified like Pavlov, the brain is released from the stress of activating inhibitory filters and experiences a state of high concentration by directing its frontoparietal attention network entirely to the core task.

## 3. Securing Flexibility in the Stimulus Control Routine
<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-indigo-500 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">

  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      A side effect of insisting on a stimulus-blocking environment is that you may develop hyper-sensitivity, becoming unable to focus at all in messy or noisy external places. Therefore, apply stimulus control training only during the most complex and difficult main focus hours (e.g., 1-2 hours) of the day, and practice flexibility by performing routine simple tasks in open spaces with some noise or environmental changes.
    </p>
