Jane Goodall is a world-renowned wild chimpanzee researcher and environmental activist, and a biologist who completely reformed the existing map of animal ethology by first discovering the ability of primates to use tools by directly interacting with chimpanzees in the Gombe jungle in Africa for decades. Every day, she climbed through the early morning fog and sat atop the steep cliffs of Gombe, Africa. She completely silenced the noise of her actions so that the chimpanzees would not be wary of her. And I quietly observed the chimpanzee's fine hair-picking movements, the rhythm of its fingertips using tree branch tools, and the blinking of its eyes through a telescope, and recorded the facts along with drawings in a pocket notebook.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of the habit of ‘sitting on a rock next to a group of chimpanzees and recording the micro-habits of wild chimpanzees’ and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of the brain science principles that lead to success

The training of staring silently at an object for a long time with extremely objective attention without intervening to manipulate the external object sharply activates the brain's sensory association cortex and selective sensory connection network. The process of inputting fine trajectories beyond the telescope into the brain and structuring them in real time into analog sentences or pictures strengthens the brain's fine sensory resolution, enhancing advanced insight perception abilities that preemptively capture clues that pass by in daily life or hidden trends in the business market.
