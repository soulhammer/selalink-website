"If you can dream it, you can do it." 🏰

From Mickey Mouse to Disneyland, Walt Disney built an animation empire while gifting dreams and fantasy to children and adults worldwide. His immense creativity and planning prowess were not mere products of inspiration. He possessed a unique thinking system that coolly refined seemingly absurd imaginations into flawless realities consumed by the public.

When refining ideas for a project, he moved between 'Three Rooms' to conduct meetings. The first was the Dreamer's room, spinning all kinds of whimsical imaginations without limits; the second was the Realist's room, realizing those imaginations into actual technology and budgets; and the third was the Critic's room, sharply analyzing loopholes in the plan. Disney produced innovative masterpieces by thoroughly isolating these three roles and cross-validating them.

In today's post, we look into the scientific grounds behind Disney's 'Three Rooms' routine for realizing thoughts and how to apply it in modern planning work.

## 1. Cognitive Effects of Separating Attention and Critique

A common mistake when making new plans is reviewing and criticizing simultaneously while forming ideas with thoughts like 'This is impossible' or 'It costs too much.' Neuroscientifically, such dual processing causes the divergent function of the prefrontal cortex to collide with the convergent evaluation areas, disrupting the flow of thought and delaying planning.
Walt Disney's routine thoroughly isolated these two cognitive states. In the Dreamer phase, all brakes are released to maximize divergent thinking and induce dopaminergic flow. Entering the Realist phase stimulates the design of execution plans, and applying critical analysis exclusively in the final Critic's room produces perfectly tuned solutions without damaging the wings of the idea.

## 3. Separation of Imagination and Critique is Key

Creative thinking must always happen before critical thinking. Try defining your roles clearly and applying them in sequence when planning thoughts like Walt Disney. You can prevent cognitive mistakes of breaking the wings of ideas by prematurely criticizing them before they are even created.
