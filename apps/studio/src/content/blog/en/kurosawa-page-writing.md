"Don't try to write a grand masterpiece all at once. Focus solely on the modest rule of completing 2 pages every day. If you write 2 pages daily for a year, that totals over 700 pages—enough for two or three full screenplays." 🎬

Akira Kurosawa (1910–1998) was the legendary Japanese filmmaker whose groundbreaking visual language and narrative mastery in masterworks like *Rashomon* (1950), *Seven Samurai* (1954), and *Ran* (1985) permanently reshaped world cinema. While countless directors and writers paralyzed themselves facing the intimidating wall of blank pages, Kurosawa maintained a relentless stream of brilliant creative output.

The secret behind his vast body of work was a simple, non-negotiable morning habit: writing exactly two pages of screenplay per day. Even during intense filming schedules or periods of low motivation, Kurosawa never missed his daily 2-page quota. As he wrote in his autobiography *Something Like an Autobiography* (*Gama no Abura*), attempting to write an entire cinematic epic in one go induces crippling psychological pressure. However, committing strictly to 2 pages a day yields two to three complete scripts annually. Neuroscientifically, scaling down a monumental goal into a tiny micro-goal bypasses the amygdala's threat alarm, preventing cognitive avoidance and enabling the prefrontal cortex to initiate executive focus without friction.

In today's post, we unpack the behavioral psychology and neuroscience behind Akira Kurosawa's '2-page screenplay' routine and present a practical 3-step action guide to eliminate friction and execute ambitious long-term goals.

## 1. Amygdala Threat Response Suppression and the Neuroscience of Micro-Goals

When a goal's scale feels overwhelming, the brain's emotional threat center—the <strong>Amygdala</strong>—perceives it as a threat, triggering subconscious avoidance, anxiety, and procrastination. Scaling down the daily objective to an ultra-small quota, such as writing just 2 pages, keeps the task below the amygdala's threat detection threshold. This <strong>Micro-Goal</strong> strategy disarms cognitive behavioral resistance, allowing the <strong>Prefrontal Cortex</strong> to immediately engage its <strong>Executive Function</strong> and sustain long-term project execution without friction.

## 3. Caution: Excluding Overall Project Pressure to Focus Exclusively on Completing the Daily Micro-Goal

Fixating on the total magnitude of a project induces severe cognitive overwhelm, causing the brain to surrender before even starting. Restrict your conscious awareness exclusively to today's tiny micro-goal (e.g., writing 2 pages). Rather than relying on volatile willpower, establishing a reliable daily environmental rule to complete 2 pages is the ultimate key to achieving monumental outcomes.
