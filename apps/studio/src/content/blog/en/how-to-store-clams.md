Have you ever brought home fresh clams or mussels for a flavorful soup, only to submerge them in tap water and find them dead hours later? 🦪

Live shellfish are highly perishable marine organisms. Sealing them in airtight plastic bags or soaking them in fresh tap water starves them of oxygen or causes osmotic shock, killing them rapidly and triggering swift bacterial decay.

In this guide, introduces a scientifically proven 3-step storage guide recommended by seafood safety authorities to purge, chill, and preserve live shellfish safely. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3.5 py-1.5 text-xs font-black tracking-wider bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-700 dark:bg-emerald-500/20 dark:text-emerald-300 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/30 shadow-xs">STEP 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Purge in saltwater in a dark environment — Sand Purging & Cleaning 🌊</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Submerge clams in a 3% saltwater solution (approx. 30g salt per 1L water). Add a stainless steel spoon, cover with a dark plastic bag, and place in a cool spot (0–4°C) for 2–3 hours to purge sand.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Cover with a damp towel and refrigerate at 0–4°C — Cold Storage (1–2 Days) ❄️</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    After purging, rinse with clean saltwater, drain thoroughly, place in an open bowl, and cover with a damp paper towel or cloth to keep them moist in the fridge for up to 1–2 days.
  </p>
</div>

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Purge, drain, seal, and freeze — Long-Term Storage (3 Months) 🧊</h4>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Never soak live clams in tap water or seal them airtight 🚫</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Fresh tap water causes fatal osmotic shock to saltwater clams, and sealing them in airtight bags suffocates them. Live shellfish must be stored with access to air so they can breathe.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Discard open shells that do not close when tapped ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Clams or mussels with broken shells or those that remain wide open after tapping are already dead, posing a high risk of bacterial toxins. Discard them immediately.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
