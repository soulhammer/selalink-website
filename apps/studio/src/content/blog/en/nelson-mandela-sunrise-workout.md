Nelson Mandela dedicated his life to human dignity and equality, becoming the first black president of South Africa. He was arrested and imprisoned for 27 years. However, Mandela did not break down in despair and finally won freedom. ☀️

Mandela's lifeline in the cold and narrow cell of Robben Island was early morning exercise. He woke up at 5:00 AM without an alarm, jogged in place for 45 minutes on the narrow floor, and did 100 push-ups and 200 sit-ups every day. This sunrise routine was a noble promise to guard his self-esteem and soul.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the scientific evidence behind Mandela's 'sunrise bodyweight exercise' routine and how modern people can awaken their morning energy.

## 1. Brain Science of Maintaining Autonomy Under Extreme Control

When the external environment is completely controlled and no freedom is given, the brain determines that it has lost control over actions and falls into a state of learned helplessness. This leads to depression and cognitive decline.

The early morning exercise that Mandela practiced was an action that fed back control and autonomy to the brain, saying 'I control my body and time.' Serotonin and endorphins released during exercise reduce anxiety and provide resilience. Regular movement also stimulates the motor cortex, helping to maintain cool and patient cognitive abilities under stress.
