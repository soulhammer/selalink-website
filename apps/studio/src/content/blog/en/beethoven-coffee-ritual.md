"Music should strike fire from the heart of man, and bring tears to the eyes of woman." ☕

Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827) was a musical titan who, despite the catastrophic ordeal of hearing loss, composed immortal masterpieces such as Symphony No. 5 ('Fate'), Symphony No. 9 ('Choral'), and 'Missa Solemnis'. Bridging the Classical era and Romanticism, he was famous for his fiery temperament and extreme sensitivity; yet, at the moment of creation, he maintained astonishing calm and precision.

The secret lay in his strict morning '60-bean coffee ritual' performed right before composing. Uncompromising about his coffee's flavor, Beethoven meticulously counted out exactly 60 raw beans by hand for every single cup before placing them into the grinder. Even when entertaining honored guests, he observed this '60-bean' rule without a single bean's deviation. For a man whose connection to the outer world was waning due to deafness, those tactile moments spent counting 60 beans served as a sacred morning gateway—soothing anxiety and transitioning his mind into inner musical creation.

In today's post, we analyze the brain science embedded in Beethoven's curious 60-bean ritual and present a 3-step practical guide for modern individuals to maximize daily focus through a morning ritual.

## 1. Suppression of DMN Hyper-activation and Cognitive Framing via Fine Sensory Focus

Right after waking up in the morning, the brain often overactivates the Default Mode Network (DMN) by recalling yesterday's worries or today's tasks, wasting cognitive energy and raising anxiety. At this moment, a precise fine motor task—such as feeling the tactile texture of small, round coffee beans and counting them one by one—serves as an excellent mindfulness exercise that forcefully shifts the brain's focus to the 'present sense'. This quiets the wandering thoughts of the DMN and awakens the sensory cortex and executive control networks. Furthermore, performing this identical sequence daily acts as a powerful cognitive trigger, signaling to the brain that it is now entering a state of high-level creative focus.

## 3. Caution & Tips: Preserving Refined Analog Sensory Engagement

When conducting your morning ritual, checking news or social media on a smartphone or simply pressing buttons on automatic appliances hinders the brain from sustaining a mindfulness state. Immerse yourself strictly in analog sensory stimuli—such as fine hand sensations, the tactile texture of tools, aromas, and sounds—to facilitate smooth cognitive synchronization in the brain.
