"Fragments captured by the eyes and hands during the day are completed with critical reflection in the stillness of the night." ✍️

Yeonam Park Ji-won, a prominent Realist (Silhak) scholar of the late Joseon Dynasty, was a monumental man of letters who spearheaded social reform with an unconventional prose style and sharp critical consciousness. Traveling to Qing Dynasty China in 1780 with the diplomatic mission for Emperor Qianlong's birthday, he was a reformist intellectual seeking to rectify the rigid dogmatism of Joseon society and actively adopt advanced technologies to enrich people's lives.

While riding horseback during the day, Park Ji-won scribbled quick fragments of landscapes, foreign technologies, and conversations onto small note sheets (horseback notes). At night, sitting beneath a lamp in his quarters, he meticulously reconstructed these brief notes into deeply analytical journal entries that questioned the institutions and customs of his era. This became *Yeolha Ilgi* (Jehol Diary), a crown jewel of Korean literature. As preserved in the Jangseogak archives at the Academy of Korean Studies and recorded in *Yeonamjib*, nighttime review journaling was Park Ji-won's reflection routine to elevate daily experiences into profound cognitive wisdom and maximize metacognition.

In today's post, we introduce the neuroscientific principles of Memory Reconsolidation and Metacognitive Retrospective embedded in Park Ji-won's nighttime review routine.

## 1. Nighttime Retrospective Effects Brought by Memory Reconsolidation and Metacognitive Activation

Retrieving, analyzing, and describing short-term memories gathered during the day within a quiet nighttime environment strongly stimulates the neuroscientific mechanisms of <strong>'Memory Reconsolidation'</strong> and <strong>'Metacognition'</strong>.

Combining critical thinking and questioning ('Why is this so?') with factual journal writing strengthens connectivity between the hippocampus (Hippocampus) and prefrontal cortex (Prefrontal Cortex), deeply encoding experiences into long-term memory. This builds reflective wisdom that evaluates the day objectively and solves complex problems at a higher level.

## 3. Move Beyond Mechanical Recording and Add 'Questions and Reflection'

The core of Park Ji-won's routine is not listing events that occurred, but uncovering the deeper meaning and questions behind them. Merely listing daily tasks fails to stimulate cognitive circuits; reflect deeply on 'why it happened' and 'what was learned' regarding one major experience of the day.
