Kim Gu, who served as the leader of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea, lived under constant threats of arrest, fears of assassination, and a heavy sense of responsibility. Amid this high stress, he maintained a spiritual practice: calligraphy. 🖌

Quietly grinding ink and moving his brush on paper, Kim Gu gathered his wandering mind and strengthened his resolve. Neuroscientifically, calligraphy is a moving meditation that calms the amygdala using fine motor control.

Today, BuildSelf reveals the relaxation and attention regulation mechanisms hidden in Kim Gu's calligraphy meditation routine, proposing a 3-step action plan for modern people to overcome anxiety and train deep focus.

## 1. Physiological Link Between Fine Motor Control and Amygdala De-excitation

Research shows performing fine motor control, like adjusting the force of a brush in millimeters, heavily activates the somatosensory and motor cortexes. When the brain's associative areas focus entirely on physical sensory control, abnormal overactivity of the amygdala, which causes anxiety, is physically inhibited.

Moreover, the subtle aroma of grinding ink and the rhythm of pressing the brush on paper stimulate the parasympathetic nervous system, lowering cortisol levels and matching the heart rate to a relaxed meditative state.
