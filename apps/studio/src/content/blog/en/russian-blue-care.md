Russian Blues are captivating felines renowned for their short, silvery-blue coat and striking green eyes. While quiet, gentle, and deeply loyal to their family, they are genetically shy, reserved, and highly sensitive to loud noises or environment changes. 🐱 This is why they quickly hide inside cat trees or closets when a guest visits or when a vacuum cleaner runs. Forcing them out of their hiding spots only causes more panic. Today, PetSelf details how to build a safe, stress-free space for your shy Russian Blue and introduces a 3-step routine to build a strong bond with them.

## 1. Russian Blue Body Language: Reading Warning Signs of Panic

    <h4 class="text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-white m-0">Russian Blue Body Language: Reading Warning Signs of Panic</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    Because of their gentle nature, Russian Blues show warning signals quietly before reacting. Key panic signals include 'Dilated Pupils' and 'Crouching'. When they hear a sudden noise or are approached by a stranger, their pupils will dilate, becoming large and round as their stress system activates. If they press their body flat against the floor and flatten their ears sideways into 'airplane ears', they are experiencing extreme fear and are preparing to flee or defend themselves.
