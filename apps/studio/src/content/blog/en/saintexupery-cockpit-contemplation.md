"Perfection is achieved, not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away." ✈️

Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (1900–1944) was a master of 20th-century French literature and a pioneering airmail pilot. Drawing from his experiences of isolation and reflection while flying over the Sahara Desert and the Andes, he created timeless works such as 'Wind, Sand and Stars', 'Night Flight', and 'The Little Prince'.

At the core of his literary approach is the search for fundamental truths through disciplined, restrained language. Rather than relying on ornate rhetoric, he focused on refining text down to its essential meaning. While the solitude of the skies offered a sanctuary for deep contemplation, his diligent editing process on the ground allowed him to strip away unnecessary details, achieving clarity through subtraction.

## 1. How Solitude and Subtraction Enhance Clarity of Thought

Cognitive science suggests that quiet environments free from external distractions facilitate deep reflective thinking and creative ideation. Subsequently, the deliberate practice of trimming redundant words and rhetoric engages prefrontal metacognition, refining the structure and precision of your message.

## 3. Tips and Best Practices for the Subtraction Writing Process

Do not censor or edit while generating initial thoughts. Allow ideas to flow freely in solitude first, then apply a structured review process to boldly strip away unnecessary modifiers and redundant explanations.
