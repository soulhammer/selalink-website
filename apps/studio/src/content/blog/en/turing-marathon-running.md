Alan Turing, the British genius mathematician who decoded the notorious Enigma code of the German army during World War II and laid the foundation of computer science. He suffered from chronic brain overload due to complex calculations, mechanical design, and cryptanalysis. His secret to maintaining mental equilibrium and creative thinking under such intellectual pressure was 'marathon running.'

Turing maintained a grueling long-distance running routine, covering over 10 miles (about 16 km) daily. In fact, he was a pro-level runner who finished 5th in the 1948 British Olympic marathon trials with an impressive time of 2 hours, 46 minutes, and 3 seconds. For Turing, running was not just exercise, but an essential contemplation channel that stopped frontal lobe cognitive activity to purify the brain.

Today, BuildSelf explores the neuroscientific mechanisms of Alan Turing's running routine and a 30-minute running guide for mental awakening in modern people.

## 1. Brain Cell Generation and Cognitive Refresher Promoted by Aerobic Exercise

Consistent aerobic exercise daily induces the synthesis of <strong>BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)</strong>, a crucial substance for brain health. BDNF promotes the generation of new neurons in the hippocampus, which is responsible for memory and learning, thereby expanding cognitive working memory space.

Additionally, during running, the frontal lobe responsible for cognitive control enters a temporary state of rest, stimulating the <strong>Default Mode Network (DMN)</strong> which utilizes unconscious resources. Thanks to this phenomenon, complex computations or creative ideas that seemed unsolvable while racking your brain are instantly activated with a flash of insight right after running.
