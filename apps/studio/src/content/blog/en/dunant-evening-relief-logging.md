"When tragedy is recorded in objective language, despair turns into a mission to save humanity." 🕊️

In 1859, on the battlefield of Solferino in Italy, amid a tragic scene where over 40,000 wounded soldiers lay groaning without medical care, one man lit a monumental torch of human compassion: Henri Dunant, founder of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) and co-recipient of the very first Nobel Peace Prize in 1901. Passing through on business, Dunant witnessed the horrors of war firsthand and organized local villagers to provide relief to all wounded soldiers, regardless of their nationality.

Every night after completing harrowing relief work, Dunant sat under a lamplight to meticulously record the day's casualty numbers, shortages of medical supplies, and the physical state of the volunteers. Alongside these facts, he penned a diary of deep heartbreak and humanitarian reflections. These nightly logs later became his landmark book, *A Memory of Solferino*, serving as the catalyst for the founding of the Red Cross movement and the drafting of the Geneva Conventions.

The secret that kept Dunant mentally intact amidst overwhelming horror and allowed him to transform tragedy into a historical miracle lay in his evening recording routine. In today's post, we introduce the cognitive science principles of Expressive Writing and Trauma Cognitive Restructuring embedded in Henri Dunant's evening relief logging routine.

## 1. Expressive Writing, Amygdala Calming, and Narrative Memory Restructuring Mechanisms

Objectifying and recording shocking scenes or extreme stress in written language triggers the brain's Expressive Writing mechanism. As raw emotional shock is processed into structured sentences, hyperactivation in the amygdala—the brain's fear and trauma center—is calmed, while executive emotion regulation centers in the prefrontal cortex are activated.

Furthermore, narrative integration of hard objective data (casualty stats, resource needs) with subjective reflections allows the brain to reorganize chaotic stress stimuli into structured Narrative Memory. Through this process, tragic pain is transformed from psychological helplessness into 'actionable societal tasks', maximizing mental resilience even amidst extreme trauma.

## 3. Combine Objective Facts and Constructive Action Beyond Raw Sorrow

Dunant's diary was never a mere venting of helplessness or grief. Crucially, it combined objective operational metrics (casualty numbers, supply deficits) with constructive reflection on solutions. When logging high-stress experiences, over-focusing purely on negative emotions can trigger trauma re-experiencing. It is vital to maintain a balance between hard facts and actionable next steps.
