Peanuts are one of the most widely consumed legumes and nuts worldwide, packed with rich flavor and essential nutrients! However, have you ever stored them at room temperature only to find them stale or rancid? 🥜

Peanuts are high in unsaturated fatty acids, making them prone to rapid oxidation caused by the lipoxygenase enzyme when exposed to oxygen. Furthermore, in environments with humidity above 65% and water activity (Aw) above 0.70, Aspergillus flavus mold rapidly thrives, producing Aflatoxin B1—a potent Class 1 carcinogen.

In this guide, we introduce a scientifically proven 3-step peanut storage guide based on guidelines from leading agricultural and food safety authorities to preserve crispness and prevent deadly mold toxins. 🛡️

## 1. Scientifically Proven Storage Guide

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3.5 py-1.5 text-xs font-black tracking-wider bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-700 dark:bg-emerald-500/20 dark:text-emerald-300 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/30 shadow-xs">STEP 1</span>
    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Airtight packaging to seal out oxygen and moisture — Moisture & Air Blocking 📦</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Exposing shelled or in-shell peanuts to ambient air triggers lipid oxidation upon contact with oxygen. 💡 Storage Tip: Place peanuts in Ziploc bags or airtight containers with food-grade desiccant packets (silica gel) or oxygen absorbers. Minimizing air exposure and reducing water activity is your first line of defense.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">
    <span class="px-3.5 py-1.5 text-xs font-black tracking-wider bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-700 dark:bg-emerald-500/20 dark:text-emerald-300 rounded-full border border-emerald-500/30 shadow-xs">STEP 2</span>
    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Refrigerate at low temperatures (0–4°C) — Cold Storage (3–6 Months) ❄️</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    Higher room temperatures activate lipolytic enzymes, causing loss of crunch and unpleasant off-odors. 💡 Storage Tip: Store sealed peanuts in the refrigerator crisper drawer at 0–4°C (32–39°F). Low temperatures suppress lipoxygenase activity and fungal growth, keeping peanuts fresh for 3 to 6 months.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-emerald-500/20 bg-gradient-to-b from-emerald-500/[0.04] to-transparent dark:border-emerald-500/20 dark:bg-emerald-950/20 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md transition-all">
  <div class="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 mb-4">

    <h4 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-extrabold text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 m-0">Freeze long-term at -18°C or below — Freezer Storage (1+ Year) 🧊</h4>
  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0 font-normal">
    If you buy peanuts in bulk or need to store them for more than 6 months, freezing is essential. 💡 Storage Tip: Portion peanuts into individual Ziploc bags, seal tightly, and place in the freezer (-18°C / 0°F or colder). Sub-zero freezing in an airtight environment halts lipid oxidation, preserving flavor and nutrients for over a year.
  </p>

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Never consume moldy or rancid peanuts ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Aflatoxin B1 produced in peanuts is a potent Class 1 carcinogen that causes liver cancer and is extremely heat-stable, remaining intact even at temperatures between 260–300°C (500–572°F). If peanuts show visible mold, smell rancid, or taste bitter, washing or roasting will not destroy the toxins—discard them immediately.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
