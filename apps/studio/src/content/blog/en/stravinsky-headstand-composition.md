Igor Stravinsky, who shook the 20th-century classical music scene with avant-garde melodies like The Rite of Spring and The Firebird. Behind his amazing musical experiments was a unique physical routine. Whenever he ran out of inspiration or felt heavy-headed while composing, he would do a headstand next to his piano. He believed that going upside down wakes up the brain and changes perspective. Let's explore how this physical inversion sparks creativity.

## 1. How Headstands Reset Brain Blood Flow and Vestibular Sensation

When the brain is stuck in a single thought, vertically inverting physical posture triggers two neurological changes. First, gravity instantly pushes blood flow toward the head, boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to the prefrontal cortex. Second, as the inner ear's vestibular organs detect an unusual gravity signal, the spatial orientation networks in the cerebellum and cerebral cortex awaken. This bypasses rigid attention filters to facilitate creative association.

## 3. Precautions and Tips

  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      If you have neck or blood pressure issues, replace full headstands with gentle half-inversions, like resting your back against a wall or putting your legs on a chair. Perform it for less than 1 minute to allow brain blood pressure to adapt. After finishing, do not stand up suddenly; lie down or keep your head low for 30 seconds before recovering.
