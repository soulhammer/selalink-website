Louis Pasteur, an absolute pioneer of modern medicine and microbiology and a French genius chemist who pioneered vaccines, saved humanity from the fear of infectious diseases by inventing pasteurization and anthrax/rabies vaccines. He sat at his lab desk and stared motionless for hours at a flask of culture under a microscope magnifying glass. He meticulously drew and recorded the minute shaking traces left behind by microorganisms as they proliferate in his notebook. His disinfection method and vaccine discovery were born from this observational feedback routine while staring at the flask.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of the habit of ‘observing the shaking of microorganisms in flask cultures placed under a microscope’ and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of brain science principles that lead to success

The act of forming a hypothesis and inputting the trajectory of changes in the subtle observation object into the brain to operate a real-time feedback loop improves the brain's fine sensory resolution. The process of immediately converting and recording changes in observation data into text strengthens the neural network connection between the brain's lateral prefrontal cortex and the visual association cortex, enhancing the insight and analysis ability to detect minor changes in business or environmental errors one step ahead of others.
