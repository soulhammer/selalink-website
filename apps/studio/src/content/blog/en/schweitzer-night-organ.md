Dr. Albert Schweitzer was a Nobel Peace Prize winner, devoted medical doctor, philosopher, and world-renowned authority on Johann Sebastian Bach. How did he overcome the extreme physical and mental exhaustion faced while caring for patients in the hot and humid jungle of Lambaréné, Gabon?

Every night after exhausting medical practice, Dr. Schweitzer sat at a pedal piano built for the jungle and quietly played Bach. Immersing himself in the majestic, orderly melodies of Bach was his ultimate 'nervous system reset routine' to soothe mind and body and prevent burnout.

In today's post, we introduce the science of autonomic nervous system relaxation embedded in Dr. Schweitzer's evening organ routine.

## 1. Parasympathetic Nervous System Activation Induced by Structured Classical Melodies

Excessive stress during the day continuously overactivates the Sympathetic Nervous System, raising cortisol levels and disrupting autonomic balance.

Structured classical melodies with a tempo of 60–80 BPM, like Bach's music played by Dr. Schweitzer, act as an <strong>'autonomic relaxation switch'</strong> that immediately triggers the Parasympathetic Nervous System. Playing an instrument or focusing on music stabilizes heart rate and improves heart rate variability (HRV), swiftly recovering mental fatigue accumulated during the day.

## 3. Choose Relaxing Music Over Intense Screens Before Bed

Consuming stimulating videos or texts late at night prevents the brain from entering rest mode. Like Dr. Schweitzer, send relaxation signals through quiet musical immersion. Rather than relying solely on willpower, designing the physical environment and habit cues beforehand is key to consistency.
