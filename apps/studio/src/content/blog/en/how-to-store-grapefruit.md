Beloved for its signature bittersweet flavor and invigorating citrus punch, grapefruit is a staple for breakfasts and healthy diets. But have you ever stored grapefruits in the fridge for too long, only to find pitted brown spots on the skin and an overwhelmingly bitter, dry flesh? 🍊

As a subtropical citrus fruit, storing grapefruit long-term in standard home refrigerators (0–4°C / 32–39°F) triggers chilling injury. Temperatures below 10°C (50°F) disrupt cell membrane lipid structures, inducing surface pitting and causing abnormal leakage of naringin, the natural flavonoid responsible for intense bitterness.

In this guide, we introduce a scientifically proven 3-step grapefruit storage guide recommended by food safety authorities to preserve juiciness and optimal flavor. 🛡️

## 2. Essential Storage Rules and Cautions

  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Avoid long-term storage in home fridges at 0–4°C ⚠️</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">
      Because grapefruit is a subtropical fruit, long-term exposure to temperatures below 10°C causes chilling injury. This results in sunken surface pitting and leakage of bitter naringin into the pulp.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="my-6 p-5 md:p-6 rounded-r-2xl rounded-l-md border-l-4 border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/5 dark:bg-amber-500/10 bg-amber-500/[0.06] dark:bg-amber-500/10 border-amber-500/30 flex items-start gap-4 shadow-xs backdrop-blur-xs">
  <span class="text-xl md:text-2xl flex-shrink-0 mt-0.5">⚠️</span>
  <div class="flex-1 min-w-0">
    <strong class="text-slate-950 dark:text-white font-extrabold block text-base md:text-lg mb-1.5 tracking-tight">Never seal in plastic bags at room temperature 💧</strong>
    <p class="text-slate-800 dark:text-slate-200 text-sm md:text-base m-0 leading-relaxed font-normal break-words">

    </p>
