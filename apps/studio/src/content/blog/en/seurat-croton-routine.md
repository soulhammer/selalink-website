"A perfect whole is the sum of meticulously validated small fragments." 🎨

Georges Seurat, the master who founded Neo-Impressionism in the late 19th century, transplanted scientific color theory onto canvas, and created the masterpiece 'A Sunday on La Grande Jatte'. Systematizing the optical mixing of light and color through Pointillism, he rejected mere emotional intuition in favor of a strictly scientific and observational methodology.

Before tackling his massive canvas spanning over 3 meters, Seurat produced roughly 60 palm-sized wooden panel studies (crotons/croquetons) to individually test color harmony, contrast, and dot placement. As documented in archives from the Art Institute of Chicago and the Musée d'Orsay, working on 'crotons' was Seurat's sacred prototyping (PoC) routine to eliminate risks before embarking on a massive project and to precisely quantify complementary color contrasts.

In today's post, we introduce the cognitive science principles of Hypothesis Testing and Working Memory Risk Reduction embedded in Georges Seurat's 'Croton' prototyping routine.

## 1. Cognitive Risk Reduction Induced by Small-Scale Prototyping and Hypothesis Testing

Attempting to execute a massive project all at once without prior testing causes the brain to face excessive failure anxiety and predictive uncertainty, leading to <strong>'Cognitive Overload'</strong> in the prefrontal cortex.

Rapidly testing hypotheses on palm-sized panels, as Seurat did, triggers <strong>'Prototyping'</strong> and <strong>'Rapid Hypothesis Testing'</strong> circuits. Testing variables in a low-risk environment suppresses fear responses in the amygdala and enables explosive precision and refinement during main execution.

## 3. Do Not Get Trapped in Perfectionism During the Testing Phase

The core of Seurat's Croton routine is not crafting finished art, but rapidly verifying core hypotheses (color combinations, dot density). Avoid obsessing over perfecting the prototype itself; focus on swift, concise experimentation so you retain energy for main execution.
