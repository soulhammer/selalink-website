The peak of French symbolist literature and sharp intellect, Paul Valery. Throughout his life, every day at 4 AM, he woke up without fail and turned on his lamp. He headed straight to his writing desk. During the quiet hours before dawn when the world was silent, Valery recorded the stream of consciousness and thoughts. Over 51 years, these notebooks accumulated to 261 volumes, serving as the source of all his philosophical ideas. Explore how the quietness of dawn filters cognitive noise to extract the purest ideas.

## 1. Blocking Cognitive Noise and Action of Waking Hormones in Dawn Quietness

During normal daytime hours, the brain wastes cognitive resources facing endless external stimuli like noise, emails, and messengers. However, at 4 AM, the surrounding world is completely still, reducing the energy spent filtering external stimuli to near zero. Simultaneously, the gradual rise in cortisol hormone levels during waking calmly alerts the brain without stress, creating perfect physiological conditions for deep, prefrontal-driven reflective writing and self-contemplation.
