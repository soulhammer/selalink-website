Charlemagne, the great monarch who laid the historical foundation of Western Europe and led the renaissance of culture and education. In the 8th century, an era of illiteracy where writing was rare and empire expansion was busy, how did the Emperor pursue knowledge and writing? 👑

Although Charlemagne had an immense thirst for learning, his writing skills were initially lacking. Eager to master the art of penmanship even in his later years, he kept a wax tablet and iron stylus under his pillow or on a bedside chest. Whenever he went to bed or woke up in the middle of the night, he sat in the candlelight to trace letters and practice Latin sentences. This lifelong learning habit gave him the drive to restore the empire's educational infrastructure. For Charlemagne, the bedside wax tablet was the ultimate environmental cue to direct even a fleeting cognitive moment into study.

## 1. 'Memory Consolidation Effect' to Support Long-term Memory Retention During Sleep

Neurophysiologically, information acquired during the day is primarily stored in the hippocampus, the brain's temporary storage. For this information to convert into permanent knowledge, it must undergo memory consolidation to move to the cerebral cortex. This process runs maximally during slow-wave sleep. If word learning and writing are induced right before sleep or during wake-ups like Charlemagne, the brain immediately consolidates the concentrated cognitive data with sleep mechanisms, rapidly archiving it into long-term memory circuits. Placing a notepad at the bedside is the most efficient cognitive tool utilizing this natural memory engine.
