Carl Jung, the world-renowned psychiatrist who mapped the human unconscious and proposed the concepts of the collective unconscious and complexes. Where did his creative energy, which counseled countless patients and wrote vast academic studies, recover? 🪵

Jung built and periodically stayed in the 'Bollingen Tower,' a remote cabin on the shores of Lake Zurich that lacked electricity and running water. There, he chopped wood, carried water in heavy buckets, and built fires to cook. Immersing himself in these primitive physical tasks, he temporarily stepped away from complex psychological studies. Surprisingly, in this silent space, his brain cleared, and he was able to draw deep inspirations from the unconscious.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive principles behind Carl Jung's 'cabin reset' routine and a practical digital detox routine for busy modern people.

## 1. 'Default Mode Network' to Restore Brain's Cognitive Resources

When we are exposed to constant digital stimuli such as smartphone notifications, emails, and social media throughout the day, the frontal lobe of our brain consumes a huge amount of cognitive energy to process information and maintain selective focus. This eventually leads to chronic cognitive fatigue and depletion of creativity.

Carl Jung's Bollingen routine was a process of forcibly breaking this loop of stimulation and activating the brain's <strong>'Default Mode Network (DMN)'</strong>. When performing simple, repetitive, primitive physical tasks such as chopping wood or fetching water in a state where artificial visual stimuli are completely blocked, the brain stops conscious cognitive control and enters the DMN state. The relaxed brain organizes scattered memories and concepts, restores emotional fatigue, and derives new creative ideas through deep subconscious reflection.
