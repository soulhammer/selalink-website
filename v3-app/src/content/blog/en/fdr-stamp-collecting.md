Franklin D. Roosevelt, the only 4-term U.S. president who led the New Deal to overcome the Great Depression and guided the Allies to victory in WWII. He lived in heavy duties testing human limits. ✉️

Despite the physical pain of polio, one of the secrets of his bright energy was his stamp sorting routine before bed. He ordered aides not to disturb his 30 minutes before sleep. Clearing documents and sorting small paper pieces by continent and year, he purified his brain from the noise of war.

Today, BuildSelf looks at the brain science behind Roosevelt's 'stamp collecting' routine and how modern people can safely close the day with analog relaxation.

## 1. Cognitive Shifting That Cools Overheated Working Memory

When executing high-stress tasks, the brain's prefrontal cortex rotates constantly, consuming massive energy. If tension is not relieved after work and cognitive residue remains, it causes sleep disorders and damages neurons long term.

Enjoying non-work visual-tactile hobbies, like Roosevelt's stamp sorting, sends a 'safety signal' to the brain to gently lower the arousal level of the frontal lobe. Studies show that this cognitive shifting promotes melatonin release, aiding deep sleep. It acts as an essential cooling system to cool down the overheated brain.
