Walt Disney built an animation empire by gifting dreams to children and adults alike. His creativity was not just a product of inspiration. He had a unique system of thinking that refined seemingly absurd imaginations into perfect reality.

When refining a project, he moved between three rooms. The first was the Dreamer's room, where all kinds of imaginative ideas were presented without limitations. The second was the Realist's room, where those ideas were realized with practical technology and budget. The third was the Critic's room, which analyzed the flaws of the proposals. Disney generated innovative masterpieces by strictly isolating and cross-checking these three roles.

Today, BuildSelf looks at the science behind Disney's 'Three Rooms' routine and how to use it in modern planning work.

## 1. Cognitive Effects of Separating Focus and Criticism

A common mistake when making plans is to critique ideas simultaneously by thinking 'This is impossible' or 'It costs too much.' Brain science shows that this simultaneous processing causes the frontal lobe (responsible for divergence) and evaluation areas (responsible for convergence) to conflict, disrupting the flow of thought.

Disney's routine strictly isolated these two cognitive states. In the Dreamer phase, all brakes are released to maximize cognitive divergence, inducing dopaminergic focus. Entering the Realist phase stimulates the design of execution plans, and only in the Critic phase is critique applied, yielding polished solutions without damaging the original ideas.
