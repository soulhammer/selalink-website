Simone de Beauvoir, a French philosopher and novelist who wrote 'The Second Sex' and made a huge milestone in modern feminism and existential thought. Her high-focus state, which produced high-level intellectual thoughts and meticulous sentences every time, was woven not in a glamorous and sealed study, but in a corner of a bustling Paris cafe. ☕

Every morning at 10 AM, Beauvoir went to 'Café de Flore', her famous favorite artistic cafe in Paris. She sat in the quiet corner seat where she always sat, ordered a cup of tea, and was fully immersed in writing without moving for 4 hours until 2 PM. The cafe staff refrained from unnecessary serving to avoid disrupting her writing flow, and the surrounding noises acted as a great white noise blocker that erased her loneliness and stray thoughts. For Beauvoir, the cafe was not a space where she was interrupted, but a sanctuary where she completely simplified and isolated herself from all the complexity of life.

Today, BuildSelf looks into the brain science concentration principles hidden in Simone de Beauvoir's 'cafe writing' routine, and discovers how to set up 'My Own Focus Zone' for modern people who suffer from constant distractions and stray thoughts.

## 1. Working Mechanisms of White Noise and Cognitive Script

Scientifically, facing the same physical environment (favorite seat, ordered drink) every day activates a <strong>'cognitive script'</strong> that helps transition directly into immersion, reducing unnecessary decision-making when shifting tasks. 

In addition, the gentle white noise of 50-70 decibels (dB) in cafes prevents the brain's attention network from obsessing over specific stimuli or feeling bored. White noise gently stimulates brainwaves to activate alpha waves that induce creativity and problem-solving, and light dynamic movement around naturally touches the mirror neurons, easing the sense of isolation and maximizing Deep Work efficiency with psychological safety.

## 3. Social Noise That Is Only Immersion in Appearance
<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-indigo-500 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">💡</span>
  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      Not all noises are beneficial white noise to the brain. Music over 70 decibels or distinct conversations of people nearby (speech noise) actually interrupt the brain's phonological loop, significantly degrading reading and logical writing abilities. It is essential to find a place where a natural noise level is maintained, or use noise-canceling headphones if speech noise is clear.
    </p>
