Stephen King, the master of modern horror behind countless masterpieces like 'The Shining' and 'The Shawshank Redemption,' has maintained a strict discipline for decades: he starts writing every morning and never gets up from his desk until he finishes 2,000 words. ✍️

Regarding inspiration, King says, 'The amateur sits and waits for inspiration, the rest of us just get up and go to work.' He highly utilized sensory triggers to build a system where the brain automatically switches to focus mode.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes the sensorimotor learning principles hidden in Stephen King's writing routine and proposes a 3-step action plan for modern people to achieve daily productivity goals without fail.

## 1. Sensory Triggers and Deep Focus Systems: Sensorimotor Learning

In brain science, sensorimotor learning is the process of repeatedly linking a specific sensory stimulus (hearing, taste, physical setting) with a specific action (writing, studying, working) to form an automated neural pathway. Stephen King sitting in the same spot, taking a vitamin, and listening to set music every morning is a ritual to activate this sensory trigger.

Once repeated, the brain recognizes, 'When this trigger starts, it's time for super focus,' sliding into deep focus naturally without draining willpower. This is a cognitive strategy to maximize the prefrontal lobe's energy and extract equal productivity daily.
