Cleopatra VII captivated Caesar and Antony, the great heroes of Rome. While popular media depicts her with beauty, actual history records her as an educated and political strategist. 👑

The key to her intellectual persuasion was her direct communication ability without interpreters. According to Plutarch, she spoke at least 9 languages, including Egyptian, Greek, Hebrew, and Latin. She allocated afternoon time every day for language study. Deep conversation without translation pierced the hearts of foreign envoys.

Today, BuildSelf looks at the science behind Cleopatra's 'multilingual study' routine and how to build cognitive flexibility in global work.

## 1. Power of Foreign Language Study That Increases Brain Connection Density

The process of speaking and translating foreign languages alternately operates Wernicke's area (language comprehension) and Broca's area (language production). Constantly switching grammar frames highly stimulates the brain's cognitive control network.

Studies show that brains that steadily study multilingualism have thicker gray matter in the cerebral cortex, displaying superior attention and problem-solving capacities. Quickly switching language codes also helps flexible thinking and delays age-related cognitive decline.
