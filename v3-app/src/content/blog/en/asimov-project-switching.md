Writing over 500 books in vast genres throughout his life, Isaac Asimov was celebrated as an SF master and the preeminent popular science writer of his time. Behind this incredibly prolific master's ability to magic up manuscripts daily lay his unique and brilliant work habit. ⌨️

It was the 'autonomous switching of tasks.' Asimov set up multiple typewriters side-by-side in his study, each loaded with a different project (a novel, a science guide, an essay, etc.). When he hit a block or felt bored with one manuscript, instead of stressing to squeeze out words, he simply stood up, moved to the next typewriter, and continued writing a completely different draft.

Today, BuildSelf illuminates the cognitive science principles hidden in Isaac Asimov's 'typewriter switching' routine and proposes a 3-step action plan for modern people to overcome cognitive bottlenecks and maximize efficiency.

## 1. Context Switching and the Novelty Effect: Brain Region Recovery

According to cognitive psychology and working memory research, focusing on a single task for a long time over-activates specific neural networks, causing cognitive stagnation. Squeezing your brain during this Writer's Block state secretes cortisol and drops cognitive efficiency sharply.

At this moment, like Asimov, switching the context (Context Switching) to a completely different task (e.g., from writing fiction to factual science articles) allows the previously active and fatigued cortical region to rest. Meanwhile, a different neural network wakes up. This is the 'Novelty Effect,' allowing the brain to safely re-enter deep focus without feeling the previous fatigue.
