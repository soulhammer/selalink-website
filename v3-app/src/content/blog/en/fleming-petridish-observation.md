Penicillin, the first antibiotic that saved hundreds of millions of lives. Was this achievement born in a high-tech laboratory? 🧫

Actually, penicillin was a product of a 'mistake' in Alexander Fleming's cluttered lab. Before leaving for vacation, he left staphylococcus culture dishes on his desk. Upon return, while collecting dishes to clean, Fleming stopped to look at one dish that others would have discarded. The bacteria around the blue mold had dissolved. His habit of meticulously examining every dish and recording anomalies led to medicine's greatest serendipity.

Today, BuildSelf analyzes how Fleming's routine adjusted the brain's cognitive filter to trigger creativity and proposes a method to find breakthroughs from failures.

## 1. The Principles of 'Divergent Thinking' to Break Cognitive Fixation

The brain seeks efficiency based on experiences, creating a tendency of <strong>cognitive fixation</strong> where anomalies or mistakes are filtered out as noise. Examining a 'failure' meticulously, as Fleming did, resets the brain's cognitive filters by stimulating the frontoparietal attention network. This triggers <strong>divergent thinking</strong>, moving the brain from analytical processing to creative exploration, capturing value from errors.
