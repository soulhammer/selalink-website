Paul Cezanne, praised by Picasso and Matisse as the father of us all, laid the foundation for 20th-century cubism and abstraction. His works capture the eternal geometric structures of nature. The source of his unique vision was his early morning routine. Waking at 6 AM, he carried his easel to walk the trails of Aix-en-Provence. As the sun rose over Mont Sainte-Victoire, he stood and meditated on the shifting angles of light and transmuting colors. We explore how exposing the visual cortex to dawn sunlight sharpens focus and enhances creative intuition.

## 1. Dawn Sunlight and Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN) Activation

Daylight between 6 AM and 8 AM is rich in short-wavelength blue light, which activates melanopsin-containing ganglion cells in the retina. This signal travels directly to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), the brain's circadian pacemaker, shutting down melatonin and boosting cortisol to trigger high alertness. Furthermore, actively scanning natural landscapes and color contrasts during walking triggers broad neural synchronization across the visual cortex. This state of visual wakefulness clears cognitive fatigue and enhances perceptual sensitivity, preparing the brain for creative problem-solving.

Sunglasses block the specific wavelengths of solar radiation required to trigger the SCN's circadian reset. Likewise, checking your phone screen narrows your focus, causing cognitive tunneling that cancels the restorative benefits of scenic scanning.
