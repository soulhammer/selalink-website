Benjamin Britten, a maestro who led a great leap in 20th-century British music by composing the opera 'Peter Grimes' and 'The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra.' The harmonic threads of his complex and intellectual modern music were unraveled not in front of a sheet of music, but on the walking path of the Aldeburgh beach in Suffolk where a harsh sea breeze blew. 🌊

Britten, who kept rules as strict as Brahms, composed music from 8 AM to 1 PM every morning, and then walked out of his house, leaving his pen and music sheet completely on his desk. He walked along the pebble beach of his hometown on a constant course every day. While walking, he delicately refined the melodies and harmonic structures written in the morning to the sound of waves. He repeatedly listened to his brain's virtual orchestra performance on this path, erasing flaws in the melody and fully establishing the logical structure. Britten wrote about walking, 'During the walk, I resolve all the most troublesome and difficult challenges of my music.'

Today, BuildSelf introduces the brain science principles of the 'afternoon brain liberation walk' routine that Benjamin Britten stuck to every day, and a brain recovery method to revive the work efficiency of modern people whose brains are overloaded with information.

## 1. Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and Subconscious Cognitive Integration

The brain that has performed high-level calculations or planning throughout the morning operates the 'Central Executive Network (CEN)', which maintains a focused state, depleting cognitive energy and falling into a state of 'Attention Fatigue.'

According to environmental psychology's <strong>'Attention Restoration Theory (ART)'</strong>, when walking slowly outdoors, which has nature's non-stimulating soft fascination elements like wind or wave sounds, the brain finally relaxes the attention network. During this rest period, the brain operates the 'Default Mode Network (DMN)' to classify and transfer fragmented information or concerns from the morning to long-term storage, achieving <strong>'cognitive integration.'</strong> Through the afternoon walk, Britten cleared the overload on his brain and used subconscious thinking to intuitively solve complex problems.

## 3. Difference Between Active Brain and Passive Rest

    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">

Even though the body lies down, the brain's visual cortex and dopamine system consume attention to decode new video signals, increasing fatigue. Only walking while completely blocking information input, like Britten, is a scientific rest that resets the frontal lobe's cognitive fatigue.
