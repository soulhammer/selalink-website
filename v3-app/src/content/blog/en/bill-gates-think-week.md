Bill Gates, who founded Microsoft and led technological innovation. Amidst enormous turbulence and competition, he steered the company's huge rudder at the right timing to open a new paradigm. The secret to his capture of the decisive future vision was none other than his cabin isolation routine. 🌲

Twice a year during his Microsoft days, he completely cut off outside contact and headed to a quiet forest cabin, holding a massive amount of technical proposals, papers, and books. For a week, without contact with anyone, including his family, he focused solely on reading and reflecting. The famous 'Internet Tidal Wave' memo, which marked the beginning of the web browser war, was planned in this isolation routine.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the scientific evidence behind Gates' 'Think Week' routine and how modern people can use it to maximize productivity in a complex digital era.

## 1. Why Think Week Isolation Awakens the Brain

Modern people are always exposed to a state of 'attention residue' where attention is distractedly scattered amidst the flood of digital information. The connection of the neural network that helps deep thinking is blocked by reacting instantly.

Gates' Think Week cleanses this attention residue and synchronizes the brain with one giant topic. When external input stimuli are controlled, the prefrontal cortex is optimized for high-level cognitive processing. Brain science shows that this deep immersion promotes long-term memory consolidation between the hippocampus and the cerebral cortex, helping to weave fragmented knowledge into a long-term master plan.

<div class="my-8 p-6 md:p-8 rounded-[2rem] border border-slate-200/80 bg-white/50 dark:border-white/5 dark:bg-slate-900/30 shadow-sm backdrop-blur-md">

  </div>
  <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 leading-relaxed text-sm md:text-base m-0">
    Print out papers, professional books, and proposals in advance for the strategic tasks you want to solve, or collect them in physical books. Read only printed materials for more than 2 hours without opening internet search windows.
  </p>
