Amidst microscopic media attention and the brutal pressure on the court where losing a single point could slip her ranking, the weapons that kept her at the top for 20 years were her obsessive and unique pre-match routines. Before entering the court, she always listened to music with the same headphones, and bounced the ball exactly 5 times before the first serve. We explore how these physical actions blocked uncontrollable anxiety variables and focused her mind.

## 1. Amygdala Control and Cognitive Framing through Pre-Performance Routines (PPR)

The intense anxiety felt right before an exam or match stimulates the brain's hypothalamus, secreting epinephrine, stiffening muscles, and causing hyper-vigilance. A 'Pre-Performance Routine (PPR),' verified in sports psychology, turns the brain's eyes away from uncontrollable external threats (opponents, crowd noise) and forces focus onto 100% controllable 'physical actions' (bouncing the ball, arranging bags). This physical control halts the amygdala alarm circuits and activates a cognitive framing effect, tuning the brain into a safe flow state to reduce execution mistakes.
