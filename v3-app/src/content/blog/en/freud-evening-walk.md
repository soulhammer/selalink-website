Sigmund Freud, the father of psychoanalysis who reshaped modern psychology by introducing concepts like the subconscious and the interpretation of dreams. Where did his insights come from? 🧠

Freud spent his days listening to patients' heavy mental struggles. To recharge his exhausted prefrontal cortex, he kept a daily evening routine of walking 3 miles at a very fast pace around Vienna. He believed that fast walking cleared the mind and surfaced insights hidden in the subconscious. Returning to his study, he immediately wrote down his thoughts in a journal.

Today, BuildSelf introduces the scientific mechanisms behind Freud's 'fast walk and journaling' routine and how to apply it today.

## 1. Bilateral Stimulation and Prefrontal Emotion Control

Walking at a fast pace with alternating steps generates 'bilateral stimulation' that supplies tactile and motor inputs to the left and right hemispheres of the brain. Used in cognitive therapies (EMDR), this inhibits overactivation of the amygdala and brings emotional tranquility. Writing down subconscious thoughts immediately after walking stimulates the prefrontal cortex, blocking complex emotions and restoring working memory.
