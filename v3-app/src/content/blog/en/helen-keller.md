Helen Keller, an icon of overcoming who overcame the triple ordeal of being invisible and unable to hear (visual, hearing, and speech impediments) and dedicated her life as a great social activist and writer, coloring humanity's despair with hope. In the silent darkness, she focused all her nerves on the traces of Mr. Sullivan's tactile movements as he meticulously wrote the letters with his fingertips on the palm of his hand. The moment the cold spring water wetted the back of her other hand, she combined the tactile signal 'W-A-T-E-R' on her palm with the spirit of the water in her brain for the first time. This sophisticated training of tactile imitation was the key that made her an immortal writer.

Today on BuildSelf, we introduce the cognitive scientific principles of the habit of 'training tactile perception by constantly tapping letters on the palm' that he adheres to and a 3-step guide that modern people can easily practice in their daily lives.

## 1. Analysis of brain science principles that lead to success

The act of relying on the tactile location and movement trajectory of the body's epidermis with eyes closed and visual stimulation turned off enhances cognitive plasticity (neuroplasticity) in the somatosensory cortex. As sensory information becomes denser, the brain thickens the neural network that calmly structures the essential causal relationships of information without being distracted by visual noise, dramatically improving metacognitive resolution that keeps long-term memories clear.
