Louis XIV, who marked the golden age of the French absolute monarchy and stood at the pinnacle of power with the title 'Sun King' by building the Palace of Versailles. Throughout his long 72-year reign, processing constant territorial conflicts, factional struggles, and complex state affairs, what was the secret behind establishing his unwavering authority? Under the creed "By setting working times and rules, one can completely dominate life," Louis XIV divided his 24 hours strictly by the minute. Today, BuildSelf introduces the neurological advantages and modern implementation of Louis XIV's 'time blocking' routine.

## 1. Preventing Decision Fatigue via Establishing Time Blocking

The situation of having to choose 'what to do next' at every moment causes <strong>'Decision Fatigue'</strong>, which is the main culprit that consumes the cognitive energy of the frontal lobe. Neurologically, <strong>'Time Blocking'</strong>, which fixes and allocates the day's action plan to specific time blocks, maximizes the efficiency of the executive control network. By blocking the brain's selective worries about the next action and stimulating implementation intentions, it prevents procrastination and makes the brain focus its attention resources most efficiently on the target single task.

## 3. Include Margins Between Schedules, Not Perfectionist Tightness
<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-2xl border-l-4 border-indigo-500 bg-indigo-500/5 dark:bg-indigo-500/10 flex items-start gap-4">
  <span class="text-xl">💡</span>
  <div>
    <p class="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300 text-sm m-0 leading-relaxed">
      The most important thing to avoid for the time blocking routine to persist is drawing up a tight, zero-margin timetable. Perfectionism that allows no minute of error makes you give up the whole timetable when unexpected situations arise. You must intentionally leave a buffer time of about 10-15 minutes between schedules so the brain can avoid stress caused by unexpected delays and continuously hold control over time without burning out.
    </p>
