George Orwell, a giant of 20th-century English literature who sharply warned of the nature of human power and the dangers of dictatorship through '1984' and 'Animal Farm.' How were his foresight and cool analytical sentences maintained every day? 📰

Orwell started his day by waking up early every morning, sitting at his writing desk, and reading a pile of newspapers published that day. With scissors in hand, he carefully read through the articles. He then cut out specific facts, strange political phenomena, or sharp sentences that could be used as evidence for his arguments or give him inspiration, and pasted them neatly in his large scrapbook by date and category. This manual scrapbook was the best knowledge training tool that trained his brain in a multidimensional way. The basis of his dystopian control mechanisms created in his novels all originated from this clipping.

Today, BuildSelf explains the cognitive neurological working principles of Orwell's 'scissors clipping' routine and how modern people can use it as brain nutrients in the midst of digital information overload.

## 1. Brain Science of Information Encoding and Schema

Scanning countless digital articles simply with your eyes only temporarily keeps them in short-term memory, the brain's temporary storage, before most are forgotten. Conversely, the act of selecting, 'clipping' (physical or digital action), and categorizing articles induces the brain's 'deep encoding' processing.

This information structuring work not only deposits information into the long-term memory in the cerebral cortex but also forms a 'schema network' that integrates with existing knowledge systems. A brain with a highly developed schema quickly grasps the context of new information, pinpoints the core of a problem, and displays high fluid intelligence that recalls background knowledge at optimal speeds when writing or making decisions.
