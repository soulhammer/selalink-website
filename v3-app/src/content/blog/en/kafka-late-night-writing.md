Franz Kafka, widely regarded as a pioneer of existentialist literature, sharply depicted the anxiety and alienation of modern humans. His creative world only opened late at night when the bustle of the day had completely ceased. During the day, Kafka fulfilled his social role as a diligent administrator at a workers' accident insurance institute. However, at 11 PM, when the darkness deepened and the noise of family and neighbors quieted down, he finally sat at his desk.

Kafka wrote in extreme silence from 11 PM to 2 or 3 AM, sometimes until dawn. When all the stimuli of the world disappeared and only he and the blank page remained, his imagination awoke with the utmost clarity. Today, BuildSelf introduces the cognitive science behind Kafka's 'midnight writing' routine and how modern people can apply it to enhance concentration.

## 1. 'Frontal Down-regulation' to Release Control and Reach Essence

During the day, our frontal lobe actively consumes cognitive energy required for social norms, logical reasoning, and attention. However, late at night when energy is depleted, the strong control filter of the frontal lobe temporarily weakens. Cognitive science calls this state 'Frontal Down-regulation'.

When control is released, the brain escapes from rational suppression and relentlessly pours out emotional depth, unconscious association, and original creativity that were difficult to access normally. This is the physiological driving force that allowed Kafka to write masterpieces that give deep resonance in the dark night. In addition, the perfect physical silence of midnight completely blocks noise interference that disturbs working memory, creating an optimal neurochemical environment for the brain to immerse itself in a single topic.

## 3. Create a Shield to Completely Block Incoming Noise

The key is not to stay awake all night, but to intentionally create a quiet environment where external noise and visual stimuli are completely cut off for just one hour.
